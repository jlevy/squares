## A Constructive Strictness Reserve

Physical compactness proves that the twenty-five row exclusions persist in some
neighborhood, as derived in the
[previous hand argument](research-2026-10-07-n17-full-square-partner-coupling.md#actual-collective-restriction-and-prospective-regional-lift).
It does not prove that the existing fixed-epsilon common-core algorithm succeeds for
every sufficiently small radius.
That algorithm may certify coverage exactly on the boundary of its own inner collision
regions.

A different finite certificate can preserve the accepted union proof.
Write $P_{jk}(0)$ for an accepted point-conditioned row hull, $K_i$ for an accepted
nonempty owned hull, and $D_{jk}$ for the strict zero-centred core of that row’s full
closed angle interval.
For each of the same twenty-five rows, exp296 proves

$$
P_{jk}(0)\subseteq\bigcup_{i\ne j}(K_i-D_{jk}).
$$

Suppose exact closed-arc checks give a common rational $\delta>0$ such that every used
$q\in K_i$, every centre in every remaining point-conditioned row of owner $i$, and both
signed body axes satisfy a support slack at least $\delta$ below $1/2$. It suffices to
check hull vertices and the exact quadratic minima on the closed angle intervals.
A positive minimum $m$ of the half-angle numerator gives the lower slack
$m/(1+t_{\mathrm{hi}}^2)$; take the minimum over all required checks.
Owner0 uses its fixed accepted pose in this calculation.
No such numerical $\delta$ has been evaluated here.

Set $r=\delta/4$, $B_r=[-r,r]^2$ and $\widehat K_i=K_i+B_r$. Every unit body axis has
$\ell_1$ norm at most $\sqrt2<2$, so enlargement consumes at most $2r=\delta/2$ of the
slack. Thus $\widehat K_i$ remains strictly owned in the point context, with at least
$\delta/2$ body-coordinate slack.
Minkowski addition distributes over finite unions, giving

$$
P_{jk}(0)+B_r\subseteq
\bigcup_{i\ne j}(\widehat K_i-D_{jk}).
$$

Consequently a regional certificate can reuse the accepted row coverage after checking
only these two new geometric obligations:

1. Every $\widehat K_i$ is freshly strictly owned across every regional row of its
   owner, with all regional centre and angle alternatives included.
2. For each of the same twenty-five rows, $P_{jk}(h)\subseteq P_{jk}(0)+B_r$. An
   accepted empty point row requires the regional row to be empty instead.

The second check is convex polygon containment; the first uses exact closed-arc
quadratics. Fresh conditioning of all 992 rows is still required.
This route avoids repeating their union sweeps; it does not avoid the regional-domain
join. The enlarged hulls are new owned sets requiring proof, not transported point
certificates.

There is also a method-specific existence argument under explicit continuity premises.
For the regional owner-0 core $Q_0(h)$, require a common interior seed and convergence
to $Q_0(0)$ as $h$ decreases to zero.
The current finite continuous halfplanes and the interior seed supply the relevant
structure, but a quantitative consumer must check its declared parameters.
Then $Q_0(h)-D_{jk}$ converges as a convex body with interior.
A limit of points outside its interior is outside the limiting interior: any point
strictly inside the limit is eventually strictly inside the approximating body.
Intersecting with each original compact residual piece, taking the finite union of
closed outside-facet alternatives, and then its convex hull therefore gives the
one-sided limit property

$$
\limsup_{h\downarrow0}P_{jk}(h)\subseteq P_{jk}(0).
$$

Compactness makes this uniform over the finitely many rows.
The strict slack of the enlarged hulls and the positive box width $r$ then make both
obligations true for sufficiently small $h$. Empty limiting rows remain empty for all
sufficiently small $h$, by the same compactness argument.
This proves existence for the strengthened certificate shape, subject to its stated
domain-limit premises.
It still supplies no numerical radius or runtime bound.

The scientific question is whether a useful radius survives, rather than whether an
arbitrarily tiny patch exists.
Any radius rule, slack extraction and finite ladder must be registered before
evaluation. First assess the fixed $1/512$ experiment.
If a later certificate covers only a tiny region, require a complete guard-cover plan
before expanding a patch library.

## Propagation After a Verified Restriction

Exp296’s removed rows can support new ownership implications.
Let $S_i$ be an owner’s current finite set of live row alternatives and let $K_i(S_i)$
be the common owned set obtained by intersecting its exact ownership halfplanes.
Deleting rows removes conjuncts, so $S_i'\subseteq S_i$ implies
$K_i(S_i)\subseteq K_i(S_i')$, with the same bounding polygon and margin convention.
Common ownership consequently becomes stronger as certified alternatives disappear.

A sound iteration first recovers all groups from the accepted surviving rows, then
simultaneously removes every row whose complete centre hull is covered by the union of
other owners’ collision regions.
Each implication uses the previous accepted context; it does not assume the result of
its own deletion. Keep every closed interval and seam in the surviving union.
Whole-owner emptiness or shared strictly owned points prove contradiction only in the
current guard.

With fixed centre hulls, fixed row partition and whole-row deletion only, this process
has finite height: every changing round deletes at least one of the at most 992 foreign
rows. A complete no-change round is a fixed point of this specified operator.
It is neither a physical feasible packing nor evidence against stronger coupling.
This is an algorithmic termination statement, not a claim that 992 rounds are
affordable. Here no change means no row deletion; a deletion at a redundantly covered
closed seam can leave interval-union length unchanged and must not be called a fixed
point.

The next bounded test should perform one newly registered recovery-and-coverage round on
the accepted regional result if available, or explicitly on exp296’s point context
otherwise. Require additional exact interval-union loss or a checked contradiction;
retain point and regional flags separately.
Only a measured positive round justifies selecting another bounded round.
This continuation extracts consequences of the existing proof before adding a new branch
selector or producer recipe.

independent mechanical checks, fixed scientific criteria, and a protected final thirty
minutes. The first regional packet above is ready for source implementation after the
kickoff review. Its positive-width row restriction and stronger contradiction branches
are distinct prospective outcomes.

Use the first 1–2 hours for that discriminator while exact coverage optimization and
certification proceed in parallel.
Use its actual result to select propagation or the strictness-reserve consumer.
Reserve one later mathematical slice for the normal-form proof interface or a ready
external admission, rather than letting every slice become a smaller-radius retry.
Compare actual source, verification and integration costs at each boundary; the proposed
work allowances do not justify moving the research cutoff.

Stop unchanged propagation at a verified fixed point.
Stop a failed radius recipe before changing its radius or margin.
Stop speculative atlas enumeration before its completeness consumer exists.
Preserve resource-incomplete runs as incomplete and keep exp284’s unaccepted geometry
out of every dependency.
A successful six-hour block can establish a regional implication, stronger finite
propagation, or a new admitted external family; none supplies an evidence-based date for
completing n17 optimality.
