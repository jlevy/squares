## An Exact Polynomial Guard

For $t\in[0,1]$, write the positively oriented square axes as

$$
u(t)=\frac{(1-t^2,2t)}{1+t^2},\qquad
v(t)=\frac{(-2t,1-t^2)}{1+t^2}.
$$

Freeze $a=1/2-2^{-21}$. For every vertex $q$ of $Q_0$, both axes $n\in\{u,v\}$ and both
signs $\sigma\in\{-1,1\}$, require

$$
\sigma n(t)\cdot(q-c)\le a.
$$

Call the resulting closed set of owner0 poses $G(Q_0)$. Convexity extends the
inequalities from vertices to every point of $Q_0$. Because $a<1/2$, every point is
strictly interior to the unit square, including when a guard inequality holds with
equality.

Put $x=q_x-c_x$ and $y=q_y-c_y$. Multiplication by the strictly positive denominator
$1+t^2$ gives the exact inequalities

$$
p_u=(a-\sigma x)-2\sigma yt+(a+\sigma x)t^2\ge0,
$$

$$
p_v=(a-\sigma y)+2\sigma xt+(a+\sigma y)t^2\ge0.
$$

They are affine in the centre for fixed $t$, quadratic in $t$ for fixed centre, and have
**joint total degree three** because of terms such as $c_xt^2$. A consumer must not
advertise a joint quadratic guard.
Retain coefficients in the fixed monomial order

$$
1,c_x,c_y,t,c_xt,c_yt,t^2,c_xt^2,c_yt^2.
$$

The new guard is intersected with the original parent’s physical pose domain and the
same container hypothesis.
It does not replace their wall, occupancy, nonoverlap or custody premises.
