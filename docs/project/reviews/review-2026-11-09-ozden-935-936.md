## The Claim and What It Needs

For $n \in \{172, 270\}$ the issue claims $s(n) \le C$, where $C$ is its 12-place print.
The certificate proves $s(n) \le S$ for its side $S$: there are $n$ closed unit squares,
rotated freely, with pairwise disjoint interiors inside the closed square $[0, S]^2$.
Since $C \ge S$, the claim follows.
A Witness/v2 corner file gives square $i$ as four rational corners $p_0, \ldots, p_3$
and nothing else, and the schema leaves the corner order to the file.
The claim rests on six hypotheses, each checked on its own:

1. **The reading.** The envelope is `packing.squares:Witness/v2`; the representation is
   `corners`, the scalars `rational`, the size `'1'`, the origin `lower-left` and the
   axes `x-right-y-up`, so the box is $[0, S]^2$. Every scalar is an integer or $p/q$
   with $q > 0$, in canonical decimal form.
2. **The count.** There are $n$ squares with distinct ids, and the side is the stated
   fraction.
3. **Unit squares.** The corners, in the listed order, are a unit square: with
   $u = p_1 - p_0$ and $f = p_3 - p_0$, $u \cdot u = 1$, $f = \pm u^\perp$ and
   $p_2 = p_0 + u + f$. The edge directions $u$ and $u^\perp$ are then exact rational
   unit vectors, so every separation measured along them is an exact length.
   Method B checks the polygon another way: four edges of length 1 with turns of one
   sign make a convex rhombus of side 1, and area 1 makes it a square.
4. **Containment.** A convex polygon lies in $[0, S]^2$ exactly when its corners do
   (method A), and exactly when clipping it to the box keeps its whole area (method B).
5. **Disjoint interiors.** Two convex polygons have overlapping interiors exactly when
   no line separates them, and a separating line can always be taken parallel to an edge
   of one of them. So the interiors are disjoint exactly when, on one of the four edge
   normals $u_i, u_i^\perp, u_j, u_j^\perp$, the projections overlap in at most a point
   (method A; the pair margin is the largest gap).
   Independently, $Q_i \cap Q_j$ is convex and has positive area exactly when its
   interior is nonempty, so the interiors are disjoint exactly when the clipped
   intersection has area 0 (method B).
6. **The rotation sense.** The corners fix it.
   Reflected in their own horizontal centre lines, which reads each rotation the other
   way, both packings overlap (control G6), so the sense as written is the one that
   holds.

`l72_check.py` decides hypotheses 1 and 2 through the reader, and 3 to 5 twice: method A
in `fractions.Fraction`, method B in `gmpy2.mpq`. The two share only the reader, which
returns integers. The reader fails closed on any layout it does not expect; the JSON
twin, read with the standard library’s `json`, must hold exactly the same witness.
Method B also classes each pair and wall three ways: a nonempty clipped set of area 0 is
a contact, the empty set is clear, so its classes can be compared with method A’s margin
signs.

## Is the Improvement Real?

`l72_houses.py` writes each house witness on `main` as exact corners and decides it with
the frozen checker at its own side.
Both are accepted, with no pair touching.
It then measures each house certificate’s uniform-contraction margin exactly.
Every centre is scaled by a common factor $\lambda \le 1$, orientations kept, and the
box shrinks to the packing’s extent.
On each axis a pair’s gaps are linear in $\lambda$, so the first contact $\lambda^*$ is
exact, and the margin is the house side less the contracted extent.
The program then decides the contracted packing at $\lambda^*$ (accepted, the binding
pair touching) and $10^{-30}$ below it (refused).
