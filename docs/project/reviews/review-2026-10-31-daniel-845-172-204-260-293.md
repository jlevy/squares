**The checker.** `exact_check.py` reads a header `n S` and $n$ lines `x y t` of integers
or integer fractions, refusing any other token, a comment after the data, or a row count
other than $n$. Each line becomes a unit square with edge directions $u = (c, s)$ and
$v = (-s, c)$, $c = (1 - t^2)/(1 + t^2)$, $s = 2t/(1 + t^2)$; $c^2 + s^2 = 1$ is
asserted exactly. Its corners are $(x, y) + \alpha u + \beta v$,
$\alpha, \beta \in \{\pm\tfrac12\}$.

- **Method A, separating axes.** A square lies in the box exactly when its four corners
  do. Two convex polygons have disjoint interiors exactly when one of their edge normals
  weakly separates their projections.
  Along a unit normal $w$ of square $p$, square $p$ projects to half-width $\tfrac12$
  and square $q$ to $(|u_q \cdot w| + |v_q \cdot w|)/2$, so the gap is
  $|(c_q - c_p) \cdot w| - \tfrac12 - (|u_q \cdot w| + |v_q \cdot w|)/2$. Every pair is
  decided on its four normals, with no distance filter.
- **Method B, clipped areas.** Each square clipped to the box (Sutherland–Hodgman, in
  `Fraction`) must keep area 1. Each pair whose centres lie within $\sqrt2$ is clipped,
  one square against the other’s four half-planes, and must have area 0. A pair farther
  apart is disjoint because the two circumscribed discs, radius $\sqrt2/2$, are.
