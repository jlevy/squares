## 3. The Theorem and What Changed in the Argument

closed unit squares with pairwise disjoint interiors, each at any orientation, boundary
contact allowed. Then $s(17) > 466001/100000$.

The reduction, the charging argument, the strict cores, the envelope, the
Möbius-expanded event sweep, the boundary argument and the compactness step are exactly
those of §3 of the [4.640020 review](review-2026-09-27-n17-kleddamag-4640020.md), which
this review does not restate.
What differs is the certificate, in four places.

**Why every pattern has capacity one, and why that matters.** For a weighted threshold
with positive coefficients $a_i$ and threshold $k$, disjoint cores capture disjoint
subsets, so at most $\lfloor\Sigma a_i/k\rfloor$ fire; here $\Sigma a_i/k$ is $9/5$,
$7/4$, $9/5$, $5/3$, $5/3$, $7/4$, $3/2$ or $1$ for the eight threshold patterns, all
with floor one. For a mask rule whose listed masks pairwise intersect, two firing cores
would share a site, so at most one fires; the pairwise-intersection predicate was tested
here over the actual masks of all 78 mask patterns.
Beyond the claimed bounds, the *exact* capacity of every one of the 86 patterns, the
largest number of pairwise-disjoint winning subsets, was computed here by dynamic
programming over all $2^n$ capture patterns ($n \le 10$): it is 1 in every case.
So the certificate again has **every rule of capacity one**, which is what §7 needs.

**The catalogue.** 2,168 half-angle intervals: 2,040 of width $207107/1024000000$ (the

The README’s account (“eight of the original 2,048 intervals were subdivided into

**The cores.** At every row $B$ is chosen so that the source-style containment margin

## 7. Planning: The Ceiling, the Saturation, and Where the Queue Goes

### The lemma refutes H-248 at both of its targets

The clique-weighted ceiling lemma, proved in the $4.640020$ review’s §8 and re-proved in
the addendum’s §3, needs three things of a certificate at side `S`: every rule of
capacity one, cores strictly inside parents, and $\Gamma$ certified over every legal
parent with $17\Gamma > M$. This certificate has all three (F4, step 4, §4). So **no
family of unit squares in $[0, S]^2$ with a clique-constrained weighting summing to 17
or more exists at any $S \le 4.66001$**, and in particular none at $465/100$ or
$466/100$. H-248’s claim is refuted at both of its targets by the certificate itself, as
H-243’s $4.63$ was by $4.640020$, and BC-387’s n = 17 searches at $4.65$ and $4.66$ are
pre-empted before they run.
The architecture’s ceiling now lies in $[4.66001, s(17)]$, inside $[4.66001, 4.67553]$.

**What target remains meaningful.** The lemma is monotone in the side (a family at `S`
is a family at every `S' ≥ S`) and tight at $s(17)$ (two copies of any packing of
seventeen give $\alpha^{\ast} = 17$), so a family search can only ever show where the
architecture stops short of a *given* target below $s(17)$. The window left is $0.0155$
wide. The two targets worth a run are $4675/1000$, just under Bidwell, where a family is
easiest to fit and where a hit would say the architecture cannot close the gap at all,
and $467/100$, the round target the author has not claimed; the search should run at
$4675/1000$ first, since a family there is implied by one at $467/100$ and not
conversely. If the exact checker finds no family at $4675/1000$ at the search budget,
that is inconclusive as H-248 already says, and the question of whether the recipe
reaches $4.67$ is then the producer’s to answer, not the search’s. The n = 12 half of
BC-387 (H-244) is untouched.

### What the surplus and the flat ledger imply

The surplus $54{,}340$ is not slack in the argument.
$M/17 = 1{,}000{,}023{,}647.5$ and $\Gamma = 1{,}000{,}026{,}844$, so each core carries
$3{,}196.5$ units over its share, a relative $3.2\times10^{-6}$, against $331$ units at
$4.640020$; both read as the residue of rounding an LP at normalised charge 1 to integer
weights. What is new is F5: every row is at the same minimum and the next cell value is
within $10^{-7}$ of it.
At $4.640020$ the addendum’s conditional cap on reweighting was `17Γ'/M' ≤ 1.00127`, the
plateau, under the assumption that the source’s LP was optimal for its dictionary; here
the plateau is the floor, so under the same assumption the cap is the present ratio
itself, $17\Gamma/M = 1.0000032$, up to the rounding residue.
The assumption is not checkable from the certificate (`METHOD.md` says only that the
weights are integers at scale $10^9$ and that rounding is not relied on), but a ledger
flat to $10^{-7}$ on every row is what an optimal weighting looks like, and no reading
of it leaves measurable room.
Idea 259, a first-party reweighting of the published dictionary at a higher target, is
therefore closed by this certificate and should be retired rather than left “not
selected”: there is nothing to reweight.
The addendum’s other reading stands and sharpens: the fixed-weight charge is a step
function of the side, the optimised sites sit on core boundaries, and the only way up is
new sites and rules, which is the producer that does not exist here.

### Whether the recipe can reach `4.67`

Evidence for: three releases in six days ($4.6198$, $4.640020$, $4.66001$) with the same
engines and a dictionary that grew each time (8,876 to 20,856 sites; 29 to 86 patterns;
a quarter and then a third of the budget in families the previous release did not have),
two steps of $+0.020$ each; the lemma cannot rule $4.67$ out, since the ceiling is
bounded only by $s(17)$; and the two-minute anneals in the plan found no refuting family
at $4.65$ or $4.66$, which the certificate now explains.
Evidence against: the gap to Bidwell is $0.0155$, less than one of the author’s steps;
the certificate is saturated everywhere (F5), so the next step is again a whole new
dictionary and not a refinement; the eight subdivided intervals show the catalogue
already at the resolution where interval width costs charge; and the author claims
nothing (“No 4.67 claim or improved packing”, `CHANGELOG.md`). The honest reading is
that $4.67$ is plausible for the recipe and unpriced, and that the only first-party
instrument that prices it cheaply is the family search at $4675/1000$; this review does
not recommend a first-party n = 17 producer on this evidence, as the plan did not.

## 8. The Rung

Under [`epistemics.md`](../../../epistemics.md):

- **`V4`.** An exact-algebraic certificate with a replay command and, on the
  coordinator’s replay of `verify.py`, a passing status; the source’s two-language
  receipts and the full independent re-derivation here support it.
- **`C3`,** once the repository replay is recorded: repository-origin exact evidence
  with a certificate and a passing replay.
  This review’s independent sweep of all 2,168 rows is repository-origin exact evidence
  of the same method, so it strengthens `C3` and does not reach `C4`.
- **Not `C4`.** One method (F1); the native route needs the intersecting-family atom and
  a larger cap lift before it can decide the certificate.

External bounds carry no `T-NNN` row, so the `C5` mapping is not recorded; this review
is the case file’s `audit_record`. The advance should be quoted against $4.640020$, the
rung it displaces, and the citation to `PROOF.md` should carry the $4.640020$ review’s
