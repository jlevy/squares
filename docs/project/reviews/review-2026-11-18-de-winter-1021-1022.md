## The Claims and What They Need

Each issue claims $s(n) \le C$, $C$ the 12-place print of the certificate’s side $S$
rounded up. A certificate proves $s(n) \le S$ when its $n$ closed unit squares, rotated
freely, have pairwise disjoint interiors inside the closed square $[0, S]^2$; the claim
follows when $C \ge S$, and is decided directly by placing the same squares in
$[0, C]^2$.

Each report has two files.

- **Form J**, `n2NN_exact.json`: one JSON object with exactly the keys `n`, `side`,
  `square_size`, `format` and `squares`, each square `[center_x, center_y, cos, sin]`,
  and the format sentence “(cos, sin) is the unit direction of one edge”.
  The square is $C + \alpha u + \beta v$ with
  $\lvert\alpha\rvert, \lvert\beta\rvert \le 1/2$, $u = (\cos, \sin)$ and
  $v = (-\sin, \cos)$; it is the same square whichever of its four edge directions is
  given. It states no origin.
- **Form C**, `n2NN_witness.yaml`: a softschema envelope
  (`contract: packing.squares:Witness/v2`) around a witness with
  `representation: corners`, `scalar.kind: rational`, `origin: lower-left` and
  `axes: x-right-y-up`, four rational corners per square, ids `square-1` to `square-n`,
  and a claim block saying `coordinate_provenance: reported`.

The hypotheses are five:

1. **The reading.** Form J is ASCII JSON with no duplicate key, no non-integer number,
   no constant, exactly its five keys, `square_size` the string `1`, the format sentence
   as quoted, and exactly $n$ rows of four scalars.
   Form C is one YAML document with no anchor, alias, tag, directive, flow collection,
   duplicate key, tab or carriage return, holding only plain or single-quoted scalars,
   the side and every corner plain; the envelope above, the witness keys inside the
   schema’s list with the required ones present, the squares in order with exactly an id
   and four corners each.
   Every scalar is `p` or `p/q` with an optional minus, no plus, no leading zero,
   $q \ge 1$ and no negative zero; lowest terms are recorded, not required.
   Every scalar is read twice, by the parser and line by line, with the same result.
2. **The count and the twin.** $n$ squares in each file, one side in both, and square
   $k$ of form C the square of row $k$ of form J: its corners are $C \pm u/2 \pm v/2$,
   as a cyclic list in either sense.
3. **Unit squares.** In form J, $\cos^2 + \sin^2 = 1$ exactly.
   In form C, the corners in the order listed have four sides of squared length 1 and
   two diagonals of squared length 2.
4. **Containment.** Each square lies in $[0, S]^2$, origin at the lower-left corner.
5. **Disjoint interiors.** No two squares’ interiors meet.

`r1021_check.py` decides the first two through the readers and the next three twice.

- **Method A** (`r1021_geom.py`, Python integers) is handed poses and never a corner.
  Each centre is written as $(X, Y)/E$ and each direction as $(a, b)/r$ in integers.
  On each of the four edge normals $w$ of a pair the two squares’ half-widths add to the
  same $H = 1/2 + (\lvert\cos D\rvert + \lvert\sin D\rvert)/2$, $D$ the angle between
  them, and the separation is $\lvert d \cdot w\rvert - H$, $d$ the centre difference.
  All four share the denominator $2 E_i E_j r_i r_j$, so the margin, their maximum, is
  decided on one integer numerator and kept exact: positive is apart, zero is contact
  with disjoint interiors, negative is overlap.
  Two convex polygons have disjoint interiors exactly when a line parallel to an edge of
  one of them weakly separates them, so these are exactly the three classes.
  A wall’s clearance is the signed distance from the square’s extreme point to it, from
  the half-extent $(\lvert a\rvert + \lvert b\rvert)/(2r)$.
- **Method B** (`r1021_clip.py`, `gmpy2.mpq`) is handed corners and nothing else.
  A pair is decided by clipping one square against the four closed half-planes of the
  other (Sutherland–Hodgman, keeping points on the line), both ways round, the two
  results required to agree in class and area: empty is apart, non-empty with zero area
  is contact, positive area is overlap.
  A square listed clockwise is clipped with its half-planes reversed.
  Each wall is classed by clipping the square against the closed half-plane outside it,
  and containment is the area inside the box, exactly 1.

The two share the readers (`r1021_read.py`) and nothing else.
Form J is decided by method A natively and by method B on the corners its rows imply;
form C by method B natively and by method A on the poses its corners imply.
The verdict is `ACCEPT` only when both files read, they are one packing, both decisions
accept every square, wall and pair with equal classes everywhere, and the margins and
clearances from the two files are identical.

**Option D’s quantities.** The owner’s decision (`think-b0w5`, 17 November 2026): a
re-certification of a standing packing is a new result only if its side is below the
predecessor’s side once the predecessor is shrunk until its squares first touch.
`r1021_contract.py` shrinks a certificate exactly: every centre is scaled by one factor
$\lambda$, each square keeping its size and angle, and a pair keeps disjoint interiors
exactly while $\lambda \ge f$, its contact factor $H / \max_w \lvert d \cdot w\rvert$,
so the squares first touch at $\lambda^* = \max f$ over all pairs.
The shrunk side is the side of the smallest square box holding the shrunk packing, the
larger of its width and height, and the contraction margin is $S$ less that side: the
box’s slack at scale 1 plus what the contraction closes.
Each step’s test, successor below the shrunk predecessor, is a comparison of two exact
fractions, and `r1021_measure.py` decides every shrunk packing by both methods, which
must accept it with exactly the binding pairs touching.

**Identity.** `r1021_identity.py` maps the predecessor onto the successor under each of
the box’s eight symmetries, in the lower-left frame and with the box centres aligned,
pairs centres by nearest neighbour (decided exactly among the four nearest found in
binary64) and by the optimal assignment of least summed squared move (chosen in binary64
with SciPy, every reported move computed exactly), and counts the squares that move
beyond $10^{-9}$ to $10^{-1}$. A pairing is one to one when every square of the
predecessor is taken exactly once.
