## 3. The Theorem and the Charging Argument

**Theorem.** Let $s(17)$ be the least side of a closed square containing seventeen
closed unit squares with pairwise disjoint interiors, each at any orientation, boundary
contact allowed. Then $s(17) > 232001/50000$.

**Reduction.** A packing at side $S = 232001/50000$ scales by $A = L/S$ to seventeen
parents of side $A = 32950/33143$ inside $[0, L]^2$, $L = 4613/1000$. The certificate
gives a finite D4-invariant site set (1,116 orbits, 8,876 distinct sites, every one
inside the container) and 546 rule orbits, each a complete D4 orbit of 4 or 8 site
tuples carrying one nonnegative integer weight.
Every point orbit carries weight zero; all charge is in the rule orbits.
A closed core $Q$ **captures** a site if the site lies in $Q$. A rule **fires** on $Q$
when the captured subset of its tuple is a winning subset:

| Family | Orbits | Winning subsets | Capacity bound claimed |
| --- | ---: | --- | ---: |
| One of one (a point) | 280 | the site | 1 |
| Two of three | 155 | any 2 | $\lfloor\frac{3}{2}\rfloor = 1$ |
| Three of five | 26 | any 3 | $\lfloor\frac{5}{3}\rfloor = 1$ |
| Four of seven | 1 | any 4 | $\lfloor\frac{7}{4}\rfloor = 1$ |
| Weighted $(2,1,1,1) \ge 3$ | 54 | $\lbrace0,1\rbrace,\lbrace0,2\rbrace,\lbrace0,3\rbrace,\lbrace1,2,3\rbrace$ and supersets | $\lfloor\frac{5}{3}\rfloor = 1$ |
| Winning-subset rules on 7 sites | 30 | 1 to 12 listed pairwise-intersecting masks | 1 |

The charge of $Q$ is `F(Q) = Σ w·[rule fires on Q]`. The budget $M$ is the sum over
listed tuples of weight times capacity, $M = 16{,}978{,}369{,}232$, re-derived here.

**Why the budget bounds the total charge of disjoint cores.** Take closed cores
$Q_1,\ldots,Q_{17}$, pairwise disjoint as sets.
A site lies in at most one of them.
(a) A weighted threshold with positive coefficients $a_i$ and threshold $k$: each firing
core captures a subset of coefficient sum at least $k$, the captured subsets are
disjoint, so the number of firing cores is at most $\lfloor\Sigma a_i/k\rfloor$.
Ordinary $k$-of-`n` is $a_i = 1$, and a point is $n = k = 1$. (b) A winning-subset rule
whose listed masks pairwise intersect: two firing cores would contain winning subsets
$W \subseteq Q_i$, $W^{\prime} \subseteq Q_j$ with $W \cap W^{\prime} \ne \emptyset$,
putting a site in both cores; so at most one fires.
Hence $\Sigma_i F(Q_i) \le M$. The `pairwise-intersecting` predicate the checkers test
is exactly what (b) needs, and it was tested here over the actual masks of all 30
orbits; brute force over all $2^7$ capture patterns gives maximum disjoint-winner count
1 for every one of the 29 distinct rule patterns, weighted and ordinary ones included.
So every rule in this certificate has **capacity one**, a fact §8 returns to.

**Why every strictly interior core carries at least $\Gamma$.** For a parent at
half-angle $u = \tan(\theta/2)$, $\theta \in [0, \pi/2)$, and the interval `[a, b] ∋ u`
of the catalogue, the core is the concentric closed square of side $B$ at the midpoint
orientation $\theta(t)$. It lies strictly inside the open parent iff
$B(\cos \delta + |\sin \delta|) < A$ with $\delta = \theta(t) - \theta(u)$, which needs
$|\delta| \le \pi/4$ (checked at both endpoints as `dot > 0`, `dot ≥ |cross|`) and the
monotonicity of $\cos \delta + |\sin \delta|$ in $|\delta|$ on that range, so the
endpoint check suffices (F2). The legal parent centres at half-angle $u$ form the square
$[A(c(u)+s(u))/2, L - A(c(u)+s(u))/2]^2$; over $[a, b]$ their union lies in $[r, L-r]^2$
with `r = A·min{c+s at a, b}/2`, because $c(u)+s(u) = \sqrt{2}\cdot\sin(\theta+\pi/4)$
is concave (F2). The sweep computes, for each interval, the exact minimum of $F$ over
every open event cell that meets the envelope $[r, L-r]^2$, a superset of the legal
centres. Boundary and event-line centres are covered by monotonicity: capture sets are
closed, so the captured set at such a centre contains the captured set at every nearby
generic centre, every rule is up-closed, and the envelope is a closed square with
nonempty interior, so every point of it is a limit of generic interior points of swept
cells. Degenerate (segment) rectangles the sweep drops meet no open cell and so change
nothing. The 2,048 intervals chain without gap from $0$ to $207107/500000$, where
$u^2 + 2u - 1 = 309449/250000000000 > 0$, so they cover $\tan(\pi/8)$ and
$\theta \in [0, \pi/4]$; since the sites, rules and weights are D4-invariant,
`F(gQ) = F(Q)` for the eight container symmetries, and a parent at
$\theta \in (\pi/4, \pi/2)$ is handled by reflecting it alone across the diagonal,
without moving the packing.

**Contradiction and strictness.** Cores strictly inside parents with disjoint interiors
are pairwise disjoint even when parents touch, so $17\Gamma \le \Sigma F(Q_i) \le M$;
the sweep gives $\Gamma = 998{,}727{,}933$ and $17\Gamma - M = 5{,}629 > 0$. Bounded
centres and orientations are compact and containment and interior-disjointness are
closed, so the minimum side is attained and the exclusion at `S` gives $s(17) > S$.

### F1. One method, two implementations; the native route cannot load the certificate (blocks `C4`)

`verify_global_variable.js` is a structural translation of `exact_general.py` plus
`integer_sweep.py`: the same D4 expansion, the same Möbius transform, the same
polygon-crossing slab query with `bisect_right(floor) − 1` and `bisect_left(ceil)`, the
same lazy segment tree.
It is separate code, in BigInt with `Float64Array` accumulators guarded by the $2^{50}$
bound and a per-query `isSafeInteger` check, and it parses the 104-digit integers
losslessly by stringifying them with a regular expression before `JSON.parse`, so unlike
R052’s checker it does not need Node 24. But it is the same method, and this review’s
own instrument is a third implementation of the same event-cell method, differently
constructed. Under [`epistemics.md`](../../../epistemics.md) that is `C3`.

The method-distinct route is the native interval branch and bound, and
`ParentCoreCertificate` in `packing/src/sqpack/fractional/parent_core.py` holds only
point atoms and $k$-of-`m` threshold atoms.
This certificate’s weighted thresholds and winning-subset rules have no native
representation, so a native decision needs one new atom kind before any ceiling question
arises: an **intersecting-family atom** given by its minimal winning subsets, of which
points, $k$-of-`m` at capacity one, the weighted $(2,1,1,1) \ge 3$ rule and the 30 mask
rules are all instances.
On a box of centres such an atom surely fires when some winning subset is surely
captured and cannot fire when none is possibly captured, so the interval engine’s bound
is mechanical.
The rectangle count after expansion and merging (up to 13,284 a row) again
exceeds the ceilings BC-386 is lifting.

### F4. The dictionary is uniformly capacity one (note, and a lemma for §8)

Every one of the 29 distinct rule patterns has exact capacity 1, by brute force.
That is not an accident of this certificate but a property of the family: a monotone
rule has capacity one exactly when its minimal winning subsets pairwise intersect, so
“pairwise-intersecting subset rules” are the whole class, and the ordinary and weighted
thresholds here are members of it.
Sixteen of the 30 mask rules are Fano planes (seven triples, each pair of sites in
exactly one), the most permissive intersecting family of triples on seven points; two
others (orbits 521 and 527) list a single one-site winning mask and so are ordinary
point charges written on seven sites.
Harmless, and worth knowing when reading the budget shares in §8.

## 8. Planning: What `4.640020` Says About the Ceiling and the Next Slice

### The capacity-one lemma, and what the new certificate does to it

The [post-R052 plan](../specs/archive/plan-2026-09-25-after-r052-planning.md) derived
but did not review a ceiling lemma.
It holds, and the proof is short.
Let a certificate consist of capacity-one rules (F4) with cores strictly inside parents.
Let $P_1,\ldots,P_N$ be legal unit squares in the container at side `S` whose
interior-overlap graph is triangle-free, and `Qᵢ ⊂ int Pᵢ` their cores, each with
$F(Q_i) \ge \Gamma$. If a rule fires on two cores they share a site, so their parents
overlap; the firing cores form a clique of the overlap graph, so at most two fire.
Hence $N\Gamma \le \Sigma F(Q_i) \le 2M$, and with $17\Gamma > M$ this forces $N < 34$.

The same argument gives the exact dual.
Assign weights $w_i \ge 0$ to a finite family of legal squares with `Σ_{i∈K} wᵢ ≤ 1` on
every clique $K$ of the parent-overlap graph.
Then $\Gamma\cdot\Sigma w_i \le \Sigma w_i F(Q_i) \le M$, so **any family with such a
weighting summing to 17 or more refutes every capacity-one certificate at that side**,
whatever its sites and rules.
Triangle-free 34-families are the case $w_i = \tfrac{1}{2}$; `N` squares with no
$\omega+1$ pairwise overlapping and $N \ge 17\omega$ are the uniform case
$w_i = 1/\omega$; the general instrument is the clique-constrained fractional stable-set
value of the overlap graph, an LP over the family’s cliques.
It is a necessary condition on the architecture only: where no such family exists, a
finite certificate may still fall short.

Applied to this certificate: since every rule has capacity one, **the $4.640020$ proof
is also a proof that no triangle-free family of 34 unit squares fits in a square of side
$4.640020$**, and no family with clique-weighted value 17 or more either.
That decides H-243’s first target: no family exists at $463/100$, so BC-387’s search at
$4.63$ is pre-empted and its $4.65$ target is the live one; the plan’s trigger “a
first-party producer is reconsidered only if the ceiling is at least $4.64$” is met by
the third party rather than by the search.
The ceiling lies in $[4.640020, s(17)]$; two copies of any packing of seventeen show the
lemma is tight at $s(17)$ itself, so the lemma can never prove the architecture stops
short of the truth, only where it stops short of a given target.

### How saturated the method is, and where the gain came from

The plan read R052 as “nearly converged”: 95 % of its rows within $10^{-3}$ of $\Gamma$,
reweighting worth at most $+0.0014$. That reading was correct about R052’s frozen
dictionary and wrong as a reading of the architecture, which the release shows by buying
$+0.02$ with a new dictionary at a *coarser* catalogue: 2,048 intervals of $0.023^\circ$
against R052’s 15,721. What changed is the dictionary, not the sweep:

A quarter of the budget is in families R052 did not have, and the sites are continuously

### Transfer to n = 11 and other n

- **n = 11.** The record is $31/8 < s(11) \le 3.87708\ldots$, a gap of $0.0021$, under
  the owner’s hold on lower-bound increments.
  The transferable content is the ceiling instrument, not the producer: a triangle-free
  family of 22 unit squares, or a clique-weighted family of value 11, below $3.8771$
  would show the capacity-one architecture cannot close n = 11 at all, which settles
  whether BC-389’s two-class certificate is the right tool; if none exists near the top
  of the gap, the free-site plus intersecting-rule dictionary is the producer to try
  when the hold lifts.
- **n = 12, 18, 21.** The same instrument prices each case in minutes once built (H-244
  at n = 12 with 24 squares).
  Where the ceiling clears the target, the $4.640020$ recipe transfers whole: free
  rational sites, capacity-one rules including Fano and general intersecting families, a
  coarse catalogue with maximal cores, and the Möbius-expanded event sweep.
- **The native engine.** One new atom kind serves every case above and is the shortest
  path to `C4` for the strongest n = 17 bound.
