## Proof Obligations

For each rational witness, I rebuilt the vertices from the centre and the half-angle
tangent $u$ in the matching `.cert` row.
The rotation has cosine $(1-u^2)/(1+u^2)$ and sine $2u/(1+u^2)$. Applying it to the four
offsets $(\pm 1/2,\pm 1/2)$ gave the same four exact points as the matching corner YAML
row for all 465 records.
Both encodings give the same exact side at each count.
I checked the geometry on the cyclic YAML corner encodings.

With cyclic vertices $p,q,r,s$, the checker requires $q-p$ and $s-p$ to be perpendicular
unit vectors and $r-q=s-p$. These identities establish a convex unit square.
Vertex containment in the closed box establishes containment of the whole square by
convexity. For each unordered pair, exact weak separation of its projections on a
coordinate axis suffices to make the interiors disjoint.
If the coordinate axes fail to separate the pair, the checker tests the normals of two
adjacent sides from each square.
The separating axis theorem for convex polygons makes those four axes sufficient.
The weak inequality allows contact.
Exact arithmetic accounts for all pairs, including those separated by the coordinate
axes.

For the quadratic data I evaluated the supplied rational coefficient vectors at
$t=(-6+\sqrt{3})/11$ and checked that the declared rational interval isolates this root
of $11t^2+12t+3$. All rows have `m=0`. I decoded the centre and half-angle tangent in
each row and constructed its four vertices using the same rotation formula.
Coordinates and geometry comparisons use exact $a+b\sqrt{3}$ with $a,b\in\mathbb{Q}$.
For opposite-sign terms, comparing $a^2$ to $3b^2$ settles their sum’s sign.
For each side, I checked identity with the stated expression, substitution in its stated
polynomial, strict ordering below the other root, and isolation in the declared rational
interval. Both supplied rational sides exceed their quadratic counterparts by a quantity
strictly between $10^{-67}$ and $10^{-66}$.

## Findings

- **Minor, tilt convention.** The class stored in both `algebraic/n202.minpoly.json` and
  `algebraic/n263.minpoly.json` (line 1 in each) has a signed negative angle.
  Its cosine is $(14+3\sqrt{3})/26$ and its signed sine is $(-21+2\sqrt{3})/26$. Thus
  $4\cos\theta-6\sin\theta=7$ at the encoded angle.
  The plus sign in #1037 holds when $\theta$ denotes the positive magnitude of the
  common nonzero tilt.
  There are 70 squares in that class at each count.
- **Note, corner configuration.** In the quadratic 263-square packing, precisely the 202
  squares listed in `algebraic/n202_corner.json`, lines 6–209, lie wholly in the closed
  lower-left box of side $S_{263}-2$. That side equals $S_{202}$. Comparing exact point
  sets in the published frame gives 119 common poses with the standalone quadratic
  202-square certificate.
  The other 83 poses differ in that frame.
  The issue’s nesting claim only gives the count.
  In the rational data, all 202 nested poses equal the standalone 202-square witness in
  the same frame.
- **Note, attribution scope.** Both rational YAML files, lines 22–25, point to
  `../sources/GeiserX_n263.csv` and the same upstream revision.
  The exact rational containment is consistent with this metadata.
  Pose-by-pose attribution to the upstream submission and identity with any prior
  registered witness remain separate comparisons.
  The geometry review establishes existence of packings at the checked sides.
  Tightness for the specified contact systems and global optimality were outside its
  scope.

For public wording, call the angle in the plus-sign identity the magnitude of the shared
