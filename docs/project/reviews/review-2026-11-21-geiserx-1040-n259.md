## Claim and Hypotheses

Let $Q_i$ be the closed square described by row $i$, with rows numbered 1 through 259.
The sufficient hypotheses are:

1. The input gives exactly 259 squares and a positive exact rational side $L$.
2. Each $Q_i$ is a unit square in the Euclidean plane, not merely a quadrilateral whose
   coordinates appear plausible.
3. Every $Q_i$ is contained in the closed box $[0,L]^2$.
4. For each $i<j$, a line separates the interiors of $Q_i$ and $Q_j$.

Then the collection is a packing of 259 unit squares, so the infimum container side
$s(259)$ is at most $L$. Neither a search algorithm nor a claim that the placement is
locally or globally optimal is needed for this implication.

The interpretation of the JSON requires the author’s stated half-angle convention.
The YAML interpretation requires the listed points to be cyclic vertices, which the
checker verifies. The coordinate direction and origin are explicit in its header.
All inequalities allow boundary contact.
The supplied certificate in fact has positive separation for every square pair and some
exact contacts with the box.

## Derivation and Checks

### JSON: Centers and Rational Rotation

For each rational $t=a/b$ with $b>0$, define

$$
C=b^2-a^2,\qquad S=2ab,\qquad D=b^2+a^2.
$$

Then $D>0$ and $C^2+S^2=D^2$. Thus $u=(C,S)/D$ and $v=(-S,C)/D$ are perpendicular unit
vectors. The row describes

$$
Q=c+[-1/2,1/2]u+[-1/2,1/2]v.
$$

This is a unit square.
The checker explicitly verifies the rational rotation identity for all 259 rows.

The half-width in either coordinate direction is
$h=(\lvert C\rvert+\lvert S\rvert)/(2D)$. The four inequalities $c_x-h\ge0$,
$c_y-h\ge0$, $L-c_x-h\ge0$, and $L-c_y-h\ge0$ therefore imply containment.
The checker evaluates all 1,036 of them exactly.

For a unit direction $w$, projection of $Q_i$ is a closed interval centered at
$c_i\cdot w$ with radius

$$
r_i(w)=(\lvert u_i\cdot w\rvert+\lvert v_i\cdot w\rvert)/2.
$$

If $\lvert(c_j-c_i)\cdot w\rvert\ge r_i(w)+r_j(w)$, these intervals have disjoint
interiors, and their perpendicular projection lines separate the square interiors.
The checker tries $u_i,v_i,u_j,v_j$ for every unordered pair and requires at least one
nonnegative gap. Soundness of accepting the pair needs only this exhibited line; it does
not depend on assuming that a failed candidate search proves overlap.

Writing $c_i=(X_i,Y_i)/q_i$, let $\Delta_x=X_jq_i-X_iq_j$ and $\Delta_y=Y_jq_i-Y_iq_j$.
Also set

$$
K=\lvert C_iC_j+S_iS_j\rvert+\lvert C_iS_j-S_iC_j\rvert.
$$

The numerator of the gap on $u_i$, over positive denominator $2q_iq_jD_iD_j$, is

$$
2\lvert\Delta_xC_i+\Delta_yS_i\rvert D_j
-q_iq_j(D_iD_j+K).
$$

The other three numerators follow by using $v_i,u_j,v_j$. Their maximum must be
nonnegative. This is the integer expression in `check_centers`, starting at
[reviewer’s final replay program](../../../packing/resources/web/geiserx-1040-n259-review-2026-11-21/verify_n259.py.txt),
line 175. The full scan evaluates 133,644 such candidate inequalities, with no
approximate filter or skipped pairs.

### YAML: Direct Corners and Oriented Edges

The YAML path does not reconstruct corners from the JSON. It reads each of the four
supplied rational vertices directly and clears their denominators to obtain integer
vertices $P_k$ over one positive denominator $d$ for that square.

For each quadrilateral it verifies all four consecutive edge lengths squared equal
$d^2$, all four adjacent edge dot products are zero, opposite corners have equal sums,
and the first two edge vectors have cross product $d^2>0$. Consequently the points in
their listed order form a counterclockwise unit square.
These checks rule out degenerate, reflected-order, self-crossing, nonunit and
nonorthogonal shapes.
Every vertex is then checked against all four box walls.
A convex square is the convex hull of its vertices, so vertex containment implies square
containment.

For an edge from $p$ to $p+e$ of a counterclockwise square, its interior lies in
$e\mathbin{\times}(x-p)>0$. If all four corners of another square satisfy
$e\mathbin{\times}(x-p)\le0$, their convex hull lies in the opposite closed half-plane.
The square interiors are disjoint.

If the owner has denominator $d_a$, the other square denominator $d_b$, the owner edge
starts at integer point $P$ with integer direction $E$, and a target corner is $R/d_b$,
the cross-product numerator is

$$
E_x(R_y d_a-P_y d_b)-E_y(R_x d_a-P_x d_b),
$$

over positive denominator $d_a^2d_b$. Each owner edge has unit length, so the negated
maximum cross product over the target corners is a signed distance from that edge line.
The pair is accepted when one of the eight owner-edge choices, four from each square,
has a nonnegative distance.
Selection and sign decisions use integer cross multiplication.

`edge_gaps` at line 227 and `check_polygons` at line 242 implement this direct
half-plane check. For each of all 33,411 pairs, all eight edges and all four target
corners are evaluated: 267,288 edge tests and 1,069,152 vertex half-plane inequalities.
There is no broad-phase filter, tolerance or omitted pair.
