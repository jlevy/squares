## The Claims and What They Need

**#939** claims $s(199) \le 14.61468985328358057514628890087947061619$, the side rounded
up at 40 places. **#946** claims
$s(172) \le 13.6146898532835805751462889008794706161897273020017576988\ldots$, the
expansion of the root, and that this root of
$p_{172}(x) = 62x^5 - 4116x^4 + 107933x^3 - 1397031x^2 + 8918035x - 22427977$ is the
exact local optimum of T-282’s arrangement.

A certificate proves $s(n) \le S$ for its own rational side $S$: $n$ closed unit
squares, rotated freely, with pairwise disjoint interiors inside the closed square
$[0, S]^2$. It needs the six hypotheses of the precedents: the reading, the count, unit
squares, containment, disjoint interiors and the rotation sense.
At 199 disjoint interiors include 25 exact contacts, so a gap of exactly 0 must be a
contact to both methods.
At 172 the tight gaps are $10^{-320}$, so both methods must decide gaps and overlaps
there exactly.

The algebraic claims need, for each quintic: irreducibility, exactly one root in the
interval the issue states below the side, and the side’s distance above it.
Local optimality is not claimed as proved, and this review does not prove it.

`l82_check.py` decides the reading and the count through the reader, and the rest twice:
method A in `fractions.Fraction`, method B in `gmpy2.mpq`. The reader fails closed on
any scalar not a rational literal in lowest terms, on ids out of order, on a count that
differs from $n$, and on any key it does not expect.
