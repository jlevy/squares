## The Claim and What It Needs

The issue claims $s(179) \le C$ with $C = 13.880960371559$, the 12-place print of the
certificate’s side $S$, rounded up.
The certificate proves $s(179) \le S$: there are 179 closed unit squares, rotated
freely, with pairwise disjoint interiors inside the closed square $[0, S]^2$. The claim
follows when $C \ge S$, and directly when the same squares are accepted in $[0, C]^2$.

The hypotheses are six:

1. **The reading.** The envelope is `packing.squares:Witness/v2` with `representation:
   corners`, `scalar.kind: rational`, `square_size: '1'`, `origin: lower-left`,
   `axes: x-right-y-up`, `angle_unit: not-applicable`; every scalar is a canonical $p$
   or $p/q$ with $q > 1$ and $\gcd(p, q) = 1$; the JSON twin holds the same tree.
2. **The count.** 179 squares with ids 1 to 179 in order.
3. **Unit squares.** Each listed quadruple of corners is a unit square, listed in cyclic
   order.
4. **Containment.** Each square lies in $[0, S]^2$.
5. **Disjoint interiors.** No two squares’ interiors meet.
6. **The rotation sense.** The corners are read in the plane as given, $x$ to the right
   and $y$ up; control G6 shows that reading the rotation the other way changes the
   verdict.

`l108_check.py` decides the first two through the reader, and the next three twice.

- **Method A** (`l108_sat.py`, `gmpy2.mpq`). A square is accepted when $u = p_1 - p_0$
  has $u \cdot u = 1$, $w = p_3 - p_0$ is $u$ turned a quarter either way, and
  $p_2 = p_0 + u + w$; its edge normals $u$ and $w$ are then exact rational unit
  vectors. A pair’s margin is the largest projection gap over the four normals of the two
  squares: positive is apart, zero is contact with disjoint interiors, negative is
  overlap. Two convex polygons with disjoint interiors are weakly separated by a line
  parallel to one of their edges, so the three signs are exactly those three classes.
  A wall’s clearance is the least distance from a corner to it.
- **Method B** (`l108_clip.py`, `fractions.Fraction`). A quadrilateral is a unit square
  when its four sides have squared length 1, both diagonals squared length 2 (a rhombus
  with equal diagonals is a square) and its shoelace area is $\pm 1$. A pair is the
  first square clipped by the second’s four closed half-planes, with boundary points
  kept: empty is apart, a non-empty result of area 0 is contact, positive area is
  overlap. For convex bodies the interior of the intersection is the intersection of the
  interiors, so the area decides.
  A wall is the square clipped by the wall’s closed outer half-plane, read the same
  three ways, and containment is also decided by clipping to the box and keeping area 1.

The two share only the reader, which hands them integers.
The reader (`l108_reader.py`) refuses YAML anchors, aliases, explicit tags, duplicate
keys and any resolved type but string, integer, mapping and sequence; it refuses witness
keys outside the schema’s list, and any layout it does not expect; it requires the JSON
twin, read with floats, constants and duplicate keys refused, to hold exactly the same
tree with exactly the same types.
The verdict is `ACCEPT` only when both methods accept every square, wall and pair, their
classes agree everywhere, and their orientation readings agree.
