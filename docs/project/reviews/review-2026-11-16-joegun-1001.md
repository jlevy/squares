## The Claims and What They Need

Issue #1001 claims $s(n) \le C_n$ at five counts, each $C_n$ the 12-place print of the
certificate’s side $S_n$, rounded up.
Each certificate proves $s(n) \le S_n$: there are $n$ closed unit squares, rotated
freely, with pairwise disjoint interiors inside the closed square $[0, S_n]^2$. The
claim follows when $C_n \ge S_n$, and directly when the same squares are accepted in
$[0, C_n]^2$.

Each file is a softschema envelope (`contract: packing.squares:Witness/v2`) around a
Witness/v2 record with `representation: corners`, `scalar.kind: rational`,
`origin: lower-left` and `axes: x-right-y-up`: four rational corners per square, ids 1
to $n$. The hypotheses are five:

1. **The reading.** One YAML document with no anchors, aliases, explicit tags, duplicate
   keys, tabs or carriage returns, holding only strings, plain decimal integers,
   mappings and sequences; the envelope above, the witness keys inside the schema’s list
   with every required key present, `square_size: '1'`; the squares listed with ids 1 to
   $n$ in order, each with exactly an id and four corners of two scalars; every scalar a
   string `p` or `p/q` with no sign but an optional minus, no leading zero and
   $q \ge 1$. Lowest terms are recorded, not required.
2. **The count.** $n$ squares.
3. **Unit squares.** Each square’s four corners, in the order listed, are a unit square.
4. **Containment.** Each square lies in $[0, S_n]^2$; the origin is the lower-left
   corner.
5. **Disjoint interiors.** No two squares’ interiors meet.

Unlike a centre-and-direction file, a corners file has no sense of rotation to read: the
corners are the square.
The listing’s sense is decided by each method (all 1,132 squares are listed
counterclockwise), and controls G4 to G8 show that neither method depends on it.

`l186_check.py` decides the first two through the reader, and the next three twice.

- **Method A** (`l186_sat.py`, Python integers) writes each square’s eight coordinates
  over their least common denominator $D$, so its corners are integer points $Q_k/D$ and
  its edges $E_k = Q_{k+1} - Q_k$ integer vectors.
  The square is accepted when every edge has squared length $D^2$, $E_0 \cdot E_1 = 0$,
  $E_2 = -E_0$ and $E_3 = -E_1$; the sign of $E_0 \times E_1 = \pm D^2$ is its sense.
  Its two normals $E_0/D$ and $E_1/D$ have length exactly 1, so on each of the four
  normals of a pair the two squares’ corner projections give intervals whose separation
  is an exact distance, decided over the integer denominator $D_i^2 D_j$. The margin,
  the greatest of the four separations, is kept as an exact fraction: positive is apart,
  zero is contact with disjoint interiors, negative is overlap.
  Two convex polygons have disjoint interiors exactly when a line parallel to an edge of
  one of them weakly separates them (the origin then lies outside the interior of their
  Minkowski difference, whose edges are parallel to theirs), so the three signs are
  exactly those three classes.
  A wall’s clearance is the signed distance from the square’s extreme corner to it.
- **Method B** (`l186_clip.py`, `gmpy2.mpq`) is handed corners and nothing else.
  Four points are a unit square in cyclic order when all four sides have squared length
  1 and both diagonals squared length 2; the shoelace sum, $\pm 2$, gives the sense, and
  a clockwise listing is reversed before clipping.
  A pair is decided by clipping one square against the four closed half-planes to the
  left of the other’s counterclockwise edges (Sutherland–Hodgman, keeping points on the
  line), which gives the intersection exactly; it is clipped both ways round, and the
  two results must agree in class and area.
  Empty is apart; non-empty with zero area is contact, since the interior of an
  intersection of convex sets is the intersection of their interiors; positive area is
  overlap, by that area.
  Each wall is classed the same way, by clipping the square against the closed
  half-plane outside it, and containment is decided apart: a unit square lies in the box
  exactly when its intersection with the box has area exactly 1.

The two share only the reader (`l186_reader.py`), which hands them exact rationals.
The verdict is `ACCEPT` only when both methods accept every square, wall and pair, with
equal classes everywhere; `l186_decide.py` runs both over every square, wall and pair,
and `l186_check.py` adds the file’s digest and the claim’s arithmetic.

**F1. Feasibility.** Each file is an exact packing at its side: 136,579 pairs in all,
each decided by both methods with equal classes and strictly apart, and every one of the
1,132 squares a unit square inside its box by both, with area exactly 1 inside.
The bounds $s(n) \le S_n$ hold at 127, 204, 232, 264 and 305.

**F2. Controls.** As [Controls](#controls) gives them: 32 of 32 as stated at each count.

**F3. The claims.** Each $C_n$ is the ceiling of $S_n$ at 12 places, above it by
$5.13 \times 10^{-13}$, $3.88 \times 10^{-13}$, $4.27 \times 10^{-13}$,
$1.10 \times 10^{-15}$ and $3.04 \times 10^{-14}$. Control S1e decides each claim
directly: in the box of side $C_n$ the squares are accepted by both methods, with the

would not do: the square–wall pairs at the right and top that lie within $S_n$ less the
floor of their wall, 7, 18, 19, 15 and 11 of them, cross it.
A record can therefore hold each bound as the 12-place decimal the issue prints, with
the certificate’s own corners as its proof.

**F4. The side and the extent.** No certificate states a side above its packing: each
packing touches the left and bottom walls and one of the other two, so $S_n$ is the
packing’s height (127, 264) or width (204, 232, 305) exactly.
The squares fit no box smaller than $S_n$ in place or translated, since a box must hold
the packing’s larger extent, which is $S_n$. The other extent is smaller by
$5.29 \times 10^{-15}$, $3.25 \times 10^{-15}$, $2 \times 10^{-15}$,
$4.90 \times 10^{-15}$ and $3.95 \times 10^{-16}$, comparable to the binary64 spacing at
these sides ($1.8 \times 10^{-15}$ to $3.6 \times 10^{-15}$); where it comes from was
not examined. Nothing in the bound depends on it.

**F5. Margins of $10^{-11}$ and $10^{-12}$.** At 127, 204 and 305 the near pairs start
at $1.12 \times 10^{-11}$; at 232 and 264 at $10^{-12}$, and at 264 all 237 near pairs

$1.1 \times 10^{-11}$. The review does not test the mechanism.
Unlike the near pairs, the wall contacts are exact: each touched wall has a corner or an
edge whose coordinate is exactly 0 or exactly the side.
