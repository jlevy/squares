## The Claims and What They Need

Each issue claims $s(127) \le C$ for a packing of 127 unit squares, $C$ a 12-place print
of the certificate’s side $S$, rounded up, or (#1017) $S$ itself.
Each certificate proves $s(127) \le S$: there are 127 closed unit squares, rotated
freely, with pairwise disjoint interiors inside the closed square $[0, S]^2$. The claim
follows when $C \ge S$, and directly when the same squares are accepted in $[0, C]^2$.

The files come in two forms.

- **A centre file** (`n127_exact.json`): `n`, `side`, `square_size`, a `format` sentence
  and `squares`, each square four rationals `[center_x, center_y, cos, sin]`, where by
  the sentence “(cos, sin) is the unit direction of one edge”.
  The square is the set $c + a u + b v$ with $\lvert a\rvert \le 1/2$,
  $\lvert b\rvert \le 1/2$, $u = (\cos, \sin)$ and $v = (-\sin, \cos)$. The file does
  not say where the box is; this review reads it as $[0, S]^2$, the reading its twin
  states.
- **A witness file** (`n127_witness.yaml`): a softschema envelope
  (`contract: packing.squares:Witness/v2`) around a Witness/v2 record with
  `representation: corners`, `scalar.kind: rational`, `origin: lower-left` and
  `axes: x-right-y-up`: four rational corners per square.

The hypotheses are five:

1. **The reading.** One document, read strictly.
   As JSON: no duplicate key, no number but the integer count, no `null`, boolean or
   constant; exactly the five keys, `square_size` the string `1`, the format sentence
   exactly as quoted, each square exactly four string scalars.
   As YAML: block style only, with no anchors, aliases, tags, directives, flow
   collections, block scalars, duplicate keys, tabs or carriage returns, holding only
   strings, plain decimal integers, mappings and sequences, every plain scalar resolved
   here through PyYAML’s implicit-type table and refused unless it is a string or a
   decimal integer; the envelope, the witness keys inside the schema’s list with every
   required key present, each square exactly an id and four corners of two scalars, the
   ids distinct; and every corner scalar read a second time, line by line, with the same
   result. Every scalar is a string `p` or `p/q` with no sign but an optional minus, no
   leading zero and $q \ge 1$. Lowest terms are recorded, not required.
2. **The count.** 127 squares, the number the file states.
3. **Unit squares.** Each square’s four corners, in the order listed, are a unit square;
   in a centre file, the direction is a unit vector.
4. **Containment.** Each square lies in $[0, S]^2$.
5. **Disjoint interiors.** No two squares’ interiors meet.

`r127_check.py` decides the first two through the readers (`r127_read.py`), and the next
three twice (`r127_decide.py`).

- **Method A** (`r127_axes.py`, Python integers) writes each square’s eight coordinates
  over their least common denominator $D$, so its corners are integer points $Q_k/D$ and
  its edges $E_k = Q_{k+1} - Q_k$ integer vectors.
  The square is accepted when $E_0 \cdot E_0 = E_1 \cdot E_1 = D^2$,
  $E_0 \cdot E_1 = 0$, $E_2 = -E_0$ and $E_3 = -E_1$; the sign of $E_0 \times E_1$ is
  the sense of its listing.
  For a centre file the corners are $c + (\pm u \pm v)/2$, so a direction off the unit
  circle gives corners this test refuses.
  On each of the four edge directions of a pair the two squares’ corner projections give
  intervals whose signed separation is exact, over the integer denominator $D_i^2 D_j$.
  The margin, the greatest of the four, is an exact fraction: positive is apart, zero is
  contact with disjoint interiors, negative is overlap.
  Two convex polygons have disjoint interiors exactly when a line parallel to an edge of
  one of them weakly separates them, so the three signs are exactly those three classes.
  A wall’s clearance is the signed distance from the square’s extreme corner to it.
- **Method B** (`r127_area.py`, `gmpy2.mpq`) is handed points and nothing else.
  Four points are a unit square in cyclic order when all four sides have squared length
  1, both diagonals squared length 2 and the shoelace sum is $\pm 2$, which gives the
  sense. A pair is decided by building its intersection: the convex hull of the corners
  of each square that lie in the other and of the points where an edge of one meets a
  non-parallel edge of the other, all with closed tests.
  An extreme point of the intersection that is a corner of neither square lies inside an
  edge of each, and those edges cannot be parallel, so the hull is the whole
  intersection. No point is apart; a hull of zero area is contact; positive area is
  overlap, by that area.
  Each wall is classed the same way, by intersecting the square with a polygon covering
  the closed half-plane outside it, and containment is decided apart: a unit square lies
  in the box exactly when its intersection with the box has area exactly 1.

The two share only the readers, which hand them exact rationals in the file’s own terms.
The verdict is `ACCEPT` only when both methods accept every square, wall and pair, with
equal classes everywhere.

A third formulation, the closed form of `r127_frame.py`, is used for the controls’ moves
and the contraction, and is held equal to method A on every pair and wall (control C0).
A unit square with centre $c$ and unit edge direction $u$ has half-extent
$(\lvert w \cdot u\rvert + \lvert w \cdot v\rvert)/2$ on a unit direction $w$: $1/2$ on
its own two edge directions and $(\lvert\cos\Delta\rvert + \lvert\sin\Delta\rvert)/2$ on
the other square’s. So on all four candidate axes of a pair the two half-extents sum to
the same $T = (1 + \lvert\cos\Delta\rvert + \lvert\sin\Delta\rvert)/2$, and the pair’s
margin is $M - T$ with $M = \max_w \lvert w \cdot d\rvert$ over the four directions, $d$
the centre difference.

## Against the House and Each Other

### The contraction margin

`r127_contract.py` measures the margin as owner decision think-b0w5 defines it, from the
definition in the docstring of `devtools.contraction_margin`: scale every centre by a
factor $\lambda$ about the origin, every rotation fixed.
By the closed form a pair’s margin becomes $\lambda M - T$, so the pair stays
interior-disjoint iff $\lambda \ge T/M$, and the least feasible factor is
$\lambda^* = \max T/M$ over all pairs, an exact rational at which the binding pair
touches. The contracted packing is moved into the corner and boxed:
$L' = \max(\text{width}, \text{height})$, each extent read with every square’s axis
half-width $(\lvert a\rvert + \lvert b\rvert)/2$. The margin is $S - L'$, and a later
certificate beats its predecessor shrunk to first contact iff its side is below the
predecessor’s $L'$. The program decides each input by both methods first, then writes
the contracted packing as corners and decides it by both methods in the box of side
$L'$, where it must be accepted with its binding pair touching, and checks that
$\lambda^* - 10^{-30}$ makes the binding pair overlap by exactly $10^{-30} M$ (method A)
and overlap (method B).
