## The Claim and What It Needs

The issue claims $s(237) \le C$ with $C = 15.893805580573$, the 12-place print of the
certificate’s side $S$. The certificate proves $s(237) \le S$ for its side: there are
237 closed unit squares, rotated freely, with pairwise disjoint interiors inside the
closed square $[0, S]^2$. The claim follows when $C \ge S$, and directly when the same
squares are accepted in $[0, C]^2$.

The hypotheses are six:

1. **The reading.** The envelope is `packing.squares:Witness/v2` with `representation:
   corners`, `scalar.kind: rational`, `square_size: '1'`, `origin: lower-left`,
   `axes: x-right-y-up`; every scalar is a canonical $p$ or $p/q$ with $q > 1$ and
   $\gcd(p, q) = 1$; the JSON twin holds the same tree.
2. **The count.** 237 squares with ids 1 to 237 in order.
3. **Unit squares.** Each listed quadruple of corners is a unit square.
4. **Containment.** Each square lies in $[0, S]^2$.
5. **Disjoint interiors.** No two squares’ interiors meet.
6. **The rotation sense.** The corners are read in cyclic order in the plane as given,
   $x$ to the right and $y$ up; control G6 shows that reading the rotation the other way
   changes the verdict.

`l109_check.py` decides the first two through the reader, and the next three twice.

- **Method A** (`l109_axes.py`, `fractions.Fraction`). A square is accepted when
  $u = p_1 - p_0$ has $u \cdot u = 1$, $p_3 - p_0 = \pm u^\perp$ and
  $p_2 = p_0 + u + (p_3 - p_0)$; its edge directions are then exact rational unit
  vectors. A pair’s margin is the largest projection gap over the four edge directions of
  the two squares, in both senses: positive is apart, zero is contact, negative is
  overlap. For convex polygons these three classes are exactly “closed sets disjoint”,
  “touching with disjoint interiors” and “interiors meet”, since the Minkowski
  difference of two squares is a polygon whose edges are parallel to theirs.
  A wall’s clearance is the extreme corner coordinate’s distance to it.
- **Method B** (`l109_clip.py`, `gmpy2.mpq`). A polygon is a unit square when its four
  edges have squared length 1, each two consecutive edges have dot product 0, and every
  turn has the same sign.
  A pair is the first square clipped by the second’s four closed inner half-planes:
  empty is apart, area 0 is contact, positive area is overlap.
  A wall is the square clipped by the wall’s closed outer half-plane, read the same
  three ways, and containment is clipping to the box keeping area exactly 1.

The two share only the reader, which hands them integers.
The reader (`l109_reader.py`) parses and composes the YAML with PyYAML and never
constructs it; it refuses anchors, aliases, explicit tags, duplicate keys and every
resolved tag but string, integer, mapping and sequence, builds plain values itself, and
fails closed on any layout it does not expect.
It requires the JSON twin, read with the standard library with floats, constants and
duplicate keys refused, to hold exactly the same tree with exactly the same types.
The verdict is `ACCEPT` only when both methods accept every square, wall and pair and
their three-way classes agree everywhere.
