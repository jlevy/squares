## The Argument, Re-Derived

Write $\lVert x \rVert_\theta = \lVert R(-\theta)x \rVert_\infty$ for a square at angle
$\theta$, $h = 1/2$, and $U = 4822876/10^6$. The 21 marks are the images of (390,390),
(617,617), (797,359), (1000,716) and (1000,1000) under the eight symmetries of
$[0, 2000]^2$, scaled by $U/2000$: four corner marks, eight edge marks, four diagonal
marks, four marks around the centre and the centre.

**Step 0, the test box.** $(2U-7)^2 > 7$, so $U > H$, by $3.4 \times 10^{-7}$. A packing
in a square of side $L < H$ translates into $[0, U]^2$; the true walls stay where they
are.

**Step 1, the covering lemma.** Every unit square inside $[0, U]^2$ has a mark $m$ with
$\lVert m - c \rVert_\theta < (1 - \varepsilon)h$, $\varepsilon = 10^{-5}$. Your
`check_stabs.py` proves it on bisected $t$-intervals with a midpoint-frame square of
half-side $(1-\varepsilon)/(2(1 + t_1 - t_0))$, which lies in every contracted square of
the interval because $d(2\arctan t)/dt \le 2$ and a turn by $\varphi$ inflates supports
by $\cos\varphi + \lvert\sin\varphi\rvert \le 1 + \lvert\varphi\rvert$; the outer centre
square uses the endpoint minimum of the concave half-width $(\cos\theta+\sin\theta)/2$.
Each step is correct.
C2 decided the lemma independently.

**Step 2, the assignment.** Each square chooses a mark nearest in its own norm.
The chosen mark is no farther than the captured one, so it lies in the contracted
square; two squares cannot choose one mark, since it is interior to both.
Every other occupied mark lies outside the closed square (it is interior to another
square), and no mark is strictly nearer.
Ties are allowed.
The kernel’s seed, a lattice polygon inside the diamond of $L^1$-radius
$\varepsilon/4$ about the chosen mark, therefore lies inside the open square for every
admissible pose.

**Step 3, the cases.** The three unoccupied marks determine the case; a symmetry of the
test box moves any case to its representative and the packing to a packing in another
square of side $L$ inside the test box.
Your representative is the lexicographically first triple of indices in the marks’
sorted $(x, y)$ order, and a case’s number is its mask’s rank
(`verify_rack_angle.chartrows`).

**Step 4, the kernel.** For each case and terminal region, the kernel holds, per square
and per angle row, a convex lattice polygon of possible centres, and per square a
guaranteed-interior polygon $H_i$. Rows are closed intervals uniform in
$t = \tan(\theta/2)$ on $[0, 1]$, covering every orientation modulo a quarter turn.
The rules, each re-derived here from the code:

- *Outer axes and inner cores* (`rack_lattice_input.py`, `ragged_half_angles.inc`). A
  row’s four axes bound the midpoint-frame support by
  $r_m = \max(\cos\varphi + \lvert\sin\varphi\rvert)$ over its endpoints, rounded up;
  its core is the midpoint-frame square of half-side $(1 - 10^{-7})/(2r_m)$ with its
  four axis points, rounded inward and centrally symmetric, so it lies strictly inside
  every square of the row.
  The refined rows of `fine()` use integer normals within half a unit of
  $M(\cos\theta_m, \sin\theta_m)$, $M = 10^5$, with offsets
  $\lceil D(M(G+1)/(2G) + 1) \rceil$, a half-width of $(1 + 1/G)/2$ plus room for the
  rounding, and cores of half-side $(1 - 6 \cdot 10^{-6})G/(2(G+1))$; a row’s angles
  stay within $1/G$ of its $t$-midpoint, so
  $\cos\varphi + \lvert\sin\varphi\rvert \le 1 + 1/G$ covers both.
- *Initial domains.* The wall box $[w, U - w]^2$ with $w$ the endpoint minimum of the
  half-width, cut by the axes so that the square holds the seed, enclosed outward.
- *Nearest-mark bans* (`nearest_rack.py`, K1, K2). For mark $z$ within $\sqrt2$ of the
  owner $m$ and each axis $x$, the ban is the intersection over both endpoint frames of
  $\{X \cdot (z - c) \ge x \cdot (m - c)\}$ over the other three axes $X$, skipped
  unless $x \cdot z > x \cdot m$ at both.
  On its interior every such form is strictly positive at both endpoints; the forms are
  homogeneous in $(\cos\theta, \sin\theta)$, so they stay positive across the row, and
  then $\lVert z - c \rVert_\theta <
  -x\cdot(m - c) \le \lVert m - c \rVert_\theta$: $z$ is strictly nearer.
  Only interiors are removed, so ties survive.
- *Forbidden regions by guaranteed interiors* (`lattice_kernel.cpp`, K4). A centre $c$
  of square $i$ in row $k$ is removed when it lies in the open Minkowski sum
  $H_j \oplus \mathrm{core}_k$: the core of $i$ and the guaranteed interior of $j$ would
  overlap, and both lie in open squares.
- *Forbidden regions by full partner domains, inner cores* (`pair_variant.py`,
  `short_facets.py`). For every live row $l$ of $j$ and every centre of $j$, the set
  $c_j + \mathrm{core}_k \oplus \mathrm{core}_l$ has the facet normals of both cores;
  the intersection over $l$ is exactly the half-planes with per-normal minima $S$
  (normals of $\mathrm{core}_k$) and $T$ (the rest), as coded.
  The redundancy pruning removes a half-plane only when two neighbours imply it
  ($b_k\,(a \times c) \ge a_k\,(b \times c) +
  c_k\,(a \times b)$), which leaves the region unchanged.
- *Forbidden regions by row endpoint normals* (`arc_pair_variant.py`, used by the
  `verify1` binaries).
  Here the forbidden set uses the eight endpoint normals $N_d$ of each row with offsets
  built from $\mathit{lit}$, the least support of a unit square over a row (attained at
  an endpoint, because the support’s kinks fall on grid angles), and
  $\mathit{av} = \tfrac12\lvert N\rvert\cos\alpha$, $\alpha$ the angle from the endpoint
  to the row’s $t$-midpoint $\hat m$. The lemma that makes it sound: a facet normal $n$
  of square $j$ in row $l$ is $\lambda \hat e_1 + \mu \hat e_2$ with
  $\lambda, \mu \ge 0$, and
  $\lambda\cos\alpha_1 + \mu\cos\alpha_2 = n \cdot \hat m \le 1$, so the $\mathit{av}$
  terms bound $j$’s own support $1/2$; when rows $k \ne l$, the support of square $i$ is
  linear on row $l$’s cone (its facet normals lie in row $k$), so the $\mathit{lit}$
  terms bound it; when $k = l$ the `self` term $2\,\mathit{av}$ bounds both supports
  together. Every facet normal of $T_i \oplus T_j$ is then strictly inside, so the region
  forces overlap. Rows must be disjoint grid intervals, which the code checks.
- *Interior growth and contradictions* (K5, K6). Every clip rounds new vertices outward
  to their lattice cell; `edgeclip.py`’s first-exit hull contains $P \setminus
  \mathrm{int}\,F$ (a point of $P$ outside the open convex $F$ lies on a chord of $P$
  whose ends avoid it).
  $H_i$ grows only by lattice points that satisfy every half-plane of the guaranteed
  interior exactly, in integers.
  A square with no live row, or two guaranteed interiors that meet, is a contradiction;
  meeting suffices because both lie in open squares.
- *Cuts and refinement* (K3, K7). Cut types 0 to 3 are $x \le v$, $x \ge v$, $y \le v$,
  $y \ge v$; types 4 and 5 keep rows below and from an index.
  `covered()` requires every node of a cut tree to split on one complementary pair.
  After the coarse rounds, `fine()` refines live rows from the reached domains and
  reruns without the cuts, which those domains already carry.
  A terminal counts only when the binary prints `C` or `F`; `OPEN` fails the replay’s
  assertions.

Arithmetic is int64 with __int128 products; the largest products (grid $G \le 8192$,
normals near $1.3 \times 10^8$, coordinates near $5 \times 10^6$) stay inside both
types.

**Step 5, the terminal lemmas** (`wall_chord_guard.py`, `genuine_facing_bridge.py`,
`double_probe_bridge.py`). Let the true container be $[x_0, x_0 + L]^2$ inside the test
box.

- *Row support.* A vertical line $x = \xi$ that crosses each of $p$ squares between the
  two faces normal to its near-vertical axis meets each in a chord of length
  $1/\cos\varphi \ge 1$; the chords are disjoint and lie above $y_0$. If the terminal
  square is topmost, its upper-right corner $R$ satisfies $R_y - y_0 \ge p +
  (R_x - \xi)s/c$ and $R_x - x_0 \ge c + \max(0, -s)$. `orient()` takes the crossing
  interval $[c_x^{\max} - (c_{\min} - s_{\max})/2,\ c_x^{\min} + (c_{\min} -
  s_{\max})/2]$ per row state and the topmost condition from centre bounds widened by
  $\lvert\tan\rvert$; `stack()` then requires $\xi \le R_x^{\min} - e/r_l$ and
  $\xi \ge R_x^{\max} - (1 - e)c_{\min}/r_h$ with $e = s_{\max}/(1 + c_{\min})$. Using
  $1 - c = s^2/(1+c)$, these give $a(R_x - x_0) + b(R_y - y_0) \ge a + pb$ for every
  direction $(a, b)$ with slope in $[r_l, r_h]$, for both signs of $s$. In the reflected
  frame the same holds for the two-square row at $Q$, and the offsets cancel:
  $(Q - P)\cdot(a, b) \le (L-3)a + (L-4)b$.
- *Chord guard.* The slopes of $Q - P$ over the corner boxes are bracketed by the box
  corners. `low > 2 det` makes the line $PQ$ cross both $k$-faces of $f$ and of $g$
  strictly inside the faces (the condition is $\mathrm{dist} < (\cos\psi -
  \lvert\sin\psi\rvert)/2$); the positivity of the bounds at both row endpoints carries
  across the row, and box corners suffice because $\mathrm{cross}(Q - P, z - P)$ is
  affine in each coordinate.
  $P$ is a corner of another square, so it cannot lie in $f$’s open chord; with
  $(P - z)\cdot k < 1/2$ it lies before the entry face, and $Q$ after the exit face.
  The segment holds both chords: $\lvert Q - P \rvert \ge 2$.
- *Two-face guard.* The interval tests force $u_f \cdot (f - P) \ge 1/2$ and
  $u_g \cdot (Q - g) \ge 1/2$ (otherwise a corner of another square would be interior to
  $f$ or $g$), exclude $Ju_f$ and $Ju_g$ as separating axes ($\lvert\cdot\rvert < 1$)
  and reverse separation ($> -1$). With $\delta = \theta_f - \theta_g$,
  $c = \cos\delta$, $s = \sin\delta$ and
  $\lvert s\rvert/(1+c) = \lvert\tan(\delta/2)\rvert$ bounded by the half-angle
  subtraction formula, whichever of $u_f$, $u_g$ separates gives
  $w\cdot(Q - P) \ge 1 + c + s^2/(1+c) = 2$.
- *Perpendicular probes* (case 137). For each configuration an offset $\delta > 0$ with
  $p - \xi = \delta$ and $q - \eta = (b/a)\delta$ exists exactly when `lo < hi`, and the
  tilt terms cancel, giving the corner support $ah_0 + bv_0$.
- *The algebra.* With $(a, b)$ a positive unit vector, $2 \le (L-3)a + (L-4)b \le
  \sqrt{(L-3)_+^2 + (L-4)_+^2}$. For $L \le 4$ the right side is at most 1. For $L > 4$,
  $4 \le 2L^2 - 14L + 25$, so $2L^2 - 14L + 21 \ge 0$ and $L \ge (7+\sqrt7)/2$ (the
  other root, $2.18$, is excluded).
  Equality is allowed, so a branch may hold an optimal packing.

**Step 6, the cover.** The 181 refuted cases are covered by `covered()` cut trees (235
terminals). Case 53 is split into slabs of $\tau = y_5$: its four stabilizer symmetries

Case 55 is split into slabs of $x_9$. Case 137 uses its six base terminals.
