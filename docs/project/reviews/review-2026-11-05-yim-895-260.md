## The Claim and What It Needs

The claim is $s(260) \le S$: 260 closed unit squares, rotated freely, with pairwise
disjoint interiors inside the closed square $[0, S]^2$.

**The format.** `n260.cert` is in Evan Daniel’s format.
After comment lines starting with `#`, a header `260 S` is followed by 260 rows `x y t`.
Each row gives a rational centre $(x, y)$ and the rational tangent $t = \tan(\theta/2)$
of the square’s half-angle.

**The squares.** Put $c = (1 - t^2)/(1 + t^2)$ and $s = 2t/(1 + t^2)$, with $u = (c, s)$
and $u^\perp = (-s, c)$. Square $i$ is
$Q_i = \{(x_i, y_i) + \alpha u_i + \beta u_i^\perp : \lvert\alpha\rvert, \lvert\beta\rvert \le 1/2\}$.

The claim needs six hypotheses, each checked separately:

1. **The reading.** The file is ASCII with a final newline: one comment line, the
   header, and 260 rows of three rational tokens.
2. **The side.** The header side is the fraction #895 states.
3. **Unit squares.** $c^2 + s^2 = 1$ holds identically for rational $t$, so every row is
   a unit square. Method B also requires each square’s shoelace area to be exactly 1.
4. **Containment.** $Q_i \subseteq [0, S]^2$ exactly when every corner lies in
   $[0, S]^2$ (method A). Equivalently, the area of $Q_i$ clipped to the box is 1
   (method B).
5. **Disjoint interiors.** For two convex polygons, the interiors are disjoint exactly
   when, on one of the four edge normals
   $\pm u_i, \pm u_i^\perp, \pm u_j, \pm u_j^\perp$, the projections overlap in at most
   a point (method A). Equivalently, $\operatorname{area}(Q_i \cap Q_j) = 0$, since a
   convex set has positive area exactly when its interior is nonempty (method B).
6. **The rotation sense.** Nothing in the file says whether $t$ turns the square
   counterclockwise. Read the other way, with $u = (c, -s)$, 159 pairs overlap, so the
   sense matters. The record reads Daniel’s format counterclockwise.
   The house witness `n-260.yaml` is T-257’s certificate: same side, same centres in
   order, and basis exactly $(c, s)$ for all 260 squares.

`certcheck.py` decides hypotheses 1 to 5. Method A works in `fractions.Fraction`, using
corner projections.
Method B uses Sutherland–Hodgman clipping by cross products, with the
shoelace area, in `Fraction`. The two methods share only the reader.
The reader fails closed on any token, line or count it does not expect.

## Is the Improvement Real?

The improvement is $1.99 \times 10^{-10}$. Against T-257’s precision and slack:

- **Precision.** At this side one binary64 step is $3.55 \times 10^{-15}$, so the gain
  is 56,089 steps. It is not rounding.
- **T-257’s dilation.** T-257’s line 1 calls it a dilation, with delta $10^{-12}$, of a
  candidate packing, which its register entry says is binary64. The dilation accounts
  for $1.663 \times 10^{-11}$ of its side.
  The gain is 12 times that.
- **T-257’s slack.** Scaled about the corner with orientations fixed, T-257’s poses stay
  a packing down to the scale $1 - 1.2016 \times 10^{-12}$, where its pair 28–34
  touches. That is the side $16.6339825021018960\ldots$, $1.9988 \times 10^{-11}$ below
  T-257’s.
- **Comparison.** #895’s side is $1.7928 \times 10^{-10}$ below that.
  So no rescaling of T-257’s packing reaches it; squares had to move.
- **#895’s own slack.** The same scale for #895’s poses is exactly 1, set by row 253 at
  the right wall. The side cannot be lowered without moving squares.

So the gain is real: about nine times all of T-257’s slack, made by moving squares, and
exact.
