## The Claim and What It Needs

Issue #1012 claims, as the lane’s brief states it, $s(109) \le C$ with
$C = 10.949289580944$, the 12-place print of the certificate’s side $S$, rounded up.
The certificate proves $s(109) \le S$: there are 109 closed unit squares, rotated
freely, with pairwise disjoint interiors inside the closed square $[0, S]^2$. The claim
follows when $C \ge S$, and directly when the same squares are accepted in $[0, C]^2$.

The file is a softschema envelope (`contract: packing.squares:Witness/v2`) around a
Witness/v2 record with `representation: corners`, `scalar.kind: rational`,
`origin: lower-left`, `axes: x-right-y-up` and `angle_unit: not-applicable`: per square
four corners, each a pair of exact scalars.
The schema does not say in what order the corners are listed; this review requires the
order to be cyclic, in either sense, and the file meets that.
The hypotheses are five:

1. **The reading.** One YAML document with no anchors, aliases, explicit tags,
   directives or duplicate keys, holding strings, plain decimal integers, mappings and
   sequences where the schema puts them; the envelope above, the witness keys inside the
   schema’s list with every required key present, `square_size: '1'`; the squares listed
   with distinct ids, each with exactly an id and four corners of two scalars; every
   scalar a string `p`, `p/q` or a finite decimal, with no sign but an optional minus,
   no leading zero and $q \ge 1$. Lowest terms are recorded, not required.
2. **The count.** 109 squares.
3. **Unit squares.** The four corners, in the order written, are the vertices of a unit
   square in cyclic order.
4. **Containment.** Each square lies in $[0, S]^2$; the origin is the lower-left corner.
5. **Disjoint interiors.** No two squares’ interiors meet.

`r1012_check.py` decides the first two through the reader, and the next three twice.

- **Method A** (`r1012_sat.py`, Python integers) writes each square’s eight coordinates
  over their least common denominator $D$, so the corners are $P_k / D$ with integer
  vectors $P_k$. With $E = P_1 - P_0$ and $F = P_2 - P_1$ it accepts the square when the
  other two sides are $P_3 - P_2 = -E$ and $P_0 - P_3 = -F$ and
  $E \cdot E = F \cdot F = D^2$, $E \cdot F = 0$: then $e = E/D$ and $f = F/D$ are
  perpendicular unit vectors, the corners are $p_0$, $p_0 + e$, $p_0 + e + f$,
  $p_0 + f$, and $e$ and $f$ are the normals of the square’s edges.
  On each of the four normals $w$ of a pair it projects all eight corners.
  On a normal of square $i$ the projections are integers over $D_i^2 D_j$, and since $w$
  is a unit vector the separation
  $\max(\mathrm{lo}_j - \mathrm{hi}_i, \mathrm{lo}_i - \mathrm{hi}_j)$ is an exact
  distance. The margin, the greatest of the four separations, is kept as an exact
  fraction: positive is apart, zero is contact with disjoint interiors, negative is
  overlap. Two convex polygons have disjoint interiors exactly when a line parallel to an
  edge of one of them weakly separates them, so the three signs are exactly those three
  classes. A wall’s clearance is the signed distance from the square’s extreme corner to
  it: $\min x$, $\min y$, $S - \max x$, $S - \max y$.
- **Method B** (`r1012_clip.py`, `gmpy2.mpq`) is handed the side and the corners and
  nothing else. Four points are a unit square in cyclic order when all four sides have
  squared length 1 and both diagonals squared length 2: four equal sides make a rhombus,
  or a degenerate figure whose diagonal vanishes, and a rhombus with equal diagonals is
  a square. The shoelace sum, $\pm 2$, gives the sense, and a clockwise square is
  reversed before it clips.
  A pair is decided by clipping one square against the four closed half-planes to the
  left of the other’s counterclockwise edges (Sutherland–Hodgman, keeping points on the
  line), which gives the intersection exactly; it is clipped both ways round, and the
  two results must agree in class and area.
  Empty is apart; non-empty with zero area is contact, since the interior of an
  intersection of convex sets is the intersection of their interiors; positive area is
  overlap, by that area.
  Each wall is classed the same way, by clipping the square against the closed
  half-plane outside it, and containment is decided apart: a unit square lies in the box
  exactly when its intersection with the box has area exactly 1.

The two share only the reader (`r1012_reader.py`), which hands them exact rationals.
The verdict is `ACCEPT` only when both methods accept every square, wall and pair, with
equal classes everywhere; `r1012_decide.py` runs both over every square, wall and pair,
and `r1012_check.py` adds the file’s digest and the claim’s arithmetic.
The Euclidean gaps it lists come from a third formulation, centres and half-widths in
Python fractions (`r1012_geom.py`), and decide nothing.

**F2. Controls.** As [Controls](#controls) gives them: 37 of 37 as stated.

**F3. The claim.** $C$ is the ceiling of $S$ at 12 places, above it by

A record can therefore hold the bound as the 12-place decimal the claim prints,
with the certificate’s own corners as its proof.

**F4. The side and the extent.** The leftmost and lowest points of the packing are at
exactly 0, so the squares fill $[0, S]^2$ less a sliver: the height is
$S - 5.82895 \times 10^{-21}$ and the width $S - 3.67504 \times 10^{-15}$. The side is a
20-place decimal and the least one at or above the height: one unit of its last place
less is refused (S1b). The squares fit the box of side $S - 5.83 \times 10^{-21}$ in
place (S1c), and no translation helps, since the height is fixed.

**F5. Margins.** 246 pairs lie below $2 \times 10^{-14}$: 237 between
$2.0 \times 10^{-15}$ and $10^{-14}$, and 9 between $10^{-14}$ and
$1.96 \times 10^{-14}$. 26 of them are pairs of axis-aligned squares, whose margins are
terminating decimals of 16 places (for example $6.5 \times 10^{-15}$, six times), the
differences of 16-place coordinates; 76 pair an axis-aligned square with a turned one,
and 144 two turned squares.
For 195 of the 246 the Euclidean gap equals the margin to 9 places; for the other 51 it
is between 1.0048 and 1.4142 times the margin.
No margin falls between $1.96 \times 10^{-14}$ and $3.71 \times 10^{-4}$ (square-57,
square-64); the next is $3.96 \times 10^{-4}$ (square-56, square-67). This is the
profile of a packing optimized in binary64 and carried into exact arithmetic with its
gaps intact.

**F6. The coordinates.** `r1012_describe.py` finds three regularities.
Every centre less square-0’s centre is a terminating decimal of at most 16 places, so
the centres are 16-place decimals moved by one common translation, which is not a
terminating decimal.
Every turned square’s half-angle tangent $b/(1 + a)$, with $(a, b)$ its first edge, is a
terminating decimal: 28 places for 69 squares, 27 for one, 17 for ten and 16 for five;
the 24 axis-aligned squares have $b/(1 + a)$ equal to $0$ or $\pm 1$. And the 85 turned
squares fall into four groups: 36 within an angle whose sine is at most $10^{-15}$ of an
axis, 4 turned by $0.0199^\circ$ to $0.0227^\circ$ (square-64 to square-67), 41 at two
tilts, $29.503821921^\circ$ (21) and $23.229035995^\circ$ (20), and 4 between
$29.4357^\circ$ and $29.4398^\circ$; every one of the 49 visibly turned squares is
turned clockwise from its nearest axis.
All of this is consistent with exact rationals built from 16-place rows of centre and
angle, with each angle’s half-angle tangent rounded to 28 places and the packing
translated to touch the left and bottom walls; how the author built it is his to say
([Questions](#questions-for-the-author)). Nothing in the bound depends on it.

**F7. The near-axis squares and the wall contacts.** The 36 squares of F6’s first group

and square-63 at the bottom, each turned by a sine of $3.19231 \times 10^{-16}$, and the
axis-aligned squares nearest those walls, square-92 and square-107, stand
$1.59616 \times 10^{-16}$ off them, about half that sine, which is how far such a turn
carries a corner beyond the edge of the same square unturned.
That is consistent with rows in which square-38 and square-92 both sat against the left
wall, and square-63 and square-107 against the bottom, the slight turns carrying two
corners past the wall and the translation to 0 moving everything else off it.

lower the side by about $1.6 \times 10^{-16}$ without an overlap, is a question for the
author; the bound is unaffected.
