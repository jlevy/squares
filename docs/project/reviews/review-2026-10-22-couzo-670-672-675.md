## From Certificate to Bound

$s(n)$ is the least side of a square containing $n$ unit squares with pairwise disjoint
interiors. A certificate is optional comment lines, a header `n S` and $n$ rows `x y t`,
each a rational literal: a rational side, a rational centre and $t = \tan(\theta/2)$.
The format line of #672’s 227 certificate states this reading, and T-225’s certificates
state the same map $(c, s) = ((1 - t^2)/(1 + t^2), 2t/(1 + t^2))$. The bound
$s(n) \le S$ follows from five facts, each decided over $\mathbb{Q}$ or fixed by the
format:

1. **The rotation is on the unit circle.** For $t = p/q$ in lowest terms, the integers
   $a = q^2 - p^2$, $b = 2pq$ and $d = q^2 + p^2$ satisfy $a^2 + b^2 = d^2$, so
   $(a/d, b/d)$ is a unit vector and the row states a unit square centred at $(x, y)$.
   Route Z checks the triple on every row; route C checks unit edges, right angles,
   positive orientation and corner mean on the four corners it builds from the
   half-angle map directly.
   $t = 1$ (77 rows of #670’s) is a quarter turn, an axis-aligned square.
2. **There are exactly $n$ of them.** Both readers refuse a row count other than the
   header’s (mutant R1), and a duplicated square overlaps itself.
3. **Each lies in the closed box $[0, S]^2$.** A convex set lies in the box if and only
   if its corners do, equivalently if its centre clears each wall by its half-extent
   $(|a| + |b|)/(2d)$. Route Z decides the second form as four integer inequalities a
   square, route C the first.
   The least clearances are exactly 0 at 204 and 229, where 16, 44 and 29 square-wall
   contacts occur (#670, #675, #672 at 229), and exactly $10^{-15}$ at 227, at every one
   of its four walls.
4. **Interiors are pairwise disjoint.** Two convex polygons have disjoint interiors if
   and only if a line parallel to an edge of one of them weakly separates them: the
   Minkowski difference of the two squares is a convex polygon whose interior misses the
   origin, and its edges are parallel to the squares’ edges.
   So four axes decide a pair.
   Route Z decides every one of the $n(n-1)/2$ pairs this way, with no distance filter,
   and records each pair’s exact gap, the largest separation over the four axes.
   Route C decides every pair, again with no filter, by the exact area that one square’s
   four closed half-planes clip from the other.
   That area is positive if and only if the interiors meet, since the intersection of
   two convex sets with interiors has an interior exactly when its area is positive.
   Every area is exactly 0. A clip that leaves a point or segment would be a contact;
   there is none, and no route Z gap is 0. The least gaps are
   $5.4747204 \times 10^{-11}$ (#670, squares 123 and 132, numbered from 0),
   $1.2009999788951 \times 10^{-12}$ (#675, 67 and 72),
   $1.2025046796919 \times 10^{-12}$ (#672 at 227, 68 and 76) and
   $1.1989519660130 \times 10^{-12}$ (#672 at 229, 203 and 204), each the receipt’s
   `minimum_best_pair_gap` as an equal rational.
5. **The side is the claim’s**, by the table above.

No route here rounds, dilates or tolerates.
Route Z uses no `fractions` at all: it clears every denominator and compares integers.
A pair’s gap is $(\max_k L_k - R)/(2 d_i d_j D_x D_y)$, with $D_x, D_y$ the products of
the two centres’ denominators, $L_k$ the four scaled projections and
$R = D_x D_y (d_i d_j + |C| + |S|)$, where $C$ and $S$ are the integer cosine and sine
of the angle between the squares.
Its least clearance equals route C’s, and its touching and overlap counts equal route
C’s, at all four certificates.
Route C also counts the pairs whose centres lie within $\sqrt 2$: 498, 459, 563 and 487.
These are the `near_pairs` that the source’s manifests record for its own checker at the
same four certificates, the one place where a number of the source’s checker and a
number of this review’s can be compared.
