## The Claims and What They Need

Each issue claims $s(127) \le C$, $C$ the 12-place print of the certificate’s side $S$
rounded up, or, for #1017, $S$ itself.
A certificate proves $s(127) \le S$ when its 127 closed unit squares, rotated freely,
have pairwise disjoint interiors inside the closed square $[0, S]^2$; the claim follows
when $C \ge S$, and is decided directly by placing the same squares in $[0, C]^2$.

Each report has two files.

- **Form J**, `n127_exact.json`: one JSON object with exactly the keys `n`, `side`,
  `square_size`, `format` and `squares`, each square `[center_x, center_y, cos, sin]`,
  and the format sentence “(cos, sin) is the unit direction of one edge”.
  The square is $C + \alpha u + \beta v$ with
  $\lvert\alpha\rvert, \lvert\beta\rvert \le 1/2$, $u = (\cos, \sin)$ and
  $v = (-\sin, \cos)$; it is the same square whichever of its four edge directions is
  given, so form J has no rotation-sense ambiguity.
  It states no origin.
- **Form C**, `n127_witness.yaml`: a softschema envelope
  (`contract: packing.squares:Witness/v2`) around a witness with
  `representation: corners`, `scalar.kind: rational`, `origin: lower-left` and
  `axes: x-right-y-up`, four rational corners per square, ids `square-1` to
  `square-127`. T-305’s certificate is a form C file with integer ids and source and
  certificate blocks.

The hypotheses are five:

1. **The reading.** Form J is ASCII JSON with no duplicate key, no non-integer number,
   no constant, exactly its five keys, `square_size` the string `1`, the format sentence
   as quoted, and exactly 127 rows of four scalars.
   Form C is one YAML document with no anchor, alias, tag, directive, flow collection,
   duplicate key, tab or carriage return, holding only strings, plain decimal integers,
   mappings and sequences; the envelope above, the witness keys inside the schema’s list
   with the required ones present, the squares in order with exactly an id and four
   corners each; every corner scalar is read a second time, line by line, with the same
   result. Every scalar is `p` or `p/q` with an optional minus, no plus, no leading zero,
   $q \ge 1$ and no negative zero; lowest terms are recorded, not required.
2. **The count and the twin.** 127 squares in each file, one side in both, and square
   $k$ of form C the square of row $k$ of form J: its corners are $C \pm u/2 \pm v/2$,
   listed as a cyclic rotation in either sense.
3. **Unit squares.** In form J, $\cos^2 + \sin^2 = 1$ exactly on the file’s numbers.
   In form C, the four corners in the order listed have four sides of squared length 1
   and two diagonals of squared length 2.
4. **Containment.** Each square lies in $[0, S]^2$, origin at the lower-left corner.
5. **Disjoint interiors.** No two squares’ interiors meet.

`rb127_check.py` decides the first two through the readers and the next three twice.

- **Method A** (`rb127_axes.py`, Python integers) is handed poses and never a corner.
  Each centre is written as $(X, Y)/E$ and each direction as $(a, b)/r$ in integers; the
  pose is a unit square when $a^2 + b^2 = r^2$. On each of the four edge normals $w$ of
  a pair, the two squares’ half-widths add to the same
  $H = 1/2 + (\lvert\cos D\rvert + \lvert\sin D\rvert)/2$, $D$ the angle between them,
  and the separation is $\lvert d \cdot w\rvert - H$, $d$ the centre difference.
  All four share the denominator $2 E_i E_j r_i r_j$, so the margin, their maximum, is
  decided on one integer numerator and kept exact: positive is apart, zero is contact
  with disjoint interiors, negative is overlap.
  Two convex polygons have disjoint interiors exactly when a line parallel to an edge of
  one of them weakly separates them, so the three signs are exactly those three classes.
  A wall’s clearance is the signed distance from the square’s extreme point to it, from
  the half-extent $(\lvert a\rvert + \lvert b\rvert)/(2r)$.
- **Method B** (`rb127_clip.py`, `gmpy2.mpq`) is handed corners and nothing else.
  A pair is decided by clipping one square against the four closed half-planes of the
  other (Sutherland–Hodgman, keeping points on the line), both ways round, the two
  results required to agree in class and area: empty is apart, non-empty with zero area
  is contact, positive area is overlap.
  Each wall is classed by clipping the square against the closed half-plane outside it,
  and containment is the area inside the box, exactly 1.

The two share the readers (`rb127_read.py`) and nothing else on the form each reads
natively. `rb127_twin.py` writes each form in the other’s terms, so the check also runs
crossed, method A on the poses of form C’s corners and method B on the corners of form
J’s rows, which decides each file on its own by both methods.
The verdict is `ACCEPT` only when both files read, they are one packing, both decisions
accept every square, wall and pair with equal classes everywhere, and the margins and
clearances from the two files are identical.

**Option D’s quantities.** The owner’s decision (`think-b0w5`, 17 November 2026): a
re-certification of a standing packing is a new result only if its side is below the
predecessor’s side once the predecessor is shrunk until its squares first touch.
`rb127_contract.py` shrinks a certificate exactly: every centre is scaled by one factor
$\lambda$, each square keeping its size and angle, and a pair keeps disjoint interiors
exactly while $\lambda \ge f$, its contact factor $H / \max_w \lvert d \cdot w\rvert$,
so the first contact is $\lambda^* = \max f$ over all 8,001 pairs.
The shrunk side is the side of the smallest square box holding the shrunk packing, the
larger of its width and height, and the contraction margin is $S$ less that side.
It is the sum of the box’s slack at scale 1, $S$ less the extent, and the part the
contraction closes. Each step’s test, successor below the shrunk predecessor, is a
comparison of two exact fractions.
The controls K1c and K1p decide the shrunk packing itself.
