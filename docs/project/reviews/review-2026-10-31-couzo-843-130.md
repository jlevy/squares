## From Certificate to Bound

The certificate’s comment line states its format: n unit squares, each a centre (x, y)
and t = tan(θ/2). The edge directions u = ((1 − t²)/(1 + t²), 2t/(1 + t²)) and v =
(−u_y, u_x) are then exact rational unit vectors.
The bound s(130) ≤ S follows from four facts, each decided over ℚ:

1. **Each rotation is on the unit circle.** (1 − t²)² + (2t)² = (1 + t²)², and the
   reader also checks it row by row.
2. **There are exactly 130 squares.** The reader refuses any other row count, any field
   that is not an integer or p/q, and any row without three fields.
3. **Each closed square lies in [0, S]².** Route A checks each square’s support, ½(|u_x|
   \+ |v_x|) about its centre, against each wall.
   Route B checks the four corners.
4. **Interiors are pairwise disjoint.** Two convex polygons have disjoint interiors
   exactly when an edge normal of one of them separates their projections, touching
   allowed: if the interiors are disjoint, the origin is not interior to the Minkowski
   difference, whose edge normals are the squares’ own.
   Route A decides every pair this way.
   A binary64 hint picks the axis it tries first, and every decision is exact.
   Route B decides each pair whose centres are closer than √2 by the exact area of the
   clipped intersection, which must be 0. Every other pair is disjoint, because each
   closed square lies in the closed disc of radius √2/2 about its centre and its
   interior in the open disc.

## Identity and Lineage

`identity.py` maps one packing onto the other about the box centres by each of the eight
symmetries of the square.
It pairs the squares by the assignment of least summed squared centre distance and keeps
the cheapest symmetry.
A move is a centre displacement, and a turn an orientation change modulo 90°. *One to
one* means each square’s nearest centre in the other packing is a different square.
The bottleneck is the least largest move over all assignments.
