## The Claim and What It Needs

Each claim is $s(n) \le C$ with $C$ the 12-place print of the certificate’s side $S$.
The certificate proves $s(n) \le S$: there are $n$ closed unit squares, rotated freely,
with pairwise disjoint interiors inside the closed square $[0, S]^2$. The claim follows
when $C \ge S$, and directly when the same squares are accepted in $[0, C]^2$.

The format, from the files and the README. `nNNN_exact.json` is an object with the keys
`n`, `side`, `square_size` (`"1"`), `format` and `squares`, where `format` reads “each
square: [center_x, center_y, cos, sin] as exact rationals; (cos, sin) is the unit
direction of one edge” and `squares` lists $n$ quadruples of strings.
`nNNN_witness.yaml` is a mapping with the one key `witness`, holding `id`, `n`, `side`,
`square_size`, `representation: center-basis`, `scalar: {kind: rational}`, `coordinates`
(`lower-left`, `x-right-y-up`, `not-applicable`) and `squares`, each
`{id, center: [x, y], basis: [cos, sin]}`.

A square $(x, y, c, s)$ has centre $(x, y)$, edge directions $u = (c, s)$ and
$v = (-s, c)$, and corners $(x, y) \pm u/2 \pm v/2$. Unlike the tangent half-angle
format of #941 and #942, this does not make every square a unit square by construction:
it is one exactly when $c^2 + s^2 = 1$, which both methods check.

The hypotheses are seven:

1. **The reading.** The layouts above, with every scalar a canonical integer or $p/q$
   with $q > 1$.
2. **The count.** $n$ squares, and in the YAML the ids 1 to $n$ in order.
3. **Unit squares.** $c^2 + s^2 = 1$ for every square.
4. **Containment.** Each square lies in $[0, S]^2$.
5. **Disjoint interiors.** No two squares’ interiors meet.
6. **The rotation sense.** $(c, s)$ is the direction of an edge, measured
   counterclockwise from the $x$ axis with $x$ to the right and $y$ up; control G6 shows
   that reading it the other way, $(c, -s)$, changes the verdict.
7. **The two files.** The JSON and the YAML describe the same packing.

`l105_check.py` decides the first two and the last through the reader, and the other
four twice.

- **Method A** (`l105_sat.py`, `fractions.Fraction`). A square is accepted when
  $c^2 + s^2 = 1$; its edge normals $u$ and $v$ are then exact rational unit vectors.
  Its half-extent along a unit axis $a$ is $h(a) = (|a \cdot u| + |a \cdot v|)/2$, so
  its wall clearances are $x - h$, $y - h$, $S - x - h$ and $S - y - h$ with
  $h = (|c| + |s|)/2$. A pair’s margin is the largest of $|a \cdot d| - h_i(a) - h_j(a)$
  over the four edge normals, $d$ the centres’ difference: positive is clear, zero is
  contact, negative is overlap.
  For convex polygons these three classes are exactly “closed sets apart”, “touching
  with disjoint interiors” and “interiors meet”, since the Minkowski difference of two
  squares is a polygon whose edges are parallel to theirs.
- **Method B** (`l105_clip.py`, `gmpy2.mpq`). Each square is built as four corners and
  accepted when its four edges have squared length 1, its four turns one strict sign,
  and its shoelace area $\pm 1$. A pair is the first square clipped by the other’s four
  closed half-planes: empty is clear, area 0 is contact, positive area is overlap.
  A wall is the square clipped by the wall’s closed outer half-plane, read the same
  three ways, with the least corner distance to the wall as its exact clearance; and
  containment is clipping to the box keeping area 1.

The two share only the reader, which hands them integers.
The reader (`l105_reader.py`) reads the JSON with the standard library, floats,
constants and duplicate keys refused, and composes the YAML with PyYAML, refusing
anchors, aliases, explicit tags and directives, duplicate keys, and any scalar outside
the layout (a coordinate must resolve as a string, `n` and each `id` as a plain
integer). It fails closed on any layout it does not expect, and compares the two files
scalar by scalar, as exact rationals and as text.
The verdict is `ACCEPT` only when, for each file, both methods accept every square, wall
and pair, their classes agree everywhere, their clearances are equal exactly, $n$ is the
folder’s, and the two files hold the same packing.
