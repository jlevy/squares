## The Claim and What It Needs

Issue #992 claims $s(977) \le L = 17 + 21/\sqrt2 = 31.849242404917498\ldots$, below the
grid’s 32 by $15 - 21/\sqrt2 \approx 0.1507576$. A packing proves it if it has 977
closed unit squares with pairwise disjoint interiors inside the closed square
$[0, L]^2$. No certificate file states such a packing exactly: the CSV is in binary64 at
side 31.85, and the certificate states the packing as a recipe.
So the claim needs the recipe read exactly, and this review reads it as follows.

- $R = 21/\sqrt2$, $L = 17 + R$, $C = L/2$.
- **Diamond.** For $i, j \in \{0, \ldots, 20\}$, $u = i - 10$, $v = j - 10$, a unit
  square turned 45° centred at $(C + (u - v)/\sqrt2,\ C + (u + v)/\sqrt2)$, named
  $D(i, j)$.
- **Bottom-left staircase.** Upright unit cells $[c, c+1] \times [r, r+1]$ for
  $0 \le r < 15$, $0 \le c < w_r$, $w = (15, 15, 14, 13, \ldots, 2)$, 134 cells, named
  $BL(c, r)$.
- **The other corners.** The images of the bottom-left cells under the quarter turns
  about $(C, C)$, counterclockwise: $(x, y) \mapsto (L - y, x)$ for $BR$,
  $(L - x, L - y)$ for $TR$, $(y, L - x)$ for $TL$, keeping the bottom-left index.

Every coordinate lies in $\frac14\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt2]$: the centre is
$17/2 + (21/4)\sqrt2$ and $1/\sqrt2 = \sqrt2/2$.

## The Construction Decided

`l174_construct.py` writes the recipe above as an exact file, each number $a + b\sqrt2$
a pair of canonical fractions, and `l174_decide.py` decides it twice.

- **Method A** (`l174_method_a.py`). Every square is upright or at 45°, so the edge
  normals of any pair lie among $(1,0), (0,1), (1,1), (1,-1)$. Each square projects on
  each to an interval whose centre and half-width are in $\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt2]$ after one
  common scaling. A pair is apart if some direction separates it strictly, touching if
  one separates it weakly and none strictly, and overlapping otherwise.
  Signs of $a + b\sqrt2$ come from comparing $a^2$ with $2b^2$. Walls are the four
  clearances.
- **Method B** (`l174_method_b.py`). Each square’s corners in $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt2)$ over
  `gmpy2.mpq`; the closed intersection of a pair by Sutherland–Hodgman clipping, both
  ways round with equal areas required; apart if empty, touching if non-empty of area
  exactly 0, overlapping if of positive area.
  Each wall by clipping against its closed outer half-plane; containment by area.
  Unit squares by four sides of squared length 1, two diagonals of squared length 2 and
  area 1. Signs by bracketing $\sqrt2$ between consecutive Pell convergents until both
  rational evaluations agree, with $a = b = 0$ the only zero.

Both process all 476,776 pairs.

## The Proof Argument

Read against the exact construction, `n977_certificate.md` at the pin proves the claim.

- **Side** (lines 21–25). $21/\sqrt2 < 15$, so $L < 32$. Correct.
- **Diamond** (lines 27–33). The union of the turned $21 \times 21$ array is the closed
  diamond $\lvert x - C\rvert + \lvert y - C\rvert \le R$, since a square of side 21 has
  half-diagonal $21/\sqrt2$. It lies in the box because $R \le L/2$ is $R \le 17$.
  Correct.
- **Bottom-left staircase** (lines 35–44). The widths sum to 134; every cell has
  $c + r \le 15$, so its interior lies in $x + y < 17$, while the whole diamond lies in
  $x + y \ge 2C - R = 17$. Correct, and tight: 14 cells reach the line.
- **Other corners** (line 46). A quarter turn about $(C, C)$ maps the diamond onto
  itself, so each image staircase misses its interior; each staircase lies within 15 of
  its corner and $L/2 > 15$, so the four are apart.
  Correct.
- **Count and conclusion** (lines 48–55). $441 + 4 \cdot 134 = 977$. Correct.

Two steps are left implicit and both are immediate (F4): the 441 turned squares are
pairwise interior-disjoint because they are the cells of one grid turned as a whole, and
“by rotation” needs the diamond’s invariance under the quarter turn, which the
certificate uses without saying.
Neither is a gap in substance.

### Is the Construction Known?

Yes, as its own pin now says.

- **Göbel’s family.** Friedman’s survey (retained as
  `packing/resources/web/friedman-ds7-survey-2009-html.md`, line 102) states Göbel’s
  packing of $2a^2 + 2a + b^2$ squares in side $a + 1 + b/\sqrt2$ when
  $a - 1 < b/\sqrt2 < a + 1$; the record’s module holds the same window.
  The side $17 + 21/\sqrt2$ is the member $(a, b) = (16, 21)$, 985 squares, but
  $b/\sqrt2 = 14.849 < a - 1 = 15$, so it is outside the window by exactly
  $15 - 21/\sqrt2$, which is $32 - L$. The module is right to leave it out: taken
  literally (G985) it overlaps in exactly four pairs, $BL(15,0)$ with $BR(0,15)$ and its
  three quarter-turn images, the two bottom-row triangles of 16 meeting across a side
  shorter than 32, each overlap of area exactly $15 - 21/\sqrt2$ (`decide-G985.json`).
- **#992 is that member trimmed.** Square for square, #992’s 977 squares are G985’s 985
  less the two end cells $X(15,0)$ and $X(0,15)$ of each corner triangle
  (`l174_identity.py`, `identity.json`). Equivalently it is Göbel’s $(14, 21)$, 861
  squares at $15 + 21/\sqrt2$, centred in a ring of width 1, of whose 120 squares it
  keeps 116. Removing only one cell of each colliding pair would give 981, and so does
  #992 with the four middle squares added (A981, a packing by the frozen checker).
- **The side is classical at 982.** Friedman’s L-border (the same line: “$n + 2s(n) + 1$
  squares can be packed in a square of side $s(n) + 1$”) on Göbel’s $(15, 21)$, 921
  squares at $16 + 21/\sqrt2$ and inside the window ($14 < 14.849 < 16$), packs
  $921 + 61 = 982$ at exactly $17 + 21/\sqrt2$. The frozen checker decides that packing
  too (G982: 982 squares, 3,532 touching pairs, 122 wall contacts, none overlapping, the
  methods agreeing). So $s(982) \le 17 + 21/\sqrt2$ follows from Göbel (1979) and
  Friedman’s survey, and #992’s bound is a deletion from it, which is the derivation the
  pin gives.
- **Tables.** Friedman’s tables stop at $n = 100$ and the record’s catalogue capture at
  324, so neither lists 977.
- **Why the module misses it.** `devtools.goebel_construction` takes the three families
  the Kingbird catalogue states, with deletion, and no L-border.

### Can the Construction Checker Decide It?

No (F12). `devtools.construction_checker` reads two kinds of recipe, “square tiling and
L-border” and “deletion”, each on a parent certificate the record has replayed on both
routes, and refuses every other kind.

- #992’s recipe, a turned diamond with four staircases, is neither kind.
- The checker holds sides as `Fraction`s and writes poses with a rational rotation
  parameter; $17 + 21/\sqrt2$ and the 45° turn are irrational.
- Its classical route is closed by name: written as one L-border on Göbel’s $(15, 21)$,
  the recipe’s parent would be a classical construction, which `decide` refuses
  (think-zkxy).

So D2 does not make #992 a row, and the 14 October rule would keep it out if it did.
An exact row at an irrational side would need a certificate format or checker over
$\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt2)$, such as the two methods of this review; the record has none
maintained.

*Records lane L177, 15 November 2026, after lane L175 merged this review:* the record
does maintain exact routes over a number field: Witness/v2 states a scalar field by its
minimal polynomial and isolating interval, and `sqpack.witness.exact_verify` and
`devtools.check_algebraic_witness_independent` both decide such witnesses; both decided
this recipe over $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt2)$ through `devtools.diamond_staircases`
