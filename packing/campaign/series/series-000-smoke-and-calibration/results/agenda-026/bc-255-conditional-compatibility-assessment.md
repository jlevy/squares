## The Counting Reduction

Suppose eleven interior-disjoint unit squares fit in a square of side `L<q`, with each
orientation, modulo a quarter turn, in the near-axis or near-45-degree band of radius
`pi/720`. Scale the entire packing by `lambda=q/L>1`. The enlarged squares have side
lambda and fit in `[0,q]^2`. Their concentric **closed unit cores** are contained in
their interiors, so those eleven cores are pairwise disjoint, including their
boundaries. This is stronger than the original squares having disjoint interiors.

P10 is the original ten-point Stromquist set.
Each point belongs to at most one closed core, so at least one core avoids all ten
points. The accepted
[H-106](../../../../hypotheses/H-106-continuous-near-axis-ten-point-cover.md) rules out
a near-axis orientation for that core.
The accepted H-123 **localization lemma** puts a near-45-degree P10 avoider in one of
the coordinate-midline reflections of the canonical center region `R=[1,q/2] x [0,1]`.
Apply the same reflection to all eleven cores.
These four transformations, generated by `(x,y) -> (q-x,y)` and `(x,y) -> (x,q-y)`,
preserve the container, P10, both angle bands and disjointness.
Quarter turns need not preserve P10 and are not used here; H-109’s local anchor
reflection is not a global container symmetry either.

In that region, the accepted
[H-108](../../../../hypotheses/H-108-near45-canonical-a3-forcing.md) and
[H-109](../../../../hypotheses/H-109-near45-canonical-a1-a2-forcing.md) force the
distinguished core to contain `A1=(1,q-3)`, `A2=(q/2,q-3)` and `A3=(3/2,13/10)`. It
therefore contains their convex hull T, meaning all convex combinations of those three
points. Each other core is disjoint from T. Consequently, the following would suffice:

> Every contained closed unit square in either allowed angle band that is disjoint from
> T contains at least one of the nine unchanged points B through J.

There are ten other cores and only nine points.
Because T is contained in the diamond proved below, exp-122’s diamond-avoiding
counterexample also refutes this triangle-conditioned version.
Neither small obstacle is a live replacement for full distinguished-square
compatibility. Since the cores are pairwise disjoint, that cover would contradict the
pigeonhole principle.
Both angle bands are required: the distinguished core need not be the only
near-45-degree core.

No lower bound on a uniform clearance radius enters this argument.
Scaling supplies strict containment for each hypothetical packing separately; its slack
may tend to zero as L approaches q.

## A Larger Fixed Obstacle from Two Anchors

The orientation band gives a larger obstacle than T at little additional proof cost.
Set

$$
W=q/2-1=939/1000,\qquad b=q-3=439/500,\qquad \kappa=49/50.
$$

Define the closed diamond

$$
D=\{(1+X,b+Y):0\le X\le W,\quad
|Y|\le\kappa\min(X,W-X)\}.
$$

Its vertices are A1, A2 and `(1+W/2,b +/- kappa W/2)`. Every near-45-degree square
containing A1 and A2 contains D. This is an analytic geometric lemma, independent of the
H-110 candidate and of any conditional-cover computation.

To prove it, write `C=cos(theta)`, `S=sin(theta)`, with C and S positive.
In the square’s orthonormal frame, the two anchors have relative coordinates `(0,0)` and
`(CW,-SW)`. Any square containing both contains the whole frame-aligned rectangle
`[0,CW] x [-SW,0]`. For a point `(X,Y)` in the original frame, membership in that
rectangle is equivalent to

$$
-\cot\theta\,X\le Y\le\tan\theta\,X,\qquad
-\tan\theta\,(W-X)\le Y\le\cot\theta\,(W-X).
$$

Throughout the angle band, both tan(theta) and cot(theta) exceed kappa.
Indeed, set `d=abs(theta-pi/4)`. The elementary bound `pi<4` gives `d<1/180`, while
`sin(d)<=d` and `cos(d)>=1-d*d/2` imply

$$
|\tan(\theta-\pi/4)|<
\frac{1/180}{1-1/64800}<\frac1{100}.
$$

The tangent addition formula therefore bounds both tan(theta) and cot(theta) below by
`99/101 > 49/50`. The inequalities defining D imply all four rectangle inequalities,
including their boundaries.
Thus D is inside the distinguished core.

T is contained in D: A1 and A2 are vertices, and A3 has `X=1/2`, `Y=211/500`, with

$$
\frac{211}{500}<\frac{49}{50}\frac{439}{1000}.
$$

D also extends below the horizontal A1–A2 edge, so it strictly enlarges T. It is a
certified common obstacle, not a claim to be the largest common intersection of all
admissible distinguished squares.

## Proof Obligations and Outcomes

Using D gives a weaker sufficient covering statement than using T:

> Every contained closed unit square in either allowed angle band that is disjoint from
> D contains at least one of B through J.

This was the statement registered as H-122 and refuted by exp-122. Had it held,
localization, H-106 and H-109 would have completed the proof of H-036. H-108 is not
needed for this particular reduction, although its accepted A3 result remains valid.
All nine marks retain their source formulas from
[H-110](../../../../hypotheses/H-110-fixed-near-axis-p12-escape.md); none is moved.

| Obligation | What would establish it | What would not |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Localization (accepted) | Complete coverage of every contained near-45 center outside the symmetry-related canonical regions by P10 | A sampled angle mesh or a proof restricted to canonical centers |
| Conditional nine-point cover (refuted) | Complete coverage in both angle bands, with the fixed diamond as an excluded obstacle | One escape intersecting D, or only the exact 0-degree and 45-degree slices |
| Refutation of that sufficient cover (verified) | One independently verified contained unit square in an actual allowed angle band, disjoint from D and avoiding all nine marks | Failure of a sufficient certificate, a tolerance-based separation, or a partial packet |

The four canonical regions have union `[1,q-1] x ([0,1] union [q-1,q])`. For a near-45
angle with `h=(cos(theta)+sin(theta))/2`, their complement within the contained-center
domain is `[h,q-h]^2` intersected with the union `x<1`, `x>q-1`, or `1<y<q-1`. Its
strict boundaries matter.
H-123 covers a closed enlargement, which is sufficient and stronger than necessary.

The exp-122 counterexample does not refute H-036. A square may avoid D while overlapping
every distinguished square allowed by the stronger geometric premises.
The experiment did not test pairwise compatibility with those full squares.
A complete conditional cover using that compatibility requires a separate claim and
allocation.

Closed disjointness must be literal: touching D is not avoidance.
A separating-axis test between compact convex polygons can express it by a **strict**
gap on an edge normal of either polygon.
For continuous-angle certificates, the obligation includes every angle, center and
boundary stratum; finite samples can only provide witnesses or screening evidence.

### A Bounded Falsifier Before a Continuous Proof

The existing exact event-cell machinery in
[restricted_orientation.py](../../../../../cases/stromquist/restricted_orientation.py)
can support a small fixed-frame screen.
This is a proposed instrument, not a launched experiment.
Factor a frame-parameterized kernel while preserving its existing exact
0-degree/45-degree wrapper.
Validate `C*C+S*S=1` and use the containment half-extent `h=(abs(C)+abs(S))/2`; omitting
the absolute values is wrong for a negative near-axis angle.

Within each event cell avoiding all nine marks, each strict separation gap from D is
affine in the center.
The four unoriented separating axes are the square’s two axes and the diamond’s two edge
normals; both signs give eight affine tests.
Maximize a gap over the closure vertices.
If a vertex v has gap `a>0`, mix it with the cell’s existing strict-stratum witness w,
whose gap is b, using

$$
\varepsilon=\frac{a}{2(a+|b|)},\qquad
z=(1-\varepsilon)v+\varepsilon w.
$$

Then `0<epsilon<=1/2`, the gap at z is at least `a/2`, and every open stratum inequality
holds because w has positive weight.
The weight lies in the same exact number field as the geometry; it need not be rational
in the near-45 chart.
If no signed gap is positive at any closure vertex, that cell supplies no disjoint
square at the fixed frame.
None of this closes an interval of angles.

A possible prospective screen uses half-angle offsets `t=+/-1/500` about each of 0 and
`pi/4`. The actual offsets satisfy `abs(2 arctan(t))<1/250<pi/720` using `pi>3`. An
independent witness reader needs only exact corners, containment, actual-angle
membership, strict avoidance of all nine points and strict separation from D. It need
not trust the producer’s event-cell inventory to validate a returned counterexample.
The exact 0-degree and 45-degree conditional cases already follow from the accepted
H-104 P12 result and `A1,A2,A3 in D`; they need no repeated scientific invocation.
