## Evidence That Changes the Choice

The [BC278 audit](bc-278-boundary-band-independent-review.md) accepts a seven-disk
construction within the unchanged admitted boundary domain, including an open family of
selected poses. A uniform disk capacity of six is false there, even with the exact
selected squares. The weaker octagon model also admits seven centers.
These routes are retired at their declared scopes.

The same disk centers cannot lift to full squares: two boundary centers force
axis-aligned squares that overlap.
That is a fixed-center obstruction, not an exclusion of the continuous full-square
child. It points to information lost when residual squares are replaced by unconditional
incircles.

At center $C$, containment restricts the admissible orientations through the nearest
wall distance $r(C)$. The generic design proposes the exact common core

$$
K(r)=\bigcap_{h(\theta)\le r}Q_\theta
=\operatorname{conv}\!\left(B_{1/2}\cup[-a,a]^2\right),
\qquad a=\frac{1}{4\min(r,1/\sqrt2)}.
$$

The coordinator and model author derived this generic identity during the assessment; an
independent proof is required before target admission.
The convex hull already represents the proposed maximal common core, so separate union,
polygon-refinement and intersection targets would buy avoidable intermediate models.

At a wall, the core is the full axis-aligned unit square.
Beyond distance $1/\sqrt2$ from every wall, it reduces to the incircle.
The model therefore adds actual containment information throughout the original parent
without fixing centers to the known witness.
A verified capacity bound of six would exclude the full-square child.
An exact seven-core witness would instead rule out exclusions obtained only by replacing
these maximal cores with smaller bodies justified by the same individual center and wall
information. Joint residual-angle restrictions would remain open.

Neither result would establish H118’s strict comparison with a matched coupled LP. That
still requires its own frozen comparator and exact surviving relaxation point.
