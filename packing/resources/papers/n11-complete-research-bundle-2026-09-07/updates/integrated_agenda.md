# Integrated research agenda: compatibility, complete cases, and exact-value closure

This is the current entry point for both reviews. The original technical report
remains intact in `original_review/`. The follow-up `enumeration_addendum.pdf`
develops the finite-case proposal and reviews the Grok notes. No new global
lower bound for s(11), completed two-angle optimum, or full enumeration is claimed.

## Central objective

Prove a substantial full-domain exclusion, with 3.84 as an illustrative first
milestone, while building reusable restricted theorems that could ultimately
establish s(11) = U. Treat uniqueness of the optimum as a separate question.
The frozen packet's bracket remains 3.810025723614703... <= s(11) <=
3.877083590022814.... The upper endpoint is an exact construction, not an
optimality theorem.

## Results available from these reviews

**Exact fixed-support certificate.** Seven necessary positive-area depth rows
prove that the a.e. weighting optimum on the sixty Trump-D4 placements is at
most eleven. The known packing average attains eleven. The former 56/5 candidate
has depth 7/5 on an explicit box. The standard-library checker, same-author
second assembly, mutations, original outputs, and fresh replay outputs are all
included. This result settles only that support; it changes no packing bound.

**Physical normal-stationarity theorem.** In the full corner-projection branch
formulation, with variable side and open local angle charts, center/container
dilation strictly increases every active physical constraint. Normal KKT
multipliers suffice, their total is bounded by L+1, and a certificate can use at
most 34 positive scalar rows. Artificial partition constraints must not be
silently added to this qualification. This is a mathematical deduction, not an
implemented global stationary solver or an independent external review.

**New enumeration bridges.** The addendum proves quantitative angle transfer,
a finite-cover composition theorem, a robust-Farkas box inequality, and constant
side on connected components of the physical normal stationary set. It supplies
all 1,024 ordered angle-multiplicity descriptors and exact elementary controls.
No continuous n=11 case is solved by the descriptor catalogue.

## The unified proof architecture

Use a finite, adaptive case-cover graph. Every node denotes an explicit domain
of configurations or original stationary representatives. Each split must cover
its parent, including seams and equalities. Every terminal node carries an exact
exclusion, a lower-side inequality, or a fully applicable local theorem. Any open
terminal node keeps the global result open.

The descriptors include actual angle multiplicities, angle-bin counts, angular
cluster radii, center tiles, bottom-wall anchors, and typed separating choices.
They are selected only where useful. Do not form their full Cartesian product.
Retain actual orientation lifts: folded-angle equality is not physical equality.

A measure contributes a capacity row to the integral selection model, whether or
not it covers every square by one or has mass near eleven. Different measures
keep independent budgets. Robust conflicts, small-subsystem no-goods, and
certified residual-ten or residual-nine bounds impose compatibility. Parametric
LP duals and selected active-basis elimination close continuous leaves. The
2-pose kernel remains a parallel source of stronger global or conditional bounds.

## Four workstreams and their first discriminators

### A. Resource-and-anchor pilot: primary substantial-bound attempt

Choose one side, initially 3.84 unless a concrete cost argument suggests another.
Start with capacity-one center tiles and a small angular-bin partition. Translate
a representative jointly to a bottom-wall anchor; retain its full pose box.
Use one or two anchors, separate measure budgets, and low-order exact conflicts.

The first useful output is a complete broad anchor/angle case exclusion or a
sharply delimited residual family. A large number of sampled failed completions
is not such an output. If near-axis or near-diagonal localization is already
proved on a branch, try mixed-size cores there while keeping the frame squares
full size. Measure the geometric loss rather than assuming it is harmless.

### B. Restricted orientation optimum: primary structural theorem attempt

First target six axis-aligned squares plus five sharing one free angle, with
arbitrary centers and contacts. Do not fix Trump's contact graph. Reuse the
fixed-angle 23-variable LP and construct uniform parameter-box dual certificates.
Then assess the complete at-most-two-angle family, retaining both absolute class
angles, all multiplicities, and collision boundaries.

Within typed cases, propagate exact angle equalities only from proved
positive-length edge-edge or edge-wall contacts. A structural theorem bounding
the resulting number of angle components could reduce all minimizing
representatives to an at-most-k family; no such global bound is proved yet.

A proof s_{<=2}(11)=U would force any better packing to have at least three
actual orientations. It would not prove unrestricted optimality. Every restricted
family excluded with positive side margin should be thickened using the proved
angle-transfer inequality; that is how a theorem on exact equalities earns a
region of global coverage.

### C. Interaction kernel: high-upside parallel pilot

Keep a small explicit two-pose feature family and an adversarial compatible-pair
separator. A sampled semidefinite result is only a candidate. The first decisive
output is a continuum-certified bound below eleven, or an exact obstruction to
the chosen feature family. No current result promises that a low level succeeds.
This lane need not wait for the angle-family solver.

### D. Selective resource representation: one earned candidate at a time

Retain the original equality-support intersection, expanded finite dual support,
and boundary-null curved measures as options. Choose among them using an actual
candidate or obstruction, rather than building all new verifiers. Inner polygons
and disk relaxations remain useful locally; the new twelve-disk construction in
side 3.6 rules out a standalone improvement via the unconditioned incircle problem.

## Enabling work, not separate oversized programs

Complete the actual fixed-angle Farkas export and the geometry enclosure interface
needed by the uniform dual test. A small generic arithmetic checker is included;
it is not yet connected to packing geometry. Put every new exact exclusion into
the common case record with its domain and inheritance conditions.

Use the normal-stationarity and fixed-angle-vertex deductions for difficult
residual leaves. The new finite-stationary-values deduction permits value-only
closure of continuous families, but supplies no practical component count or
n=11 runtime estimate. Do not launch an all-contact or all-stationary-component
atlas from finiteness alone.

Keep the existing scalar probe as a bounded opportunistic lane, not the dependency
that all interaction work must await. Preserve the packet's historical experiment
records and hypotheses; these reviews are not edits to their original verdicts.

## Coverage ledger and research discipline

Each result records: exact domain; all-feasible versus stationary-only versus
restricted scope; side threshold; witness/core convention; whole-configuration
symmetry map; complete proof artifact; open remainder; and actual measured cost.
Reuse small infeasible subsets as no-goods, with their proof of uniformity intact.

A geometry-aware coverage graph remains to be implemented. The included JSON
catalogue enumerates descriptors only. The 78-to-65 elementary angle-bin reduction
is a control, not a forecast that the full case count will collapse.

The most important unanswered question is whether one or two boundary anchors,
modest angular information, and resource constraints reduce the continuous
competitors enough to close them. Both principal pilots test that question from
different directions. Success means complete mathematical cases closed and useful
bounds on the remaining domains, not merely more cells visited.
