# C1092 MMMP: the bottom square must own its ordinary right pair

All terminating decimals are exact rationals. We retain the complete original C1092 entrance, its twelve actual open squares of common side 1<a≤1.001, and the closed MMMP signs. Every stated owner is strict, and all exclusions and support contacts may be equality.

**Theorem.** In every original MMMP configuration,

    F2=(1.6,1) is excluded from Q9,
    F6=(2.4,1), G5=(2.26,1) are strictly in Q9.

The contradiction for F2 uses the complete continuous three-central-square ending already proved for MMMM. Only that ending's actual point premises are transported; no MMMM center signs or prior ownership selectors are imposed on the MMMP configuration.

## 1. A hypothetical F2 owner forces the opposite ordinary neighbor

Suppose F2 is strict in Q9. The direct MMMP wall-sign chord already puts G5 and the whole segment [2.22,2.39]×{1} strictly in this same square.

Every member of the original Q9 combined list containing F2 also contains G2=(1.74,1). This small component-list fact can equally be checked from the exact source list

    M9={4100,5124,36868,37892,4116,36884,32832,36928,
        294976,299072,36932,37956,299076,32848,36944,36948}.

Thus G2 is strict in Q9. The southwest Q4 must own a member of {G0,G2,G3}. The point G3 is already strict in actual Q7 by the MMMP core theorem, while G2 is now strict in Q9. Consequently

    G0=(1,1.6) is strict in Q4.                         (1)

The southwest F-covering row requires F0,F2 or F4. Since F2 is in Q9, suppose F0 were absent from Q4; then F4 would be strict in Q4. Apply `MIXED-SPAN-FOUR-POINTS.md` after reflection through x=2. Its exact actual data would be

| Standard datum | Reflected MMMP point | Actual source |
|---|---|---|
| Owned F4 | F4=(2,1.74) | Q4 under the present supposition |
| Owned G8 | G0=(1,1.6) | Q4 by (1) |
| Excluded G6 | G3=(1.74,2) | Q7 |
| Excluded F8 | F0=(1,1.74) | Supposed absent from Q4 |
| Excluded G5 | G2=(1.74,1) | Q9 |
| Excluded F6 | F2=(1.6,1) | Q9 |

That continuous point theorem is a contradiction. Hence

    F0=(1,1.74), G0=(1,1.6) are strict in Q4.          (2)

The left wall Q8's original G-covering row contains a member of {G0,G1,G3}. Equations (1) and the core ownership of G3 exclude its first and third options. Therefore G1=(1,2.4) is strict in Q8.

## 3. The complete rotated three-central-square contradiction

It then proves a contradiction for those actual square owners, with common side a>1, by their full support formulas and two genuine short chords. In particular it uses no remaining wall-center sign from the MMMM entrance.

Transport that entire point theorem by the quarter turn

## 4. The ordinary right pair follows from one original component row

The point G5 was already strict in Q9 by the direct wall-sign chord. In the displayed original M9 list, every component containing G5 and omitting F2 contains F6. Explicitly these components are

    32832,36928,294976,299072,32848,36944.

Every one contains F6=(2.4,1). Hence F6 is strict in the actual Q9, proving the stated ordinary right pair.

This theorem excludes a whole original ownership branch by a fixed continuous geometric theorem and a few literal single-component covering facts. It does not enumerate global assignments, and it does not assume that an auxiliary point absent from Q9 must have a different owner. Other original MMMP alternatives remain subject to their further actual-square constraints.
