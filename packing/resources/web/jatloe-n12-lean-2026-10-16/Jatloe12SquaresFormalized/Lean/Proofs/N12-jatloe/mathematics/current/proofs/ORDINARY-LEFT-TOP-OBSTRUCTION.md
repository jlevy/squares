# A foreign left marker makes the ordinary northwest square incompatible with a shallow top square

All terminating decimals are exact rationals. Owned points are strict, excluded points may touch a face, and all local squares have side greater than one. The first theorem has no wall or center hypothesis. The second retains the actual common side and actual top wall.

## 1. The ordinary square has a narrow positive angle and strict upper points

**Theorem 1.** Let N be an open square of side (a>1), strictly owning

\[
 M=(.65,.35),\qquad H=(0,.26),
\]

and excluding the seven points

\[
 P=(-.086,0),\ D=(.5,0),\ D_1=(.74,0),\ K=(0,1),
 \quad X=(.27,-.04),\ T_0=(.44,1),\ T_1=(.74,1).
                                                               \tag{1}
\]

Then N has a strictly positive canonical frame

\[
 u=(c,s),\quad v=(-s,c),\quad c\ge s>0,\quad c^2+s^2=1,
 \qquad \frac23<\frac{s}{c}<\frac8{11}.              \tag{2}
\]

Writing U,V for the lower faces and A=U+a,B=V+a, its weak faces satisfy

\[
 U\ge-.086c,\qquad V\ge-.27s-.04c,
 \qquad A\le.44c+s,\qquad B\le c.                   \tag{3}
\]

Both (Z=(.28,1)) and (Z'=(.4,1)) belong strictly to N.

### 1.1. Every nonpositive canonical representative is impossible

Use (u=(c,-s),v=(s,c)), (c\ge s\ge0), and let U,V be lower faces.
At (D_1) the lower-u band follows from H, and the upper-v band follows from M: the latter increment is (.35c-.09s>0). Thus (D_1) is upper u or lower v.

If (D_1) were lower v, then (T_1), whose two lower bands follow from H and M, could not exit upper v: the opposite width would be (v(T_1)-v(D_1)=c\le1). Hence (T_1) would be upper u. At K, its upper-u and lower-v bands follow from H. An upper-v exit would give the width (v(K)-v(D_1)=c-.74s\le1), so K would be lower u. But these u faces give (a\le u(T_1)-u(K)=.74c<1). This is impossible. Therefore

\[
 A\le.74c.                                          \tag{4}
\]

At D the lower-u band follows from H and the upper-v band from M. If D were lower v, then (T_0) has its upper-u and lower-v bands strictly from M, with increments (.21c+.65s>0) and (.65c-.21s>0). Its upper-v exit would give (a\le c-.06s\le1), so it must exit lower u. Equation (4) would then give

\[
 a\le u(D_1)-u(T_0)=.30c+s\le1.30/\sqrt2<1.
\]

Consequently D is upper u. Since M is strict,

\[
 A\le.5c,\qquad .65c-.35s<.5c,
 \qquad s/c>3/7.                                    \tag{5}
\]

Both upper bands of P follow from M. Its lower-u exit, paired with (5), would give (a\le.586c<1). Thus P is lower v, (V\ge-.086s).

The upper-u and lower-v bands of K again follow from H. An upper-v exit would give (a\le c+.086s). The latter expression decreases with (s/c\ge3/7), and its value at (3/7) is (7.258/\sqrt{58}<1). Hence K is lower u, (U\ge-s). Together with (5),

\[
 1<a\le.5c+s.
\]

The expression on the right increases through (0\le s/c\le2/3), and at (2/3) equals (3.5/\sqrt{13}<1). Therefore (s/c>2/3).

At (T_0), the same two strict bands leave only lower u or upper v. A lower-u exit, paired with (5), gives (a\le.06c+s\le1.06/\sqrt2<1). Thus

\[
 B\le c+.44s.                                       \tag{6}
\]

Now X has its lower-u and upper-v bands from H. Its upper-u band follows from (U\ge-s), because

\[
 u(X)+s=.27c+1.04s\le1.31/\sqrt2<1<a.
\]

Its lower-v band follows from (6), since (1.04c+.17s) decreases for (s/c\ge2/3), and

\[
 v(T_0)-v(X)=1.04c+.17s
 <3.46/\sqrt{13}<1<a.
\]

Thus X is strict, contrary to (1). This excludes all nonpositive canonical representatives, including the axial and diagonal endpoints. Each displayed bound of the form (bc+s\le(b+1)/\sqrt2), or (.27c+1.04s\le1.31/\sqrt2), follows because the expression increases on the complete canonical interval.

### 1.2. The positive faces and angle endpoints

Use (u=(c,s),v=(-s,c)), (c\ge s\ge0). At D its lower-u band follows from H, its upper-u band from M, and its upper-v band from H. Thus (V\ge-.5s). At K its two lower bands follow from H and its upper-u band from M, the latter increment being (.65(c-s)\ge0). Therefore (B\le c).

The two lower bands of (T_1) follow from H and M. Its upper-v exit would give (a\le c-.24s\le1). Therefore (A\le.74c+s). Both upper bands of P follow from H. A lower-v exit would give (a\le c-.086s\le1), so

\[
 U\ge-.086c,
 \qquad 1<a\le.826c+s.                              \tag{7}
\]

On (0\le s/c\le1/6), the last expression is at most (5.956/\sqrt{37}<1). Thus (s/c>1/6). Its half-angle (z=s/(1+c)) consequently satisfies

\[
 z>1/(\sqrt{37}+6)>1/13>.06.
\]

The exact identity (c+.06s-1=2z(.06-z)/(1+z^2)) shows that (c+.06s<1).

Both lower bands of (T_0) follow from H and M. Its upper-v exit, together with (V\ge-.5s), would give (a\le c+.06s<1). Hence (A\le.44c+s). Combining with (7) gives

\[
 1<a\le.526c+s.
\]

For (s/c\le2/3), this increasing expression is at most (3.578/\sqrt{13}<1). Thus (s/c>2/3).

Both upper bands of X follow from M and H. If X were lower u, the cap at (T_0) would give (a\le.17c+1.04s\le1.21/\sqrt2<1). Consequently X is lower v:

\[
 V\ge-.27s-.04c,\qquad 1<a\le1.04c+.27s.           \tag{8}
\]

For (s/c\ge8/11), the last expression decreases, and its endpoint value is (13.6/\sqrt{185}<1), since (13.6^2=184.96<185). Thus (s/c<8/11). We have proved (2)–(3), with strict endpoints.

### 1.3. The two upper points

For either (q=.28) or (q=.4), put (Z_q=(q,1)). Its two lower bands follow from H: the increments are (qc+.74s>0) and (.74c-qs>0). From (3),

\[
 u(Z_q)-U\le(q+.086)c+s
 \le.486c+s<13.346/\sqrt{185}<1<a.
\]

Here the expression increases up to the angle endpoint (s/c=8/11). Also

\[
 v(Z_q)-V\le1.04c+(.27-q)s
 \le1.04c-.01s<3.10/\sqrt{13}<1<a,
\]

using its decreasing dependence on the positive angle and (s/c>2/3). Both upper bands are strict. This proves the two point conclusions and Theorem 1. ∎

## 2. The actual ordinary/top pair excludes every shallow nonpositive top angle

**Theorem 2.** Let N and W be disjoint open squares of the same side (a>1). Suppose N has the positive frame and angle interval (2), the lower faces

\[
 U\ge-.086c,\qquad V\ge-.27s-.04c,
\]

and strictly owns (M=(.65,.35)) and (Z=(.28,1)). Suppose W strictly owns

\[
 A_0=(.44,1),\quad O=(1,1),\quad P_0=(1,1.086),
\]

excludes (T=(1.26,1)), and has its closure in (y\le2). Then W cannot have a nonpositive canonical angle (-\beta) with (0\le\tan\beta\le2/3).

**Proof.** Rename N's components (l,h). Represent the hypothesized W by its quarter-turned quadrant frame

\[
 u_W=(C,S),\quad v_W=(-S,C),\quad
 C,S\ge0,\quad C^2+S^2=1,\quad0\le C/S\le2/3.
\]

Thus (S\ge4/5,C\le3/5), including (C=0). Set

\[
 \alpha=lC+hS>0,\qquad \gamma=lS-hC>0.              \tag{9}
\]

The last sign follows from (h/l<8/11) and (S/C\ge3/2) when (C>0); it is immediate at (C=0).

Any separating normal n directed from N toward W satisfies (n_x>0), by the strict pair Z,(A_0), and (n_y>(21/65)n_x), by M,(A_0). The square separating-axis theorem therefore leaves only N's actual normal ((l,h)) or W's actual normal ((C,S)). At (C=0) the latter is an unnecessary additional direction; ruling it out is harmless.

### 2.1. The right point T is below W's lower transverse face

Write L,K for W's lower faces, J=L+a and B=K+a. The strict O gives the lower-u and upper-v bands at T, so T is upper u or lower v.

If (J\le1.26C+S), the W-normal cannot separate: (L\le u_W(T)-a<u_W(M)), since (|T-M|^2=.7946<1), while M is strict in N.

For the N-normal, its frame coordinates relative to W are ((\alpha,-\gamma)). Strict O gives (B>C-S), so the hypothesized upper-u cap yields

\[
 \operatorname{lower}_W(l,h)
 <1.26l+h-\alpha-.26\gamma S.
\]

Separation from the strict M would require

\[
 lF(C)+hG(C)>0,
 \quad F(C)=.35-C+.26C^2,
 \quad G(C)=.65-S+.26CS.                             \tag{10}
\]

The strict P0 under the same cap requires (.26C>.086S), hence (C>43/\sqrt{18749}>.31). On ([.31,.6]), (G(C)\le-.0252<0): its derivative is ((C+.26-.52C^2)/S>0), and its value at .6 is -.0252. The derivative of (F+(21/65)G) is at most

\[
 -1+.52(.6)+(21/65)(43/40)=-4429/13000<0.
\]

Here (G'\le(.6+.26)/(.8)=43/40). At (C=.31), using (S>.95),

\[
 F+(21/65)G<.064986-(21/65)(.22343)
             =-23397/3250000<0.
\]

Because (h/l>2/3>21/65) and (G<0), (10) is impossible. Thus the upper-u exit of T supplies no actual separator, and necessarily

\[
 K\ge C-1.26S.                                      \tag{11}
\]

The genuine top wall is

\[
 S(L+a)+C(K+a)\le2.                                 \tag{12}
\]

### 2.2. N's own normal cannot separate

Combining (11)–(12) gives the strict cap

\[
 \operatorname{lower}_W(l,h)<W_*=
 l\left[\frac{C-1}{S}+1.26-C\right]+h(2-S).
                                                               \tag{13}
\]

The strict inequality uses (a>1) with positive coefficient (alpha+l/S). Since

\[
 C+\frac{S}{1+C}\ge1,
\]

we have (W_*\le.26l+1.2h). The displayed elementary inequality follows from (S/(1+C)\ge1-C), obtained by squaring its nonnegative sides. But N's own upper face satisfies

\[
 U+a>1-.086l,
\]

and throughout (h/l<8/11),

\[
 .346l+1.2h<13.406/\sqrt{185}<1.                    \tag{14}
\]

The expression increases on the canonical interval in question. Equations (13)–(14) put W's lower support strictly below N's upper support, so this normal cannot separate.

### 2.3. W's own normal cannot separate

Its N-frame components are ((\alpha,\gamma)), both positive. N's lower faces therefore give

\[
 \operatorname{upper}_N(C,S)
 \ge a(\alpha+\gamma)-.086\alpha l
                  -\gamma(.27h+.04l).
\]

On the other hand, (11)–(12) give

\[
 L\le\frac{2-C(a+C-1.26S)}S-a.
\]

Subtracting, multiplying by (S>0), and using (a>1) with its positive coefficient shows that the support difference is strictly larger than

\[
 E=C+S-1+qS^2-pCS,                                 \tag{15}
\]

where the exact expanded coefficients are

\[
 p=1.26-l+h+.086l^2-.27h^2-.04lh,
 \qquad q=l+h-1-.356lh-.04l^2.
\]

Both coefficients admit simple uniform bounds. Since (2/3<h/l<8/11),

\[
 l-h>3/\sqrt{185}>.22,\quad
 h^2>4/13,\quad l^2<9/13,\quad lh>6/13.
\]

Consequently

\[
 1-p>.22-.26+\frac{-.774+1.08+.24}{13}
          =.002>0.                                 \tag{16}
\]

Also (l+h-1\ge.8lh), by (l+h-1=2lh/(l+h+1)) and (l+h<1.5). Hence

\[
 q\ge.444lh-.04l^2
   >\frac{2.664-.36}{13}=\frac{288}{1625}>\frac16.
                                                               \tag{17}
\]

The same elementary identity gives (C+S-1\ge.8CS). Inserting (16)–(17) into (15),

\[
 E>\frac{S^2}{6}-.2CS
   \ge S^2\left(\frac16-\frac2{15}\right)
   =\frac{S^2}{30}>0.
\]

Thus W's lower support is strictly below N's upper support in its own normal too. Both actual normal sources are impossible. This proves Theorem 2, including the axial top square, every equal-angle endpoint allowed by the statement, and every weak exterior or support tangency. ∎
