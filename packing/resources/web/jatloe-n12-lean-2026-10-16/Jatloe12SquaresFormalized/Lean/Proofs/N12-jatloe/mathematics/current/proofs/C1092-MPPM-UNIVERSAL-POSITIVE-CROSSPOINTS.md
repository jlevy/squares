## 2. The preceding-origin exclusion is now unconditional for positive centrals

**Theorem 1.** Every strictly positive ordinary central excludes its preceding point D, for every pattern of the other central signs and forward memberships.

**Proof.** Normalize the positive central to C=Q4. If its forward G3 is strict, the conclusion is already Theorem 1 of C1092-MPPM-FORWARD-OWNED-POSITIVE-D.md. That theorem explicitly permits an ordinary/inward overlap and does not presume ownership of the origin in Q8.

Suppose instead that G3 is omitted. If D=(1,2) were strict in Q4, actual disjointness would exclude it from Q8. Corollary 2 of C1092-MPPM-MISSING-BOUNDARY-SHALLOW-ANGLE.md, applied to this same actual Q8, then gives a strictly negative canonical angle with absolute slope less than 1/4. This is a consequence of the actual preceding positive owner, actual paired corner, actual Q8 wall and the missing origin; no particular owner of that missing origin was assumed in its proof.

On the other hand, Section 5 of C1092-MPPM-POSITIVE-ORIGIN-OWNED-WALL-ANGLE.md applies literally to the stipulated positive missing-forward Q4 and this negative Q8. Its strict inputs are Q4's F0 and D, Q8's original marker and F1, and the original outer marker (.914,3) in the actual paired corner Q3. Its real wall is the original left container wall. It proves that the absolute slope of the same actual Q8 is strictly greater than 5/12.

Since 1/4<5/12, these conclusions are incompatible. Thus D is not strict in Q4. Simultaneous quarter turns give every row of the table. This proves Theorem 1. ∎

Theorem 1 excludes D from the indicated actual central; it does not assert that D belongs to its boundary square. This distinction is retained below.

## 4. Every positive crosspoint is strict

**Theorem 2.** Every strictly positive ordinary central strictly owns its own X in the first table, regardless of the other central signs and forward memberships.

**Proof.** A positive retaining central already owns X by Theorem 2 of C1092-MPPM-POSITIVE-CROSSPOINT-SEEDS.md. Suppose a positive central C omits X. It must therefore be missing-forward.

Inspect its actual successor E. If E is nonpositive and missing-forward, Corollary 2 of C1092-MPPM-STEEP-POSITIVE-MISSING-NEGATIVE-CROSSPOINT.md puts X strictly in C, a contradiction. This is the unconditional actual-three-square transfer and has no successor-X premise.

If E is nonpositive and retaining, Corollary 2 of C1092-MPPM-STEEP-POSITIVE-NEGATIVE-CROSSPOINT-TRANSFER.md applies. Its only additional origin premise is the genuine exclusion of D from C, now supplied by Theorem 1. It again puts X strictly in C, a contradiction. No owner is assigned to D in this application.

Hence E must be positive. If its X were strict, Corollary 2 of C1092-MPPM-STEEP-POSITIVE-THREE-SQUARE-REDUCTION.md would put X strictly in C. Thus E is another positive central omitting X. It is also missing-forward by the initial seed observation.

Iterating this implication around the four actual owners forces all four to be positive, missing-forward and to omit X. But the all-missing-forward ordinary cycle is already excluded: Section 8 of C1092-MPPM-MISSING-POSITIVE-BOUNDARIES.md sums the four strict actual wall-center differences and obtains 0>0. All needed actual origins in that all-missing alternative follow from the four ordinary halfpoints and their genuine paired-corner transfers.

This contradiction proves that the initial positive X omission was impossible. Theorem 2 follows. ∎

Every positive central therefore has the simultaneous actual strict anchors M,P,G,X and the faces (1), with the stronger missing-forward alternatives where applicable. The argument neither changes any designated forward membership nor asserts universal boundary-origin ownership. In particular any remaining proof may now use a positive successor's X as a genuine strict foreign point, while keeping its actual separating-normal directions and canonical angle unrestricted.
