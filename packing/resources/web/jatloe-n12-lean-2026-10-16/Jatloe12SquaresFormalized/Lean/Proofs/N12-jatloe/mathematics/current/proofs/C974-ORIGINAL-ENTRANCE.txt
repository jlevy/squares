WHAT THIS ENTRANCE PROVES

The original case C974 is reduced to an explicit cover of 2720 labelled
contexts: two missing-corner alternatives, sixteen closed wall-center
sign alternatives, and eighty-five auxiliary ownership alternatives.
The companion exact selector table eliminates that entire cover by the
continuous-domain theorem and its dynamic-hull/parallel-strip extensions.
This is a finite rational mathematical proof for the original C974
family. It does not replace the earlier global marker classification.

All coordinates are exact. Open squares have common side 1<a<=1001/1000,
closures in [0,4]^2, and pairwise disjoint open interiors. Ownership means
strict interior containment. The original marker classification supplies
the groups below; every marker absent from a group is excluded from that
owner's open square. In particular marker 11 is excluded from all twelve
owners in this case.

The proof uses three already established geometric entrances: the
six-pair avoidance theorem for the ten-point sets F and G, the singleton
corner and paired-corner compulsory-point lemmas, and the local
four/five/six-point obstructions. Their exact uses are specified below.
The only additional published local fact is Bentz's Lemma 11, with its
missing-corner premise stated in Section 4. No thirteen-square counting
argument is imported.

3. THE LOCAL GEOMETRIC JUSTIFICATION OF THE SUBSET TESTS

For completeness, the four-point obstruction in (b) has a short proof.
If P,Q are excluded, their excluding edges cannot be the same, because
PQ crosses the strict interior segment AB, and cannot be opposite,
because |P-Q|<1<a. They are therefore adjacent. Put their common corner
O at the origin with the square in the positive quadrant, P outside the
left edge and Q outside the bottom edge. The crossing gives P_y>0 and
Q_x>0, so OP.OQ<=0. Thus O lies in the closed disk with diameter PQ.
Whichever of A,B lies on O's side of PQ lies in triangle OPQ, hence in
that convex disk. Its angle subtended by PQ is at least a right angle,
contrary to the positive dot product in (b).

The short-cluster principle behind (c),(d) is equally simple: excluded
points at pairwise distance at most 1 cannot use opposite excluding
edges of a side-a square, so they use at most two adjacent edges.
Segments of excluded points crossing a segment of required strict
points cannot share an excluding edge.

For (c), translate by (1,1.74). The excluded points are
A=(0,0), B=(.65,.61), C=(1,0), and the required points are
D=(.65,-.09), Q=(1,.52), or Q=(0,.52). The excluded squared distances
are 1,.7946,.4946. The segments AC and BC cross DQ in the first version.
Hence A,B share one excluding normal u and C uses a perpendicular normal
v. Comparing their exclusions with strict D,Q gives u_y>0,u_x<0 and
v_y<0,v_x>0. Then u.v<0, a contradiction. Reflection across the vertical
midline gives the version Q=(0,.52). All eight container images follow.

For (d), label the required points P=(.914,2), Q=(1,1.74), R=(1.74,2),
and the excluded points A=(1,1.6), B=(1,2.4), C=(1.65,1.65). Their
excluded squared distances are .64,.425,.985. Both AB and BC cross PR;
the latter intersection is (101/75,2). Thus A,C share normal u and B
uses a perpendicular normal v. Comparing A,B with the strict point
(1,2) gives u_y<0,v_y>0. The two possible signed directions are

    u=(c,-s), v=(s,c), c,s>0; or
    u=(-c,-s), v=(-s,c), c,s>0.

In the first case, comparisons with R give 37c<20s and 37s<20c,
which contradict 37>20. In the second case comparisons with Q and P
give 65c<9s and 43s<200c, contradicting 65*43=2795>1800=9*200.
The remaining zero-coordinate case already contradicts v_y>0.
This proves all uses of (c),(d) in the finite subset construction.


4. THE TWO EXHAUSTIVE MISSING-CORNER ROOTS

Put E=(1,1). The two roots are:

    root 0: E is excluded from owner 0;
    root 1: E is strict in owner 0 and excluded from owner 1.

Every actual packing satisfies one root. If owner 0 does not contain E
strictly, root 0 applies, including equality on its boundary. If it
contains E strictly, disjointness excludes E from owner 1 and root 1
applies. No assumption that one of these squares owns E is made.

The extra local theorem used for a missing corner is the following
published Lemma 11 of Wolfram Bentz (2010). A square of side greater than
one, in [0,4]^2, containing (1,.914) and excluding

    (.914,1), (.914,2), (1.65,1.65), (1,1)

contains both (1.82,1) and (1.96,.76). The hypotheses and both conclusions
were checked directly against the primary paper, printed page 7.

Thus root 0 adds these two strict points to owner 0. Root 1 applies the
coordinate-exchanged version to owner 1, adding (1,1.82),(.76,1.96), and
adds the declared strict point E to owner 0. Every required exclusion
other than E follows directly from the original marker groups above.
The other corner owners require no extra missing-corner alternative.

5. THE SIXTEEN WALL-CENTER SIGNS

For a sign index s in {0,...,15}, let epsilon_j=+1 if bit j of s is 1,
and epsilon_j=-1 otherwise. For j=0,1,2,3 respectively, constrain the
following owner's center coordinate:

    j=0: owner 8,  y coordinate;
    j=1: owner 9,  x coordinate;
    j=2: owner 10, y coordinate;
    j=3: owner 11, x coordinate.

The constraint is epsilon_j*(coordinate-2)<=0. Thus the + sign means
coordinate<=2 and the - sign means coordinate>=2. All inequalities are
closed. These sixteen alternatives cover every possible choice of real
centers, including all centers on the midlines. A physical boundary
configuration may occur in more than one alternative; that is harmless.

The labelled universe is exactly

    {0,1} x {0,...,15} x {0,...,84},

of size 2*16*85=2720. The selector table uses zero-based root, sign,
vector and owner indices consistently. Its only nontrivial surviving
wall sign after the first stage is 8, namely (-,-,-,+).


6. PRECISE INITIAL DOMAINS FOR EACH SELECTED CONTEXT

A context specifies a root, sign, resolution N, and nonempty sorted
subset I of the eighty-five vector indices. Freshly form, for each owner,

    M_i={r_i: r is a vector selected by I},
    H_i=Hull(B_i union all auxiliaries common to every m in M_i),

then add the root's strict points from Section 4 to H. These root-enhanced
hulls are common to every selected vector. The context is initialized
freshly from these data; a later single-vector context does not copy the
rounded domains of a preceding multi-vector context.

For each owner i, angle cell k and label m in M_i, first form the local
own hull Hull(B_i union owned auxiliaries of m). Apply the exact initial
rectangle, four wall clips and absent-point removals of Sections 2--4
of 03_exact_continuous_domain_theorem.txt, with alpha=1, beta=1001/1000,
G=100000000. Absent original markers and absent auxiliaries are processed
in lexicographic point order; the four point-square inequalities are in
the order u_k,v_k,-u_k,-v_k. The four wall clips have normals
(-1,0),(1,0),(0,-1),(0,1), in that order.

Next subtract each foreign root-enhanced hull plus the inner square.
The foreign hull vertex lists are sorted lexicographically for this
initialization; each Minkowski hull has the canonical vertex order.
There is no intermediate grid rounding during these initial removals.
For each surviving polygon also impose its own root-enhanced hull bounds

    n.z <= min_{h in H_i} n.h + (1001/1000)b_k/2,
    n=u_k,v_k,-u_k,-v_k in that order.

If this owner is the root's missing-corner owner, additionally subtract
the open inner-square Minkowski neighborhood of E. Convert final pieces
to the outward integer grid and merge states with equal cell and label.

Finally clip the four wall-marker owner domains by the prescribed signed
midline inequalities of Section 5, round each clipped polygon outward
in grid units, and merge. This completes the initial domains D_i.
The true state for every selected actual vector and angle survives.

Subsequent selectors use the common theorem and
05_dynamic_hulls_refinement_parallel_strips.txt. In particular, dynamic
refresh uses owner order and rounds after EACH foreign hull. Its order
is intentionally distinguished from this fresh initialization.


7. HOW PARTIAL SUPPORT AND THE TERMINAL COVER COMPOSE

A terminal contradiction for a context eliminates every vector selected
by that context, at its specified root and sign. At two contexts the
full owner domains remain nonempty. For either such context let C_i be
the set of labels still represented in D_i. It eliminates precisely
those selected vector indices j for which

    some i satisfies (vector j)_i not in C_i.                (C974-S)

This criterion is sound: the true full vector must retain its component
in every owner domain. It does not require simultaneous consistency of
all surviving components, so surviving extra vectors are simply left
for further contexts.

The first thirty-two contexts cover each root and wall sign once using
all eighty-five vectors. For each root, fifteen wall signs close
completely; sign 8 leaves forty-seven vectors after (C974-S). The table
then initializes and eliminates those forty-seven vectors separately
for each root, using ninety-four additional contexts. Three of these
single-vector contexts use a two-child center split; every child is
closed. Thus the total is 32+94=126 contexts.

The detailed terminal equalities and the exact union of eliminated
labels are in 03_fresh_arithmetic_and_coverage.txt. Every action selector,
parallel-strip point, angle refinement and tree is recorded in
02_exact_context_selectors.txt. Those files constitute the finite
calculation part; the common continuous-domain and geometric lemmas
prove that its exclusions apply to arbitrary real square poses.
