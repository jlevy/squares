# C1092 MPPM: no inward owner in OIOI can contain the physical center

Every terminating decimal is an exact rational. All squares and all
strictly owned points below are the original actual owners, transported
together when a coordinate change is stated. Excluded faces, actual walls
and separating supports allow equality. The common side satisfies a>1.
No numerical angle sample, upper side bound, or assignment of a missing
auxiliary to a new owner is used.

**Theorem 1.** In the original no-omission MPPM entrance, an inward owner
in an OIOI pattern cannot strictly own O=(2,2).

Normalize the hypothesized owner to C=Q4, with opposite inward N=Q6 and
ordinary neighbors E=Q5,W=Q7. The proof first uses the actual predecessor
to sharpen C's angle and upper face. The opposite inward owner supplies a
genuine right-wall advance. These facts force C's outer halfpoint, then
the actual bottom origin, and finally contradict both C--E square-normal
sources. The forced halfpoint does not require either outer auxiliary to
be owned by C.

## 8. Joining the actual cases

The supposition that the inward C=Q4 owns O supplied exactly the initial
positive bands A,H in (2). Its opposite N then necessarily omitted O
and owned its outer G9. That genuine ownership supplied the top origin
and the new right advance; it never supplied the omitted F9. Both ordinary
neighbors and their two side origins had their independent actual source
maps in Section 1. The predecessor comparisons forced (12), and the
successor comparisons then forced the missing bottom origin before using
it. Both possible ownership statuses of G0 were retained throughout.

The final contradiction therefore proves Theorem 1 for the entire
center-owned inward OIOI branch. Simultaneous quarter-turns give it for
every inward sector. In particular every remaining inward owner must
omit the physical center; the completed missing-center classification
then requires its outer G point to be owned and its outer F point to be
omitted. The case in which both opposite inward owners omit the center
is not inferred to be contradictory by this theorem and retains its
separate actual geometry.
