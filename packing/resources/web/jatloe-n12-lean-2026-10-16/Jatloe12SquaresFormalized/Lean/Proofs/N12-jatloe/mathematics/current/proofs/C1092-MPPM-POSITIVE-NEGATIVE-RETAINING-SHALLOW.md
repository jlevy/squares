# C1092 MPPM: a positive predecessor makes a retaining negative recipient shallow

Every terminating decimal is an exact rational. Strict ownership means
membership in the interior of the named actual open square. Exclusions,
separating supports and real-wall contacts may be weak. The local proof
uses only a common side a>1. Its literal application retains the original
no-omission OOOO entrance and 1<a<=1.001.

## 1. The two actual squares and their simultaneous faces

In the southwest chart translated by (-2,-2), let C have a positive
canonical frame and its actual successor E have a negative canonical

    C: u=(c,s), v=(-s,c), c=cos p, s=sin p,
    E: e=(d,-t), f=(t,d), d=cos q, t=sin q,
    0<p<=pi/4, 0<q<=pi/4.

Write C's lower faces as U,V and its upper faces as A=U+a,K=V+a.
Write E's physical lower e,f faces as L,B and its upper faces as
J=L+a,D=B+a. The own-sector frame of E is f,-e. Thus its native
upper first face is A_E=D, and its native upper second face is
H_E=-L. Suppose C's simultaneous actual faces satisfy

    A>=a-c-s, K<=s.                                (1)

The actual strict points are

    C: M=(-.35,-.35),
    E: M_E=(.35,-.35), B0=(.26,-1),
       B1=(.4,-1), I=(0,-.26).

In E's own southwest chart, I is its strict forward point
T=(-.26,0), and B0,B1 are its strict ordinary points
P=(-1,-.26),G=(-1,-.4). Suppose E has the genuine native faces

    U_E>=-d, V_E>=-d-t,
    -.26t<H_E<=.35(d-t),                           (2)

and that its native next crosspoint Y=(0,-.5), or physical

boundary square strictly owns the entire bar

    [-.56,0] x {-1}.                               (3)

All points of this bar belong to one actual square disjoint from E.
It is not an additional wall for E.

**Theorem 1.** Under these hypotheses,

    0<q<atan(1/4), H_E<.086,
    d>.97, t<.25.                                 (4)

No preceding boundary origin is required to belong to its wall square:
the native floor U_E>=-d in (2) is the explicit premise that is needed.

## 2. The genuine foreign crosspoint caps E's first face

At Y=(0,-.5), the lower-u band is strict by P, with increment

The lower-v and upper-u faces in (6) are not independently chosen

## 3. Three actual central normals, and two uniform exclusions

Direct a separator from C toward E. The strict marker displacement
M_E-M=(.7,0) gives n_x>0. The pair M,B0 gives

    .61n_x-.65n_y>0.

    u=(c,s), -v=(s,-c), e=(d,-t).                 (7)

Some of these may fail the cone as well, which only strengthens the
argument. At p=q=pi/4 two listed directions coincide; their support

Put

    k=cd-st=cos(p+q)>=0,
    ell=ct+sd=sin(p+q)>0.

### 3.1. C's first normal

The actual decomposition u=k e+ell f and (6) give

    lower_E(u)<=.26kt+ell(.5t-a).

By (1), the excess of upper_C(u) over that lower support is at least

    a(1+ell)-c-s-.26kt-.5ell t.

Its coefficient of a is positive. At a=1 the exact remaining scalar is

    F_u=1-Pc-Qs,
    P=1-t+.26dt+.5t^2,
    Q=1-d+.5dt-.26t^2.                            (8)

Both coefficients are positive: P>=1-t>0, while
Q>=.24dt>0 because t<=d. Also t<.71 and
z=t/(1+d)<=sqrt(2)-1<.415, so

    P<1-.38t,
    Q=t(z+.5d-.26t)<.92t.

It follows that

    P^2+Q^2<(1-.38t)^2+(.92t)^2
             =1-.76t+.9908t^2
             <1-.056532t<1.                      (9)

The right side 1-.38t is positive. Cauchy's inequality and
c^2+s^2=1 therefore give F_u>0. This excludes u, with strictness
also supplied by a>1 in its positive coefficient.

### 3.2. C's transverse normal

    lower_E(-v)<.26ell t-k(a-d).

Thus -v is impossible as well. Both actual squares have been

## 4. The last source gives a strict negative-recipient envelope

Only e from (7) can remain. Its actual C support is

    upper_C(e)=kA-ell V.

Separation requires kA-ell V<=L=-H_E. Since V=K-a<=s-a,
(1) gives

    H_E<=ell V-kA
        <=ell(s-a)-k(a-c-s)
         <d f(p)-t g(p),                          (11)

where the last inequality uses a>1 in the strictly positive
coefficient k+ell. The exact expansion is

    f(p)=(1-c)(1-s), g(p)=c-s+s^2.

On the entire interval 0<=p<=pi/4,

    f'(p)=(c-s)(c+s-1)>=0,
    g'(p)=-(c+s)+2cs<0.

    0<=f(p)<=1.5-sqrt(2)<.086,
    1/2<=g(p)<=1.

    H_E<.086d-.5t.                                (12)

    .24t<.086d,
    tan q<43/120<2/5.                             (13)

In particular d>.92 and t<.38. The proof of (12) used no
forward-membership assumption on C.

## 5. The same actual boundary promotes the angle bound

Work in E's own southwest chart for this section. Its lowest closure
vertex is

    Z_x>=ad-1-dt>d-1-dt>-.46>-.56.               (14)

Since V_E=H_E-a and A_E>=a-d, it follows that

    H_E>a-1/d+(t/d)A_E
        >=a(1+t/d)-1/d-t

## 7. The complete cycle with exactly three positive angles

**Corollary 3.** The ordinary central cycle cannot contain exactly
three strictly positive canonical angles.

Suppose it did. The sole nonpositive central E is followed by a
positive central P. The already proved signed-edge exclusions force
E to retain its forward point and P to omit its forward point:
C1092-MPPM-NEGATIVE-MISSING-POSITIVE-MISSING-EXCLUSION.md excludes
a missing negative predecessor, and
C1092-MPPM-NEGATIVE-MISSING-POSITIVE-ENDING.md excludes every
nonpositive predecessor of a positive retaining recipient.

The predecessor C of E is positive. The successor P of E strictly
owns its crosspoint, so Corollary 2 applies and gives (4) for this
same actual E. Lemma 2 of
C1092-MPPM-ONE-POSITIVE-CONTINUOUS-ENDING.md is a local theorem:
a retaining negative owner with (2),(4) and its actual native whole
bar cannot precede a positive missing owner. Its proof uses neither
the total number of positive angles nor a negative predecessor.
Here E's bar is the one already recorded in Corollary 2, and P's
required strict crosspoint is universal. Every premise is therefore
literal, so that local lemma contradicts this E,P pair.

This proves the exactly-three-positive ending. In particular the
argument does not require all four wall origins as an entrance:
the particular native bar used belongs to the actual next wall of
the nonpositive square and is supplied by that square's own
unconditional halfpoint. ∎
