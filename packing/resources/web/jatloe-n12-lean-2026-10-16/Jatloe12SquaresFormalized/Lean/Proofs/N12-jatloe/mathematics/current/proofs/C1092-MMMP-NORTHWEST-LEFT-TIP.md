# C1092 MMMP: a northwest point at the left wall's far-auxiliary height

Every terminating decimal is an exact rational. All squares are actual original owners of common side a>1. Strict ownership, weak exterior face exits and weak separation are understood as in the preceding MMMP chapters.

**Theorem.** In the whole original MMMP family the actual northwest square Q7 strictly owns

    Z=(1.15,2.4).

The proof requires no top origin and no new ownership choice for F0 or G0.

## 1. Existing strict points and the genuine opposite face

Write N=Q7. The preceding center and diagonal-point theorems supply the following points strictly in this same square:

    O=(2,2), A=(1.5,2), B=(1.5,2.5), D=(1.39,2.61).

The theorem `C1092-MMMP-UPPER-STRICT-POINTS.md`, Sections 1–4, supplies

    E=(2.14,2.5) strictly in the different actual Q6.

That proof also establishes the exact face statement needed here: for either canonical sign of N, E is an upper-longitudinal exit. In the positive frame u=(d,r), v=(-r,d), its upper u face is consequently at most 2.14d+2.5r. In the negative frame u=(d,-r), v=(r,d), the upper u face is at most 2.14d-2.5r. Here d>=r>=0 and d^2+r^2=1.

For the negative canonical sign, the same actual-support theorem proves

    r<.14 and d>.99.                                  (1)

Every assertion just listed is unconditional in the whole MMMP family.

## 2. A full-interval positive-angle bound

In the positive sign use the rotated chart

    (X,Y)=(2-y,x-2).

This quarter-turn preserves the square's canonical positive sign; choose the corresponding face labels u_C=dX+rY and v_C=-rX+dY. Let u_0,v_0 be its upper face coordinates. Section 5 of `C1092-MMMP-NORTHEAST-ANGLE.md` proved directly from strict O, the half-unit crosspoints, the foreign southwest/northeast markers, and the actual left-wall endpoint that

    u_0<=.35(d-r),
    v_0<=.35(d+r),
    v_0>=a+.39r-d.                                    (2)

For clarity, the last foreign endpoint in that chart is F=(-.39,-1), whose original coordinates are (1,2.39), strictly owned by actual Q8. Thus (2) does not use any missing auxiliary as if it were owned.

The last two inequalities give

    1<a<=1.35d-.04r.

Set tau=r/d in [0,1]. The function

    g(tau)=(1.35-.04tau)/sqrt(1+tau^2)

strictly decreases there, since its derivative has the sign of -.04-1.35tau. At tau=7/8 its value is

    g(7/8)=10.52/sqrt(113)<1,

because 10.52^2=110.6704<113. Hence necessarily

    r/d<7/8.                                         (3)

## 3. The target's four strict bands

First take the positive frame in original coordinates. The lower u band of Z follows from E's upper-u cap and the common side, because

    u(E)-u(Z)=.99d+.1r<=sqrt(.9901)<1<a.

Its upper u band follows strictly from B, since

    u(B)-u(Z)=.35d+.1r>0.

Its lower v band follows strictly from A, since

    v(Z)-v(A)=.35r+.4d>0.

Finally D supplies its upper v band, because (3) gives

    v(D)-v(Z)=.21d-.24r>0.

Thus Z is strict for every positive canonical angle.

In the negative frame the same opposite cap gives its lower u band, now with the even smaller gap

    u(E)-u(Z)=.99d-.1r<=.99<1<a.

Its upper u band follows from A:

    u(A)-u(Z)=.35d+.4r>0.

Its lower v band follows from O and (1):

    v(Z)-v(O)=.4d-.85r>.4(.99)-.85(.14)=.277>0.

Its upper v band follows from D, with difference

    v(D)-v(Z)=.24r+.21d>0.

This includes the axial endpoint. The diagonal representative has already been excluded by the complete positive-boundary support theorem; it would also violate (1) or (3). All remaining orientations have therefore been covered. The only opposite-face step used a strict inequality of a fixed Euclidean norm against a>1, so a weak exterior contact of E creates no exception. The point Z belongs strictly to the one actual N=Q7, as claimed.
