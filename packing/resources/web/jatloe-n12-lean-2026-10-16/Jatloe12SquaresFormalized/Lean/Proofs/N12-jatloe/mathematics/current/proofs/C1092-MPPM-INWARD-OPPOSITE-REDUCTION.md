# C1092 MPPM: every inward owner has an inward opposite, completing OOOI

Retain the complete original no-omission MPPM entrance, its twelve actual
open square owners and the simultaneous quarter-turn R(x,y)=(4-y,x).
The common side satisfies 1<a<=1.001. All strict ownership and genuine
omission statements below refer to those original owners. The local
continuous proofs used here require only a>1 unless their stated
entrance says otherwise.

An O owner owns its ordinary pair, even if it also has its inward pair.
An I owner is genuinely nonordinary and owns its inward pair. The
completed no-omission reduction and elbow exclusion leave only OOOO,
OOOI and OIOI up to simultaneous rotation. Every I has two O neighbors.

## 1. The entire OOOI pattern is excluded

**Theorem 1.** Every genuinely inward owner in the original no-omission
MPPM family has an inward opposite owner. Consequently OOOI is
impossible for every angle-sign pattern and every tangent boundary
alternative.

**Proof.** Normalize an inward owner to Q4. Its inward pair is F4,G3.
Split according to actual strict membership of the physical center
O=(2,2). A point on a face belongs to the omitted case.

If O is omitted and G0 is also omitted, both angle signs already have
complete contradictions. The nonpositive case is
C1092-MPPM-SMALL-INWARD-THREE-SQUARE-ENDING.md, whose local theorem
retains the actual Q4,Q5,Q9 and does not require an opposite type.
The positive case with ordinary opposite is excluded by
C1092-MPPM-POSITIVE-MISSING-INWARD-FAR-ENDING.md; its inward-opposite
continuation is excluded by
C1092-MPPM-POSITIVE-MISSING-INWARD-ORIGIN-ENDING.md. These cover the
two possible opposite types left by the original reduction.

If O is omitted and G0 is owned, nonordinariness means F0 is omitted.
C1092-MPPM-INWARD-OUTER-OWNED-ANGLE.md proves the strictly negative
frame and actual bottom origin. The complete two-square ending in
C1092-MPPM-OUTER-OWNED-INWARD-FAR-ENDING.md then excludes an ordinary
opposite. Thus an inward owner omitting O has an inward opposite.

Suppose O is strict in Q4. The completed theorem
C1092-MPPM-CENTERED-POSITIVE-INWARD-ENDING.md excludes every
nonnegative canonical angle, including zero. The centered negative
cap and angle theorems are

    C1092-MPPM-CENTERED-NEGATIVE-INWARD-H-CAP.md,
    C1092-MPPM-CENTERED-NEGATIVE-INWARD-ANGLE.md.

They prove, in the centered negative frame,

    s/(1+c)<21/100, H<17/125,
    F0 is omitted.

If G0 is owned, the latter theorem supplies the strict outer halfpoint
and actual bottom origin, and
C1092-MPPM-CENTERED-OUTER-OWNED-FAR-ENDING.md excludes an ordinary
opposite. If G0 is omitted, the stronger actual lower face
U>=-c+.4s and
C1092-MPPM-CENTERED-INWARD-OMITTED-G-FAR-ENDING.md exclude an
ordinary opposite without a bottom-origin premise. These are the
exhaustive literal alternatives for G0, including boundary contact.

Therefore every centered inward owner also has an inward opposite.
The same statement holds in every simultaneous quarter-turn. An OOOI
pattern has exactly one inward owner and contradicts this conclusion.
This proves Theorem 1. No omitted auxiliary has received a new owner.

## 2. The precise remaining inward pattern

**Corollary 2.** Every remaining configuration containing an inward
owner has the OIOI pattern. Normalize its two inward owners to Q4,Q6;
then its actual ordinary owners are Q5,Q7. All four actual central
canonical angles are strictly negative. Both inward owners omit their
respective outer F points, F0 and F9. Any inward owner missing the
physical center owns its corresponding outer G point.

Indeed the proof of Theorem 1 shows every inward owner strictly
negative, for both center statuses. The negative-inward successor-sign
theorem applies to each such owner and makes Q5,Q7 strictly negative
as well. If an inward owner omits the center, its G-omitted alternative
was completely excluded, so it owns G and, by nonordinariness, omits F.
If it owns the center, the centered negative angle corollary gives the
same F omission directly. These claims do not identify a broader
ownership family with the historical last assignment.

## 3. At least three genuine wall origins in every remaining OIOI case

**Corollary 3.** In the normalized OIOI pattern of Corollary 2,

    (3,2) is strict in actual Q10,
    (1,2) is strict in actual Q8.                   (1)

At least one of the following also holds:

    (2,1) is strict in actual Q9,
    (2,3) is strict in actual Q11.                  (2)

If both inward owners omit the physical center, all four origins in
(1)-(2) are strict in their indicated actual walls. The same is true
if both inward owners own their respective outer G points.

**Proof.** Q4 owns F4 and Q6 owns F5. Consequently ordinary Q5 omits
its forward F4, and ordinary Q7 omits its forward F5. The ordinary
halfpoint theorem gives (2.5,1) strictly in Q5 and (1.5,3) strictly
in Q7. The complete actual maps are the rotations of the first-row
map in C1092-MPPM-ORDINARY-CENTRAL-REDUCTION.md, Section 3; that local
map requires the named owner's ordinary pair, its genuine forward
omission, the corresponding actual wall marker, paired-corner point,
and adjacent ordinary wall auxiliary. It does not require the other
central squares to be ordinary. Every one of these local inputs holds
in the present original no-omission entrance.

The actual paired-corner transfer then gives (1). Explicitly the
Q5 halfpoint is foreign to Q1 and transfers the right origin to Q10;
the Q7 halfpoint is foreign to Q3 and transfers the left origin to Q8.
The real corner and boundary walls and their strict original markers
remain the same in each transfer.

At most one of the two distinct open squares Q4,Q6 can contain O.
Hence at least one omits O and, by Corollary 2, owns its outer G.
The outer-owned inward angle corollary gives the corresponding strict
outer halfpoint and its actual wall origin. The literal table is

| Inward premise | Strict halfpoint in that same owner | Strict actual wall origin |
|---|---|---|
| G0 in Q4, O omitted | (1,1.5) in Q4 | (2,1) in Q9 |
| G9 in Q6, O omitted | (3,2.5) in Q6 | (2,3) in Q11 |

If an inward owner instead contains O and owns its outer G point,
the centered negative angle corollary supplies the identical halfpoint
and actual transfer in that sector. Thus any two outer-G-owned inward
owners supply both origins in (2), as claimed.

This proves the exact remaining OIOI profile. It is a reduction, not
an OIOI contradiction. In particular an inward owner that contains O
and omits its outer G point has not been assigned that sector's
conditional origin or an ordinary far-wall advance.

## 4. Remaining scope of the broader program

The whole original C1092 MMMM and MMMP families and all MPPM
wall-omission families already have complete continuous endings.
Theorem 1 adds the entire original no-omission OOOI pattern.

The only remaining no-omission MPPM alternatives are now:

1. OOOO with a mixture of owned and omitted forward points; its
   all-four-owned and all-four-missing alternatives are excluded.
2. OIOI with the actual negative-angle and origin profile proved here.

Neither remaining obligation is used as a theorem in this chapter.
