# C1092 MPPM: a positive ordinary owner of its forward point excludes the preceding origin

All terminating decimals are exact rationals. Retain the original no-omission OOOO entrance, the twelve actual open square owners, and the common side 1<a<=1.001. A point is either strictly in its named open square or omitted from that square; the omitted alternative includes a boundary point. No omitted auxiliary is assigned another owner.

## 1. The actual exclusion without an origin premise

**Theorem 1.** Every positive ordinary central owner that strictly owns its forward point excludes its preceding wall origin. In the four literal sectors the statement is

| Positive central owner | Strict forward premise | Point excluded from that central |
|---|---|---|
| Q4 | G3=(1.74,2) | (1,2) |
| Q5 | F4=(2,1.74) | (2,1) |
| Q6 | G6=(2.26,2) | (3,2) |
| Q7 | F5=(2,2.26) | (2,3) |

The origin need not be owned by its wall for this exclusion to hold. No sign or forward-membership condition is imposed on another central square.

**Proof.** Normalize the recipient to Q5, which is positive and owns its ordinary pair G5,F6 and its forward point F4. Its actual predecessor Q4 owns the ordinary pair F0,G0.

If the actual bottom square Q9 strictly owns P=(2,1), then P is excluded from the different actual Q5, as desired. Suppose P is omitted from Q9.

The predecessor Q4 must strictly own G3. Otherwise the local ordinary-marker halfpoint theorem, with the literal first-row source map in C1092-MPPM-ORDINARY-CENTRAL-REDUCTION.md, Section 3, gives (1,1.5) strictly in Q4 and then P strictly in Q9. Its four point exclusions are Q4's own omission of G3, the Q8 marker, the Q0 inner corner, and G2 in Q9. This contradiction uses only those named actual inputs.

Furthermore Q4 must have strictly positive canonical angle. If its angle were nonpositive, Corollary 2 of C1092-MPPM-ORDINARY-NEGATIVE-HALFPOINT.md would again give its strict halfpoint and the same actual Q9 origin. That corollary is proved for the whole OOOO family and does not presume another forward membership.

The actual paired-corner transfer, Theorem 7 of C1092-DIRECT-WALL-POINT.md, now gives a different consequence by contraposition. The actual Q0 strictly owns (1,.914),(1,1) and has its real first-quadrant walls. The actual Q9 strictly owns (2,.914),F2=(1.6,1) and has its real bottom wall. If Q0 excluded H=(1,1.5), that theorem would put P strictly in Q9. Since P is omitted, H is strict in Q0, and hence H is excluded from the different actual Q4.

Apply the local Theorem 1 of C1092-POSITIVE-D-TRANSFER.md to the two actual squares Q4 and Q5, with the simultaneous quarter turn R(x,y)=(4-y,x). Both local squares have positive canonical angle and strictly own the required marker, ordinary pair, and forward point. The four exclusions of the source Q4 are exactly

| Source exclusion | Genuine actual source |
|---|---|
| H=(1,1.5) | Strict in Q0 by the preceding actual corner transfer |
| N=(1.65,2.35) | The original marker in Q7 |
| W=(.914,2) | The original marker in Q8 |
| F0_local=(1,2.26) | Actual F1, strict in Q8 by the no-omission entrance |

That local theorem concludes that R^(-1)(Q5) excludes (1,2), or equivalently that Q5 excludes R(1,2)=(2,1). This is precisely the missing actual-wall-origin alternative under consideration.

The two actual Q9 alternatives are exhaustive, including tangency. Simultaneous quarter turns preserve all owners, wall data and canonical signs, and give the other three rows. Thus Theorem 1 is proved. No all-forward-owned cycle theorem was used. ∎

## 2. Three simultaneous faces in the positive square

**Corollary 2.** Normalize such a positive forward-owned square as C=Q4, translate by (-2,-2), and write

    u=(c,s), v=(-s,c), c>=s>0, c^2+s^2=1,
    U<u·X<U+a, V<v·X<V+a,
    A=U+a, H=V+a.

Then the same actual square satisfies

    U>=-c-s, V>=.4s-c, H<=s.                       (1)

If it additionally omits I=(0,-.26), or original F4=(2,1.74), then

    A<=-.26s.                                      (2)

Every face inequality in (1)-(2) is weak; no strict interior membership is inferred from an omission.

**Proof.** The strict points of C are its marker M=(-.35,-.35), its ordinary pair P=(-1,-.26),G=(-1,-.4), and its forward T=(-.26,0). By Theorem 1 it excludes D=(-1,0).

At D its lower-u band and lower-v band follow from strict P, with increments .26s>0 and .26c>0. Its upper-u band follows from strict T, with increment .74c>0. Thus D is upper v and H<=s.

The actual paired Q0 owns C0=(-1,-1), so C excludes C0. Both upper bands there are strict, from M and P. If C0 were lower v, the opposite face H<=s would give a<=c<=1. Hence C0 is lower u and U>=-c-s.

The point B=(-.4,-1) is actual F2 in Q9 and therefore excluded from C. Its lower-u band follows from G, with increment .6(c-s)>=0. Its upper-u band follows from M, with increment .05c+.65s>0. Its upper-v band follows from P, with increment .6s+.74c>0. Consequently B is lower v and V>=.4s-c. The zero increment when c=s still compares with a strict anchor and causes no endpoint loss.

Finally suppose I is omitted from C. Its lower-u band follows from M, with increment .35c+.09s>0. Its upper-v band follows from T, with increment .26(c+s)>0. If I were lower v, H<=s would imply a<=s+.26c<=1.26/sqrt(2)<1. It therefore must be upper u, giving (2). This last conclusion uses I's explicit omission only; when I is strict in C it is not invoked. ∎

## 3. Exact scope

Theorem 1 excludes D from a positive forward-owned ordinary square, including the ordinary/inward overlap where that square also owns the adjacent inner point. Corollary 2 retains that overlap in (1); its extra cap (2) requires the stated additional omission.

This chapter supplies actual faces for the mixed-forward OOOO cycle. It does not claim a wall-center order, an angle bound, or a full cyclic contradiction for a forward-owned sector.
