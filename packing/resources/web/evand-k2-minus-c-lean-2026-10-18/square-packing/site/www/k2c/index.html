Short of a Square

Remove c unit squares from a k × k grid. For small c the rest still needs a square of side k; for larger c, tilting lets them squeeze into a smaller one. Write c*(k) for the largest c with s(k² − c) = k. Every bound on this page is an explicit packing of k² − c squares in a square of side less than k, for every k in a range, checked by the Lean kernel.

Status. All theorems below are proved in Lean 4 with Mathlib, from the plain definition of a packing, using only the standard axioms (propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound). Two of them are, as far as we know, new bounds; the others formalize results of Kevin Fang and Sungjoon Ryu, one of them on a wider range than its source. Not peer reviewed; the novelty check covers the jlevy/squares register and Ryu's preprint only.

The construction

An L of tilted rows

All the square-root and k2/5 bounds use Ryu's L-shaped packing (after Erdős and Graham): an axis-parallel grid of (k − b)² squares, and two bands of rows of b squares each, tilted just enough that a row is a little narrower than b. The whole fits in side k − b + w < k. Each row is tilted, so successive rows are spaced slightly more than 1 apart, which costs about 2L/b along a band of length L: about 4k/b in all. The rest of the loss is at the four band ends, the triangles under the first row and over the last.

Empty ends

Each end wastes about b. Total ≈ 4b + 4k/b, best at b ≈ √k: Ryu's 8√k.

Axis-filled ends (new)

About b/2 axis-parallel squares fit under the first row and over the last. Total ≈ 2b + 4k/b, best at b ≈ √(2k): 4√2·√k ≈ 5.66√k. With b = ⌈√(2k − 7)⌉ the four ends hold exactly 2b squares.

Staircase ends

Columns at one tilt, axis layers under them that follow the rising ceiling, and short rows at the wall (Ryu's §6, after Bui). End cost O(b2/3); with b ≈ k3/5 the exponent is 2/5.

For the axis-filled ends, a search over every b and every axis layering of this construction finds nothing better than 4⌈√(2k − 7)⌉ − 1 for 7 ≤ k ≤ 3·10⁵. Better packings at small k exist but come from search (de Winter's, for example), not from a formula.

The proof

What Lean checks

  • The band and its ends. A band lifted by y₀, together with any packings of the two end regions, packs the strip (Ryu's Lemma 5.1, RyuEnds.band_ends); the three parts are separated by the rotated coordinate.
  • The staircase. Columns, layers and wedge rows pack an end region when each piece fits under the ceiling and the pieces are pairwise separated by one line (RyuStair.staircase). Our variant takes the column tilt tan(α/2) = (√2 + 1/v)/v with b ≈ v³, lets each layer run to the wall when it fits, and counts squares from the floors directly, with no area argument (EndP.count). The end cost is at most 4.33 v² + 11 v + 30 (end_bound); the leading 2√2 + 3/2 is Ryu's limit constant.
  • The count. k² − N is the slack of the side, plus (R − 1) tan θ per band, plus the four end costs (Ryu's waste identity, here an algebraic identity), which gives c*(k) < 4k/b + 4(4.33 v² + 11 v + 30) (packs_stair), and then the closed forms in k2/5.
  • Fang's theorem is an induction over a gluing lemma (Arslanov–Mustafin–Shangitbayev's decomposition) with 18 exact piece certificates, each checked by decide in the kernel.
