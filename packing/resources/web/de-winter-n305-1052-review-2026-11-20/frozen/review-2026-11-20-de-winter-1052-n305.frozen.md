## The Claim and What It Needs

Issue #1052 claims $s(305) \le C$ with $C = 17.945755838742$, the 12-place print of the
certificate’s side $S$, rounded up.
The certificate proves $s(305) \le S$: there are 305 closed unit squares, rotated
freely, with pairwise disjoint interiors inside the closed square $[0, S]^2$. The claim
follows when $C \ge S$, and directly when the same squares are accepted in $[0, C]^2$.

The two files are read as follows.

- **Form J**, `n305_exact.json`: one JSON object with exactly the keys `n`, `side`,
  `square_size`, `format` and `squares`; each square a list of four scalar strings
  $[x, y, \cos, \sin]$, read as the unit square with centre $c = (x, y)$ and edge
  direction $u = (\cos, \sin)$: the set $c + \alpha u + \beta v$ with
  $\lvert\alpha\rvert \le 1/2$ and $\lvert\beta\rvert \le 1/2$, $v$ the quarter turn of
  $u$. The file’s `format` string says so.
- **Form C**, `n305_witness.yaml`: a softschema envelope with
  `contract: packing.squares:Witness/v2` around a Witness/v2 record with
  `representation: corners`, `scalar.kind: rational`, `origin: lower-left` and
  `axes: x-right-y-up`: per square four corners, each a pair of exact scalars.

The hypotheses are six:

1. **The reading.** Form J is UTF-8 JSON with no duplicate key, no number with a
   fraction or exponent and no non-finite constant; `n` a positive integer,
   `square_size` the string `"1"`, `side` and every coordinate a scalar string.
   Form C is one YAML document with no directives, anchors, aliases, explicit tags or
   duplicate keys; the envelope above with its `contract` and `envelope` keys; the
   witness keys within the schema’s list with every required key present,
   `square_size: '1'`; the squares listed with distinct ids, each with exactly an id and
   four corners of two scalars, and no plain scalar that YAML would read as a float.
   Every scalar is a string `p` or `p/q`, or in form J or a quoted YAML scalar a finite
   decimal, with no sign but an optional minus, no leading zero and $q \ge 1$; lowest
   terms are recorded, not required.
2. **The count and the twin.** 305 squares in each file, the same side, and square $k$
   of form C is square $k$ of form J: its four corners are $c \pm u/2 \pm v/2$, listed
   as a cyclic rotation of them in either sense.
3. **Unit squares.** In form J, $\cos^2 + \sin^2 = 1$ exactly for every row.
   In form C the four corners, in the order listed, are a unit square.
4. **Containment.** Each square lies in $[0, S]^2$, the origin at the lower-left corner.
5. **Disjoint interiors.** No two squares’ interiors meet.
6. **The sense.** $x$ to the right and $y$ up, which form C states; form J’s direction
   and its quarter turn span the same square whichever edge it names.

`r1052_check.py` decides the first two through the reader and the next three twice, in
each file.

- **Method A** (`r1052_poses.py`, Python integers) decides poses.
  It writes each pose over one integer denominator $D$, $c = (X, Y)/D$ and
  $u = (A, B)/D$, and accepts it as a unit square when $A^2 + B^2 = D^2$. For two
  squares with centre difference $d$, the projections on any of the four unit edge
  normals $u_i, v_i, u_j, v_j$ have half-lengths adding to the same
  $H = 1/2 + (\lvert\cos t\rvert + \lvert\sin t\rvert)/2$, $t$ the angle between the
  squares, so the separation on a normal $w$ is $\lvert d \cdot w\rvert - H$, an exact
  signed distance, and the margin is the largest of the four, computed as one fraction
  from integers. Two convex polygons have disjoint interiors exactly when a line parallel
  to an edge of one of them weakly separates them, and are disjoint exactly when one
  does so strictly, so the margin’s sign is the class: positive apart, zero contact,
  negative overlap. A wall’s clearance is the signed distance from the square’s extreme
  point, from the half-extent $(\lvert\cos\rvert + \lvert\sin\rvert)/2$. Given corners,
  method A makes its own pose: with $E = P_1 - P_0$ and $F = P_2 - P_1$ it requires
  $P_3 - P_2 = -E$, $P_0 - P_3 = -F$, $E \cdot E = F \cdot F = 1$ and $E \cdot F = 0$,
  and takes $c = P_0 + (E + F)/2$, $u = E$.
- **Method B** (`r1052_clip.py`, `gmpy2.mpq`) decides corners.
  Four points are a unit square in cyclic order when all four sides have squared length
  1 and both diagonals squared length 2; twice the signed area, $\pm 2$, gives the
  sense, and a clockwise listing is reversed.
  A pair is decided by clipping one square against the four closed half-planes to the
  left of the other’s edges (Sutherland–Hodgman, keeping points on the line), which
  gives the intersection exactly, both ways round: empty is apart, non-empty with zero
  area contact, positive area overlap, and the two orders must agree in class and area.
  For a pair apart, the squared Euclidean gap is the least squared distance from a
  vertex of one square to an edge of the other.
  Each wall is classed by clipping the square against the closed half-plane outside it,
  containment is decided apart (the intersection with the box has area exactly 1), and
  the clearances are read off the corners.
  Given poses, method B writes the corners itself as $c - u/2 - v/2$, $c + u/2 - v/2$,
  $c + u/2 + v/2$, $c - u/2 + v/2$.

The two share the reader (`r1052_reader.py`) and nothing else.
`r1052_decide.py` runs both on one file and compares them everywhere: the unit tests,
every wall’s class and clearance, every pair’s class, and, for every pair apart, that
method B’s squared Euclidean gap is at least the square of method A’s margin, as it must
be. The verdict is `ACCEPT` only when the blobs are the tree’s, both files are read and
are one packing, each file is decided `ACCEPT` by both methods with no disagreement, the
two files’ decisions are equal at every pair and wall, and both methods accept both
files in the box of the claimed side.
No distance filter and no tolerance is applied anywhere.

**F1. Feasibility.** Both files are the same exact packing at its side: 46,360 pairs in
each, each decided by both methods with equal classes and strictly apart, and every one
of the 305 squares a unit square inside the box by both, with area exactly 1 inside.
The bound $s(305) \le S$ holds.

**F2. Controls.** As [Controls](#controls) gives them.

**F3. The claim.** $C$ is the ceiling of $S$ at 12 places, above it by
$4.113289 \times 10^{-13}$. In the box of side $C$ the squares are accepted by both
methods in both files, so the claim is decided directly.
Control S1f shows that the floor would not do.
A record can therefore hold the bound as the 12-place decimal the claim prints, with the
certificate’s own coordinates as its proof.

**F4. The witness file’s form.** `n305_witness.yaml` opens with a `softschema` block
that holds only `contract: packing.squares:Witness/v2`, and its witness record has the
keys `id`, `n`, `side`, `square_size`, `representation`, `scalar`, `coordinates` and
`squares`, with no `claim`. The Witness/v2 schema lists `claim` among its required keys
(with `coordinate_provenance`, `method` and `limitations` inside it), and this lane’s
reader, written to the schema and to the layout of the author’s earlier witness at 299
(whose envelope also carries `schema`, `envelope: witness` and `status`, and whose
record carries a `claim`), refuses the file at the envelope.
The issue says the witness was not run through this repository’s checker.
The geometry is not in question: a copy with the two pieces of metadata inserted, and
nothing else changed, is read and decided `ACCEPT`, and is form J’s packing square for
square. A record that keeps the witness as its Witness/v2 form would need the `claim`
block, and the envelope’s keys, first.

**F5. Margins.** The 273 smallest margins lie between $1.715 \times 10^{-82}$ and
$4.081 \times 10^{-82}$: 195 between two axis-aligned squares, 20 between an
axis-aligned and a turned square, and 58 between two turned squares.
For 269 of them the Euclidean gap equals the margin; for the other four it is $\sqrt 2$
times the margin, corner facing corner.
Twelve margins lie between $9.999975 \times 10^{-45}$ (143, 153) and
$5.227452 \times 10^{-30}$ (107, 294), each with a square of F7, and the next is
$1.352932 \times 10^{-15}$ (80, 94); from there the margins rise through 135 below
$10^{-12}$ and 877 below $10^{-3}$, the profile of a packing optimized with its contacts
tight and then opened by a uniform $10^{-82}$-scale amount.

**F6. The side and the coordinates.** No square touches a wall.
8 squares stand exactly $3/(2 \times 10^{82})$ off the left wall and 4 off the bottom,
and 12 squares stand $1.6627324837752 \times 10^{-82}$ off the right wall and 3 off the
top, so the packing’s width and height are both $S - 3.162732 \times 10^{-82}$. Within
$10^{-20}$ of a wall stand 14 squares at the left, 7 at the bottom, 13 at the right and
3 at the top; the ones not at the least clearance are at $1.98 \times 10^{-82}$ to
$4.04 \times 10^{-30}$. The side is a terminating decimal of 82 places, the centres
decimals of up to 164 places, and every turned square’s half-angle tangent
$\sin/(1 + \cos)$ a terminating decimal of 81 or 82 places: consistent with a rational
enclosure built from decimal half-angle tangents, the whole packing then held

How the author built it is his to say ([Questions](#questions-for-the-author)); nothing
in the bound depends on it.

**F7. The near-axis squares.** 36 squares are turned from an axis by angles whose sines

## Verdict
