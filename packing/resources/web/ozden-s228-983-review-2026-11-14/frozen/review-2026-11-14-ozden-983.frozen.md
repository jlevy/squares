## The Claim and What It Needs

The issue claims $s(228) \le C$ with $C = 15.600801734812$, the 12-place print of the
certificate’s side $S$, rounded up.
The certificate proves $s(228) \le S$: there are 228 closed unit squares, rotated
freely, with pairwise disjoint interiors inside the closed square $[0, S]^2$. The claim
follows when $C \ge S$, and directly when the same squares are accepted in $[0, C]^2$.

The hypotheses are six:

1. **The reading.** The envelope is `packing.squares:Witness/v2` with `representation:
   corners`, `scalar.kind: rational`, `square_size: '1'`, `origin: lower-left`,
   `axes: x-right-y-up`, `angle_unit: not-applicable`; every scalar is a canonical $p$
   or $p/q$ with $q > 1$ and $\gcd(\lvert p\rvert, q) = 1$; the JSON twin holds the same
   tree.
2. **The count.** 228 squares with ids 1 to 228 in order.
3. **Unit squares.** Each listed quadruple of corners is a unit square, listed in cyclic
   order.
4. **Containment.** Each square lies in $[0, S]^2$.
5. **Disjoint interiors.** No two squares’ interiors meet.
6. **The rotation sense.** The corners are read in the plane as given, $x$ to the right
   and $y$ up; control G6 shows that reading the rotation the other way changes the
   verdict.

`l159_check.py` decides the first two through the reader, and the next three twice.

- **Method A** (`l159_sat.py`, Python integers).
  Each square’s eight coordinates are written over their least common denominator $D$,
  as integer points $A_k = D P_k$. The square is accepted when $U = A_1 - A_0$ has
  $U \cdot U = D^2$, $W = A_3 - A_0$ is $U$ turned a quarter either way, and
  $A_2 = A_0 + U + W$; its edge normals $U/D$ and $W/D$ are then exact rational unit
  vectors, and its own projection on each is an interval of length 1. On a normal
  $N/D_i$ of square $i$, both squares’ projections are brought to the common denominator
  $D_i^2 D_j$, so every comparison is between integers.
  A pair’s margin is the largest projection gap over the four normals of the two
  squares, kept as an exact fraction: positive is apart, zero is contact with disjoint
  interiors, negative is overlap.
  Two convex polygons with disjoint interiors are weakly separated by a line parallel to
  an edge of one of them, so the three signs are exactly those three classes.
  A wall’s clearance is the least signed distance from a corner to it.
- **Method B** (`l159_clip.py`, `gmpy2.mpq`). Four points are a unit square in cyclic
  order when all four sides have squared length 1 and both diagonals squared length 2:
  the converse of Pythagoras puts right angles at $P_1$ and $P_3$, which are then the
  two distinct points at distance 1 from both $P_0$ and $P_2$. The listing sense is the
  sign of the shoelace sum, $\pm 2$. A pair is decided by clipping one square against
  the four closed half-planes of the other’s edges (Sutherland–Hodgman, keeping boundary
  points), so the result is the intersection exactly.
  Empty is apart; non-empty with zero area is contact, since the interior of an
  intersection of convex sets is the intersection of their interiors; positive area is
  overlap, by that area.
  Each wall is classed the same way, by clipping the square against the closed
  half-plane outside it.
  Containment is decided separately: a unit square lies in the box exactly when its
  intersection with the box has area exactly 1.

The two share only the reader, which hands them integers.
The reader (`l159_reader.py`) refuses YAML anchors, aliases, explicit tags (the
non-specific `!` included), directives, duplicate and non-string keys, several
documents, and any resolved type but string, integer, mapping and sequence, with
integers in plain decimal form only; it refuses envelope, witness, claim, source and
square keys outside the schema’s lists and any layout but corners with rational scalars;
it requires the JSON twin, read with floats, constants, non-canonical integers and
duplicate keys refused, to hold exactly the same tree with exactly the same types.
The verdict is `ACCEPT` only when both methods accept every square, wall and pair, their
classes agree everywhere, and their listing senses agree.
