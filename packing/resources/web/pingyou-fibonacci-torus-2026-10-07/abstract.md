# The Fibonacci Geometry of Eleven Squares

PINGYOU LTD

*Source transcription. The original screenshot is authoritative. Mathematical symbols
were visually corrected from the raw OCR; the claims below are the source’s claims.*

**Abstract.** We give a reversible representation of Trump’s packing of eleven unit
squares by eleven congruent square–rectangle cells on a marked flat torus. The contact
equations have Fibonacci form $I+wQ$, where
$Q=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\1&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ and
$w=\sin\theta\cos\theta$. One marked point recovers every planar square, both active
clearances and the enclosing side. We prove that two inequalities characterize
feasibility in a two-parameter contact family. They force its unique minimum
$T_*=3.8770835900\ldots$ and the bound $T-T_*\ge\frac14|u-u_*|$, where
$u=\tan(\theta/2)$.

Let $\varphi=(1+\sqrt5)/2$. Among the quotients
$R_n=\mathbb Z[\varphi]/(\varphi^n-1)$ with $n\ge3$, the group generated by
$x\mapsto x+1$ and $x\mapsto\varphi x$ acts freely and transitively on unordered
pairs exactly when $n=5$, with $R_5\cong\mathbb F_{11}$. The reciprocal map
$[\alpha,\beta]\mapsto\{\alpha\pm\beta^{-1}\}$ over $\mathbb F_{11}$ identifies
the fifty-five two-element return orbits on the 121-cell cover with all fifty-five pairs
of base cells. The torus bundle defined by $-Q^3$ and its cyclic covers recover the same
finite modules and affine group. The normalized tori lie on a shortest closed modular
geodesic of length $4\log\varphi$. We prove that the minimizing side generates a
degree-eight number field whose normal closure has the full symmetric Galois group
$S_8$. We also give an explicit rational counterexample to Erdős’s square-packing
conjecture (Problem 106).
