## The Claim and What It Needs

The issue claims $s(179) \le C$ with $C = 13.881367160110$, the 12-place print of the
certificate’s side $S$. The certificate proves $s(179) \le S$ for its side: there are
179 closed unit squares, rotated freely, with pairwise disjoint interiors inside the
closed square $[0, S]^2$. The claim follows when $C \ge S$, and directly when the same
squares are accepted in $[0, C]^2$.

The hypotheses are six:

1. **The reading.** The envelope is `packing.squares:Witness/v2` with `representation:
   corners`, `scalar.kind: rational`, `square_size: '1'`, `origin: lower-left`,
   `axes: x-right-y-up`; every scalar is a canonical $p$ or $p/q$ with $q > 1$ and
   $\gcd(p, q) = 1$; the JSON twin holds the same tree.
2. **The count.** 179 squares with ids 1 to 179 in order.
3. **Unit squares.** Each listed quadruple of corners is a unit square.
4. **Containment.** Each square lies in $[0, S]^2$.
5. **Disjoint interiors.** No two squares’ interiors meet.
6. **The rotation sense.** The corners are read in cyclic order in the plane as given,
   $x$ to the right and $y$ up; control G6 shows that reading the rotation the other way
   changes the verdict.

`l101_check.py` decides the first two through the reader, and the next three twice.

- **Method A** (`l101_sat.py`, `fractions.Fraction`). A square is accepted when
  $u = p_1 - p_0$ has $u \cdot u = 1$, $p_3 - p_0 = \pm u^\perp$ and
  $p_2 = p_0 + u + (p_3 - p_0)$; its edge normals are then exact rational unit vectors.
  A pair’s margin is the largest projection gap over its four edge normals: positive is
  clear, zero is contact, negative is overlap.
  For convex polygons these three classes are exactly “closed sets apart”, “touching
  with disjoint interiors” and “interiors meet”, since the Minkowski difference of two
  squares is a polygon whose edges are parallel to theirs.
  A wall’s clearance is the least corner coordinate’s distance to it.
- **Method B** (`l101_clip.py`, `gmpy2.mpq`). A polygon is a unit square when its four
  edges have squared length 1, its turns one strict sign and its area $\pm 1$. A pair is
  the first square clipped by the other’s four closed half-planes: empty is clear, area
  0 is contact, positive area is overlap.
  A wall is the square clipped by the wall’s closed outer half-plane, read the same
  three ways, and containment is clipping to the box keeping area 1.

The two share only the reader, which hands them integers.
The reader (`l101_reader.py`) composes the YAML with PyYAML, refuses anchors, aliases,
duplicate keys and any tag but string, integer, mapping and sequence, fails closed on
any layout it does not expect, and requires the JSON twin, read with the standard
library with floats, constants and duplicate keys refused, to hold exactly the same tree
with exactly the same types.
The verdict is `ACCEPT` only when both methods accept every square, wall and pair and
their three-way classes agree everywhere.
