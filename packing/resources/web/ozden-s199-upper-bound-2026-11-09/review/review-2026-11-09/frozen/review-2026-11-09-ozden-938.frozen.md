## The Claim and What It Needs

The issue claims $s(199) \le 14.614689853287$. The certificate proves $s(199) \le S$ for
its side $S$: there are 199 closed unit squares, rotated freely, with pairwise disjoint
interiors inside the closed square $[0, S]^2$. Since the claim is at least $S$, the
claim follows.

**The lemma as used.** If $n$ unit squares pack in $[0, s]^2$, then
$n + 2\lfloor s \rfloor + 1$ pack in $[0, s+1]^2$. Keep the $n$ squares where they are.
The L-shaped region $[s, s+1] \times [0, s+1] \cup [0, s+1] \times [s, s+1]$ meets
$[0, s]^2$ only along the two segments $x = s$ and $y = s$, so nothing placed in it
overlaps an old square’s interior.

- Along the top, the squares $[a, a+1] \times [s, s+1]$ for
  $a = 0, \ldots, \lfloor s \rfloor$ fit, since $\lfloor s \rfloor + 1 \le s + 1$. That
  makes $\lfloor s \rfloor + 1$ squares.
- Along the right, the squares $[s, s+1] \times [b, b+1]$ for
  $b = 0, \ldots, \lfloor s \rfloor - 1$ fit, since $\lfloor s \rfloor \le s$. That
  makes $\lfloor s \rfloor$ squares, below the row.
- The row and the column are disjoint: the column ends at $y = \lfloor s \rfloor \le s$.

The corner cell $[s, s+1]^2$ goes to the row, whose last square reaches into it.
Giving it to the column instead also works: a row of $\lfloor s \rfloor$ and a column of
$\lfloor s \rfloor + 1$ (control L6). With $s = S_{172}$ and
$\lfloor S_{172} \rfloor = 13$ this is $14 + 13 = 27 = 2 \lfloor S_{172} \rfloor + 1$,
and $172 + 27 = 199$. Each arm keeps a slack of $S_{172} - 13 = 0.6147$; the L’s area is
$2S_{172} + 1 = 28.23$. The lemma does not use, and the issue does not claim, that 27 is
the most the L holds.

The certificate needs the six hypotheses of the precedent: the reading, the count, unit
squares, containment, disjoint interiors and the rotation sense.
Disjoint interiors here include 25 exact contacts, so both methods must class a gap of
exactly 0 as a contact: method A by a separating-axis margin of exactly 0, method B by a
clipped intersection that is nonempty with area exactly 0.

`l77_check.py` decides the reading and the count through the reader, and the rest twice:
method A in `fractions.Fraction`, method B in `gmpy2.mpq`. The two share only the
reader, which returns integers and fails closed on any layout it does not expect.
The JSON twin, read with the standard library’s `json`, must hold the same witness.
