## The Claim and What It Needs

For each $n \in \{204, 299\}$ the claim is $s(n) \le S$: there are $n$ closed unit
squares, rotated freely, with pairwise disjoint interiors inside the closed square
$[0, S]^2$. A Witness/v2 centre-basis file gives square $i$ as a centre $c_i$ and a
basis $u_i = (a_i, b_i)$, which the schema defines as $[\cos\theta, \sin\theta]$. With
$u_i^\perp = (-b_i, a_i)$ the square is
$Q_i = \{c_i + \alpha u_i + \beta u_i^\perp : \lvert\alpha\rvert, \lvert\beta\rvert \le 1/2\}$,
and the origin `lower-left` puts the box at $[0, S]^2$. The claim needs five hypotheses,
each checked separately:

1. **The reading.** The envelope is Witness/v2, the representation is centre-basis, the
   scalars are rational, the size is `'1'`, the origin is `lower-left` and the axes are
   `x-right-y-up`.
2. **The count.** There are $n$ squares with distinct ids, and the side field is the
   stated fraction.
3. **Unit squares.** $a_i^2 + b_i^2 = 1$ exactly.
   Without it $Q_i$ is a square of side $\lvert u_i\rvert$, not a unit square.
4. **Containment.** The projection of $Q_i$ on $e_x$ is $[x_i - w_i, x_i + w_i]$ with
   $w_i = (\lvert a_i\rvert + \lvert b_i\rvert)/2$, and the same holds in $y$. So
   $Q_i \subseteq [0, S]^2$ exactly when $w_i \le x_i \le S - w_i$ and
   $w_i \le y_i \le S - w_i$.
5. **Disjoint interiors.** For convex bodies,
   $\operatorname{int}(Q_i - Q_j) = \operatorname{int} Q_i - \operatorname{int} Q_j$. So
   the interiors meet exactly when $0 \in \operatorname{int}(Q_i - Q_j)$. Every facet
   normal of the polygon $Q_i - Q_j$ is an edge normal of $Q_i$ or $Q_j$, that is
   $\pm u_i$, $\pm u_i^\perp$, $\pm u_j$ or $\pm u_j^\perp$, and 0 lies outside the
   interior exactly when one facet inequality fails or is tight at 0. Hence the
   interiors are disjoint exactly when
   $\max_d \bigl(\lvert(c_j - c_i)\cdot d\rvert - h_i(d) - h_j(d)\bigr) \ge 0$ over
   those four unit axes $d$, with
   $h_k(d) = (\lvert u_k\cdot d\rvert + \lvert u_k^\perp\cdot d\rvert)/2$. Touching is
   allowed. Independently: $Q_i \cap Q_j$ is convex, and a convex set has positive area
   exactly when its interior is nonempty, so the interiors are disjoint exactly when
   $\operatorname{area}(Q_i \cap Q_j) = 0$.

`oz_check.py` decides hypotheses 1 to 3 directly.
Hypothesis 4 it decides twice: by the half-widths, in `fractions.Fraction`, and by the
explicit corners lying in $[0, S]$ with the square’s clipped area against the box equal
to its own area, in `gmpy2.mpq`. Hypothesis 5 it decides twice, by separating axes in
`Fraction` and by the exact Sutherland–Hodgman clipped area in `mpq`, for every pair.
The two methods share only the reader.
It fails closed on any layout it does not expect, and the lab’s JSON export, read with
the standard library’s `json`, must hold exactly the same squares.

## How the 299 Certificate Was Made

The 299 export’s claim block names its method: “rational half-angle basis and exact
radial repair”, with `repair_factor` $r = 10000000000000900000001/10^{22}$.
`oz_repair.py` undoes it exactly:

- every centre, mapped back by the scaling about $(1/2, 1/2)$ by $1/r$, is a decimal of
  at most 14 places (61 coordinates of one place, on the grid), and
  $S_0 = 1 + (S - 1)/r = 17.80123853397755$;
- every tilted basis is a rational half-angle with numerator and denominator of at most
  12 digits;
- the pre-repair packing has 79 overlapping pairs, the worst $-9.0 \times 10^{-14}$ at
  squares 224 and 235, and one square $2.6 \times 10^{-15}$ outside its box.

The repair clears them all.
At the worst pair, whose centres are one unit apart along the separating axis, it adds
$r - 1 = 9.00000001 \times 10^{-14}$ to the margin and leaves $9.99919 \times 10^{-23}$.
That is the certificate’s least margin.
The certificate’s 43 wall contacts (9 left, 11 right, 8 bottom, 15 top) are all upright
squares, which the dilation keeps in contact: it fixes $x = 1/2$ and $y = 1/2$ and
carries $S_0 - 1/2$ to $S - 1/2$. The certificate is checked as given; the construction
is only how the margins arose (question 3).

**F1. The 299 print is a floor, not a ceiling.** #893’s title, “s(299) ≤
17.801238533979”, and its Claim, “17.801238533979 rounded up at 12 places”, print the
12-place floor of the exact side.
So do the commit subject and the README’s title, though the title’s ellipsis marks a
truncated decimal. The exact side is $17.801238533979062111\ldots$. So $17.801238533979$
lies $6.2111469738 \times 10^{-14}$ below it, and $s(299) \le 17.801238533979$ does not

Rounded up at 12 places, the side is $17.801238533980$. The certificate and its fraction
are right, and the record states the fraction.
