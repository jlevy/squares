# s(29): minimal polynomial by homotopy continuation (2026-10-11)

**Result.**  s(29) = 5.93383346267692918968946061635201913843383418107788697463882591508025… is a root of an
irreducible integer polynomial p of degree **7213** (leading coefficient 325 digits, height 5074 digits), so p is its
minimal polynomial.  Coefficients: `work/n29_poly_q4.json` (27 MB, ascending; gitignored, not yet archived).

## How it was found

* System: `export_system.py` turns minpoly.py's elimination for n = 29 into 5 consistency polynomials F in the
  class angles t1..t5 (total degrees 26, 16, 16, 12, 26) plus S = Sn/Sd (depends on t3, t4 only).
* Run 1 (polyhedral homotopy, 44,448 paths, `solve.jl`) + `filter.jl` (contact-system consistency) + `regcheck.jl`:
  7,209 genuine regular roots.  Run 2 (multiprojective) found no others.  All ~17,650 singular / rejected endpoints
  were rechecked at 3000 bits (`hpfilter.jl`): junk.
* Refined to 40k digits (`refine.jl`); `reconstruct.py` failed: denominators at the generic LLL size.  That is the
  signature of an incomplete root set (tested on n = 108: dropping one root out of 144 gives exactly this; with all
  144, Ellsworth's polynomial is reproduced coefficient for coefficient).
* **Missing roots recovered algebraically** (`recover_k.py`): f = D P_known Q with Q of degree K unknown; the top K+1
  integer coefficients determine the rest linearly, and LLL finds integers making the next M coefficients integral.
  K = 1..3 failed; K = 4 gave a structured short vector (98 digits against ~30,000 generic) whose Q roots are stable
  under M = 12 and 24: two conjugate pairs at S = 4.9999858 +- 1.6e-6 i and 4.9999975 +- 1.0e-5 i.  (The held-out
  coefficients failed only because the coefficient denominators grow with the index up to the 325-digit leading
  coefficient; the K-recovered D only covered the constrained ones.)  With the 4 roots added (`addq.py`),
  `reconstruct.py` gives p: integral to 1e-34946 at 40k digits; the 20k-digit roots give the identical polynomial.
* The 4 roots have t1 = +-1.0000063 i (nearly +-i, where tan(theta/2) degenerates): Jacobian condition ~1e-11, next to
  the singular S = 5 junk (the 5 x 5 grid).  That is why both homotopy runs and Newton from nearby endpoints missed them.
  Their t vectors came from the same K = 4 lattice on each coordinate's own polynomial (`COL=i recover_k.py`), matched
  by F residual (`combo.jl`) and refined (`refine.jl`).

## Verification

| step | script | status |
|---|---|---|
| p irreducible over Q (primitive; degree patterns mod 12 primes ~2^61 leave no proper factor degree) | `irred.py` | rigorous |
| F has exactly one zero t* in tstar +- 1e-50 (Krawczyk, ball arithmetic) | `krawczyk.py` | rigorous |
| parametrization t_i = g_i(S)/(den_i p'(S)) (shape lemma), rebuilt from all 7213 roots; coefficients rational, integral to ~1e-21400, heights 5.3k-6.6k digits | `param.py` | numerical reconstruction (the identities below are what count) |
| F_k(tau) = 0 and Sn(tau) = x Sd(tau) in Q[x]/(p) | `verify_mod.py` | modulo 50 random 62-bit primes (Monte Carlo) |
| the full contact system (152 rows, 55 unknowns) is consistent at tau with all unknowns determined and S = x | `contacts_mod.py` | modulo 21 random 62-bit primes (Monte Carlo) |
| a root sigma of p in S0 +- 1e-38000 (sign change), p' != 0 there, tau(sigma) inside the Krawczyk box, Sd != 0 on the box | `link.py` | rigorous |

Conclusion: tau(sigma) is a zero of F in the box, hence = t*; so S(t*) = sigma, a root of the irreducible p.

The Monte Carlo steps: a false identity is a nonzero element of Q[x]/(p) whose reduction vanishes only modulo primes
dividing one fixed nonzero integer (at most ~10^10 digits for any reasonable height bound here, so at most ~5e8 of the
~1e17 primes in [2^61, 2^62)); 50 independent random primes all passing has probability < 10^-400 if the identity were
false.  A deterministic version needs explicit height bounds (billions of digits through the 1/p' denominators) or exact
arithmetic in a degree-7213 field; not attempted.

Not re-checked here: the packing inequalities (non-overlap) at the exact point.  The record's rational certificate
(`../minpoly/solve/n-29.cert`, verify_cert.py) covers validity at S slightly above s(29); `verify_exact.py`-style
exact inequality checks at t* would need interval evaluation of the centres over the Krawczyk box.
