## The Claims and What They Need

Issue #984 claims $s(228) \le C_{228} = 15.596043120921$ and #985 claims
$s(147) \le C_{147} = 12.651043556588$, each the 12-place print of its certificate’s
side $S$, rounded up.
Each certificate proves $s(n) \le S$: there are $n$ closed unit squares, rotated freely,
with pairwise disjoint interiors inside the closed square $[0, S]^2$. A claim follows
when $C \ge S$, and directly when the same squares are accepted in $[0, C]^2$.

The format is the author’s own, and it is neither of the Witness/v2 layouts the model
reviews read (corners, or centre and edge direction).
The JSON file has `n`, `side`, `side_decimal_info_only` (a decimal print, information
only) and `squares`, each `{cx, cy, t}` as exact rationals `p/q`, where $(c_x, c_y)$ is
the centre and $t = \tan(\theta/2)$. Then
$$\cos\theta = \frac{1 - t^2}{1 + t^2}, \qquad \sin\theta = \frac{2t}{1 + t^2}$$ are
rational, and with $u = (\cos\theta, \sin\theta)$ and $v = (-\sin\theta, \cos\theta)$
the square is $\{C + x u + y v : \lvert x\rvert, \lvert y\rvert \le 1/2\}$. The `.cert`
twin holds the same packing as text: two comment lines, `n S`, then `x y t` per square.

The hypotheses are six:

1. **The reading.** The JSON file has exactly the keys above, `n` a positive integer
   equal to the number of squares, every scalar a canonical $p/q$ with $q \ge 1$, no
   sign on zero, no leading zeros and $\gcd(\lvert p\rvert, q) = 1$, and no floats,
   constants or duplicate keys.
   The `.cert` twin holds exactly the same $n$, side and squares (it writes the 58 and
   38 upright $t$ as a bare `0`, which it reads as $0/1$).
2. **The count.** $n$ squares, numbered 1 to $n$ in the files’ order.
3. **Unit squares.** Any finite rational $t$ gives a rational unit vector $u$, since
   $(1 - t^2)^2 + (2t)^2 = (1 + t^2)^2$; this format cannot state a square that is not
   unit, and both methods check it anyway.
4. **Containment.** Each square lies in $[0, S]^2$. The files do not say where the box
   is; the issues say “every vertex in $[0, S]^2$”, and the files are accepted under
   that reading (F6).
5. **Disjoint interiors.** No two squares’ interiors meet.
6. **The rotation sense.** $\theta$ is read counterclockwise from the $x$-axis, $x$ to
   the right and $y$ up; control G6 shows that reading it the other way changes the
   verdict, and controls G5a and G5b that the sense is decided at a single square.

`l163_check.py` decides the first two through the reader, and the next three twice.

- **Method A** (`l163_sat.py`, Python integers).
  Each square’s centre is written over one denominator $E$, $C = (X, Y)/E$, and from
  $t = p/q$ its edge direction is $u = (a, b)/r$ with $a = q^2 - p^2$, $b = 2pq$,
  $r = p^2 + q^2$. The square is accepted when $a^2 + b^2 = r^2$. On a unit normal of
  square $i$, square $i$ projects to an interval of half-length $1/2$ and square $j$ to
  one of half-length $(\lvert\cos D\rvert + \lvert\sin D\rvert)/2$, $D$ the angle
  between them, the same on both of $i$’s normals; the gap is the centre separation
  along the normal less both half-lengths, an exact distance.
  All four gaps of a pair share the denominator $2 E_i E_j r_i r_j$, so the margin,
  their maximum, is decided on integers and kept as an exact fraction: positive is
  apart, zero is contact with disjoint interiors, negative is overlap.
  Two convex polygons with disjoint interiors are weakly separated by a line parallel to
  an edge of one of them, so the three signs are exactly those three classes.
  A wall’s clearance is the signed distance from the square’s extreme corner to it, from
  the half-extent $(\lvert a\rvert + \lvert b\rvert)/(2r)$.
- **Method B** (`l163_clip.py`, `gmpy2.mpq`). Each square is turned into its four
  corners $C \pm u/2 \pm v/2$ in cyclic order, with $\cos\theta$ and $\sin\theta$
  computed from $t$ in GMP rationals, and from then on only the corners are used.
  Four points are a unit square in cyclic order when all four sides have squared length
  1 and both diagonals squared length 2: the converse of Pythagoras puts right angles at
  the second and fourth corners, which are then the two points at distance 1 from both
  the first and the third.
  The listing must be counterclockwise (twice the shoelace sum is $+2$). A pair is
  decided by clipping one square against the four closed half-planes to the left of the
  other’s directed edges (Sutherland–Hodgman, keeping boundary points), so the result is
  the intersection exactly.
  Empty is apart; non-empty with zero area is contact, since the interior of an
  intersection of convex sets is the intersection of their interiors; positive area is
  overlap, by that area.
  Each wall is classed the same way, by clipping the square against the closed
  half-plane outside it.
  Containment is decided separately: a unit square lies in the box exactly when its
  intersection with the box has area exactly 1.

The two share only the reader (`l163_reader.py`), which hands them integers.
The verdict is `ACCEPT` only when both methods accept every square, wall and pair and
their classes agree everywhere; `l163_decide.py` runs both over every square, wall and
pair. `l163_check.py` also reads each `.cert` twin and requires it to equal the JSON
file, and decides each strict variant the same way.
