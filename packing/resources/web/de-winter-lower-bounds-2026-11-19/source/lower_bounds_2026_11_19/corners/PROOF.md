# A stronger corner measure for the Squares Project lower bound

Hosono’s computer-assisted result T-180 assigns a measure to a board of side k + δ for integer k ≥ 7. Every contained closed unit square captures at least one. On each stated interval the total board mass is at most (k + δ)² − 4(αδ + β). The global coverage of the original measures is the published premise used here. The original specification, fixed families and interval table identify it precisely. The original 136 direction and position records, which Hosono reports required 4,294 CPU-hours to replay, have not been re-run in this search. SquareMath lists T-180 as reported. The new local coverage and mass changes are replayable in this packet.

## Change at four corners

Each selected family has a fixed lower-left corner segment measure C. The source family checker derives C by subtracting the complete adjacent wall periods. Add its three quarter-turn images to identify the four congruent corners. The new measure removes selected material from a specified region at each corner. Most cases remove part of C. A wall-and-corner claim may also remove material from the adjacent wall periods or seam segments explicitly constructed by the checker. Some families also redistribute weights on rational test segments. The files in claims give the exact coordinates, fractions or new weights. Let d be the reduction in mass at one corner. All four rectangles are disjoint. A square can meet at most one since the board side is at least 7, the altered support fits in corner rectangles of width at most 2.5 (leaving at least two units between adjacent rectangles), and a unit square has diameter √2. Any additional segments only increase coverage elsewhere. A square meeting none of the removed support is covered by the original theorem. For all remaining squares the checker proves a unit of coverage using only the altered local measure and the unchanged source components explicitly included in the calculation. Thus the new total mass is at most (k + δ)² − 4(αδ + β + d).

For individual segment cuts, the checker can establish that a translated tile misses every removed segment throughout the whole offset cell. It bounds the directed distance of that segment from each tile edge over the offset and angle ranges. Five strictly positive Bernstein coefficients for any one edge place that segment outside. Some measures remove mass from several segments and add a point. If the total weight of added points guaranteed inside the tile is at least the total weight of removed segments it might meet, the original theorem covers that tile. Relative weights on both sides of this comparison are evaluated exactly in the same rational units. A proportional claim bounds only the length that can enter the swept tile. On each face a point of the segment must satisfy at least one of the five Bernstein coefficient inequalities, otherwise that face separates it. The convex hull of these five parameter intervals is an outer bound. Intersection over the four faces gives a conservative upper length, rounded upward. The unchanged area used in the wall variant is contained in [9/5,4]² for all side lengths considered. For other tiles, the checker establishes unit mass directly. Boundaries are treated as possibly intersecting in the outer test.

For each count in bounds.csv the listed side is an exact rational at which this upper estimate for total mass is strictly less than the count. If the unit squares fitted a smaller board, dilation and then replacement by centered unit squares would put pairwise separated closed unit squares into this board. Each would capture at least one. The mass budget excludes the smaller board, including for segment measures whose mass on some square edges can be positive.

## Covering precisely the local poses

Write u,v for the lower-left corner of a unit square’s axis-aligned bounding box. A square on the board has u,v ≥ 0. If it touches removed support in [0,X] × [0,Y], then u ≤ X and v ≤ Y. These offsets carry the physical wall constraint through rotations. Positive angles from 0 to 45 degrees suffice after checking both the local measure and its diagonal reflection. Square rotations by 90 degrees leave the shape unchanged.

Let t denote the tangent of half the angle, c = (1−t²)/(1+t²), s = 2t/(1+t²). The four vertices relative to (u,v) are (s,0), (c+s,s), (c,c+s), (0,c). Each directed edge gives an affine inequality in the point coordinates. Multiplication by (1+t²)² gives coefficients polynomial in t of degree at most four. On each angle interval the verifier recovers the five Bernstein coefficients by exact interpolation at five rational nodes. The corresponding half-planes define a common convex polygon inside every admissible square on that interval. The Bernstein basis is nonnegative and sums to one. The verifier checks that the intervals cover the whole range through √2−1 in both reflections.

For fixed offsets the checker integrates the length of each segment guaranteed to lie in that polygon. It rounds endpoint ranges outward, support lengths inward and densities downward. A strip represents a continuum of parallel segments. It uses the worst transverse coordinate to obtain a uniform length bound before weighting by the strip height. A moving vertical wall line is treated in the same way using the interval of its possible transverse coordinates. For a moving horizontal segment the intersection of its longitudinal ranges gives a valid stationary subsegment. Only the known central square [9/5,3]² is needed for any area contribution. The family structure describes the two adjacent walls and the translating corner primitives.

For an offset rectangle and a given segment, take its surviving parameter interval at each of the four corners. If all four have positive length, any convex combination of these four intervals is contained in the segment captured by the corresponding intermediate pose. This follows from convexity of the common polygon and linearity of the segment parameter. Its length is the same convex combination of the four lengths. After adding the lower bounds over all segments, the smallest of the four totals bounds every pose in the rectangle. Subdivide until this total reaches one. A failed leaf or a subdivision limit rejects the direction.

## Verification

Run python3 corners/verify.py from the packet root. It reconstructs the source geometry and mass saving, generates every angle constraint from the rotating vertices, recompiles the integer checker, and replays every local subdivision. The two reflected zero-density controls must fail. Run python3 corners/verify.py --independent for an additional Python arbitrary-precision replay of the two hardest reflected directions of every included family. Both options compare each claim against all 34 original formula branches, retaining outward bounds for irrational square roots.
