# Joining four translated regions of a certified measure

Let the original side be S and the new side T. Split the new board into quadrants. Enlarge each quadrant to overlap the others by extending it by radius r = 0.707107 beyond each internal midline. The radius is at least half the maximum coordinate projection of a unit square. Each tile whose center lies in a quadrant is wholly contained in its enlarged region. Assume T/2 + r ≤ S. Translate each enlarged region to the corresponding corner of the original board. In each coordinate this uses offset zero or S − T. These translations keep the enlarged regions inside the original board.

Give a point of the new board the maximum density of the translated original measures over all regions containing it. Every admissible unit square now captures at least the mass it would capture inside one complete original region, which is at least one. The density is symmetric under independent axis reflections and under exchange of coordinates. The total mass can be obtained by integrating the lower-left quadrant and multiplying by four.

For the exact upper estimate on total mass, each source rectangle in each of its eight symmetries is rounded outwards on a grid of 10⁻⁷, and its density upwards on a grid of 10⁻⁹. The new step function dominates the original. In the lower-left quadrant the interior has one patch, each boundary band has two and the central corner has four. The verifier integrates the pointwise maximum of the applicable step functions by slicing at every rectangle boundary and using integer arithmetic. The board count is greater than the resulting upper bound, so disjoint unit squares of that count cannot fit. The bundled Python replay sweeps horizontally. The search obtained its matching upper endpoint with a separate C++ implementation sweeping vertically.

When a claim gives a seam coordinate q, the split is at q on both axes. The two subintervals have lengths q and T − q. Each enlarged side fits in the source when max(q, T − q) + r ≤ S. On each axis the left, overlap and right-only zones are integrated separately. Reflection of a right-only zone into near-corner coordinates gives [0, T − q − r]. The overlap uses both translated densities. Integrating the maximum on all nine Cartesian products counts the full board once. The off-center measure need only retain transpose symmetry.

For a claim with round_steps, the central overlap uses the circumradius of a unit square. Every point of a tile lies within distance √(1/2) of its center. A point in the lower-left quadrant can therefore discard the upper-right source patch if its distance to the intersection of the two center lines exceeds that radius. The other three patches remain. Each slab is retained on the disk side of an outward-rounded integer staircase. The C++ search cuts into vertical slabs. The independent Python checker uses horizontal slabs, which have the same integral by transpose symmetry.
