# Repeating a central unit strip

Let the anchor board have side S. Its density is separately periodic with period 1 in each of p consecutive central unit strips, where p ≥ 3. The horizontal period relation holds for every vertical position, and the vertical relation holds for every horizontal position, up to boundaries of area zero. The checker clips the source rectangles to each strip, translates them to the first strip and compares the signed density changes at every rectangle corner. These equalities establish identical density functions almost everywhere. The band is centered, so each of the eight reflected and transposed copies used in the certified measure inherits the two period relations.

Add d copies of a central unit strip on each axis, or remove copies when d is negative, retaining at least two copies. Keep the material outside the periodic band, translating the far side by d. The new board is square, of side S + d. Every unit square has projection of width at most √2 on each axis. On either axis its projected interval cannot meet the exterior on both sides of the new periodic band. If it meets the exterior on the left, keep the interval in place. If it meets the exterior on the right, translate it by −d. In either case all densities on the interval agree with those of the anchor after this translation. If it lies inside the band, translate by an integer so its left endpoint is in the anchor's first central unit strip. Its right endpoint is less than 1 + √2 from the start of that strip, inside the anchor's band of at least three strips. The two coordinate translations together preserve the square's shape and its captured mass. Thus every unit square in the new board captures at least the unit mass guaranteed by the anchor certificate.

Write M for the anchor's total mass, b for the mass of one full-height repeated vertical strip and c for the mass of a central unit cell. Transpose symmetry makes the horizontal strip mass b as well. Partition the board into the interior period grid and the regions outside it. The outside corners retain their mass. Each outside edge strip is repeated linearly in the number of periods, and the interior grid contains the square of that number of identical cells. Subtracting the two totals yields M(d) = M + 2db + d²c, including for negative d. The checker integrates b and c as rational fractions of the source rectangles and applies the eightfold symmetry. A total strictly below n rules out n disjoint unit-square interiors.
