# Packing $n^2 - n + 1$ unit squares in a square of side less than $n$, for every $n \ge 15$

Kevin Fang

Draft v0.9, 2026-10-18, doi:10.5281/zenodo.23463481. Certificate bundle [Fan26] at commit 078789a4cfd36f915f1bfbe76d742413d79b2965; Lean formalisation [FanL26] at https://github.com/TheSnakeFang/squarepack-lean, commit 947bdae.

## Abstract

Let $s(N)$ be the smallest side of a square containing $N$ unit squares with disjoint interiors, rotations allowed. We prove $s(n^2-n+1)<n$ for every integer $n\ge15$. More generally, $n^2-n+1+p+2q$ unit squares fit below side $n$ whenever $p,q\ge0$ are integers and $16p+21q\le n-15$. The proof iterates the four-piece construction of Arslanov, Mustafin and Shangitbayev, using known and new base packings and exact rational packings of 257 squares in $17\times16$ and 443 squares in $22\times21$. Independently written exact programs check the finite inputs, and we supply generated certificates for $15\le n\le64$. Lean 4 formalises the first theorem and the two unmixed bounds, including kernel checks of all 18 input pieces. The contribution is an explicit threshold and construction, rather than a new asymptotic waste bound.

## 1. Introduction

Let $s(N)$ denote the side of the smallest square that contains $N$ unit squares with pairwise disjoint interiors, rotations allowed, and let $N(x) = \max\{N : s(N) \le x\}$ be the number of unit squares that fit in a square of side $x$. Trivially $s(N) \le \lceil \sqrt N \rceil$ and $N(k) = k^2$ for integers $k$. Erdős and Graham [EG75] asked about the waste $W(x) = x^2 - N(x)$ and proved $W(x) = O(x^{7/11})$. Chung and Graham obtained $O(x^{(3+\sqrt 2)/7}\log x)$ in 2009 [CG09]; their later claim of $O(x^{3/5})$ [CG20] has a calculation error identified by Arslanov and Bui [AB26], and Bui [Bui25] and McClenagan [McC26] give constructions achieving exponent $3/5$. Roth and Vaughan [RV78] proved a lower bound of order $(x\lVert x \rVert)^{1/2}$ when $x\lVert x\rVert > 1/6$, where $\lVert x \rVert$ is the distance from $x$ to the nearest integer. The quoted asymptotic upper bounds do not give the small threshold established here.

Throughout, $n$ denotes an integer side and $N$ a number of squares. For $N$ just below $n^2$: Nagamochi [Nag05] stated $s(n^2 - 1) = s(n^2 - 2) = n$ for $n \ge 2$ (Karakuş [Kar26] has since exhibited a counterexample to a lemma in that proof and independently recovered the $n^2 - 1$ case); Daniel [Dan26] has announced, unrefereed, $s(n^2 - 3) = n$ for $n \ge 6$ and $s(n^2 - 4) = n$ for $n \ge 5$. In the other direction, Friedman's survey [Fri09] records $s(n^2 - n - 1) < n$ for $3 < n < 11$ and, due to Cleemann, $s(17^2 - 17) < 17$. Arslanov, Mustafin and Shangitbayev [AMS21] proved

$$s(n^2 - n) < n \quad \text{for all } n \ge 12, \tag{1}$$

by a decomposition of the square into two integer blocks, one fixed squeezable rectangle and one squeezable rectangle from a parametric family (their Lemma 1). They showed moreover $s(16^2 - 15) < 16$, $s(17^2 - 16) < 17$ and $s(18^2 - 17) < 18$ by the same decomposition with two fixed pieces, i.e. $s(n^2 - n + 1) < n$ for $n = 16, 17, 18$, and claimed nothing for $n \ge 19$. Their three packings, refined by Ellsworth and Stead, are the entries $241, 273, 307$ of Ellsworth's table [Ell26], which continues Friedman's survey. One further case is on record: de Winter [dW26] found a packing of $211 = 15^2 - 15 + 1$ unit squares in a square of side $14.99796\ldots$ on 16 September 2026, registered and independently verified by the Squares Project [Lev26] and later improved by Ostashev to approximately $14.99634$; the record has since moved further. Before the constructions reported here (October 2026) we are not aware of any packing of $n^2 - n + 1$ squares below side $n$ for $n \ge 19$, nor of an explicit statement of the general bound; the Squares Project has since replayed our first three certificates ($N = 308, 343, 344$, Section 3) with its own exact implementations [Lev26, issue 484], and Daniel improved all three by local search from them, crediting the construction [Dan26, issue 489]: $s(308) \le 17.99827$, $s(343) \le 18.97824$, $s(344) \le 18.99446$. Since 14 October 2026 the Squares Project has tracked a theorem-level review of Theorems 1 and 2 at bundle commit `078789a` [Lev26, issue 603]; this is not a peer review.

**Theorem 1.** $s(n^2 - n + 1) < n$ for every integer $n \ge 15$.

**Theorem 2.** Let $n \ge 15$ and let $p, q \ge 0$ be integers with $16p + 21q \le n - 15$. Then $n^2 - n + 1 + p + 2q$ unit squares pack in a square of side less than $n$. In particular $s\big(n^2 - n + F(n)\big) < n$ with $F(n) = 1 + \max\{p + 2q : p, q \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0},\ 16p + 21q \le n - 15\}$, so $F(n) \ge 1 + 2\lfloor (n - 15)/21 \rfloor$ and $F(n) = 2n/21 + O(1)$.

Theorem 1 is the case $p = q = 0$. The cases $n = 15$ (de Winter) and $16, 17, 18$ [AMS21] were known; the explicit construction for every $n \ge 15$, from the two certified rectangles below, is new. Writing $J(n) = \max\{j \in \mathbb{Z} : n^2 - n + j > 0,\ s(n^2 - n + j) < n\}$ for the optimal excess, one has $J(n) < n$ trivially (area), and by [EG75] $J(n) = n - o(n)$: apply the waste bound at $x = n - 1/n$, where $N(x) = x^2 - W(x) = n^2 - 2 + n^{-2} - W(x)$. So the eventual existence of such packings is a consequence of the sublinear-waste theorems; those bounds, however, do not by themselves certify the threshold $n = 15$ or a case such as $n = 30$. As in [AMS21], what is proved here is an explicit threshold with checkable packings. We do not prove optimality or that $15$ is the smallest possible threshold.

The proof (Section 2) is a gluing lemma applied inductively, with two kinds of step: a $17 \times 16$ rectangle adds $16$ to the side and $1$ to the excess; a $22 \times 21$ rectangle adds $21$ and $2$. The base cases are finitely many exact certificates (Propositions 4–5; Section 3 gives the new direct square cases). Section 5 reports the certificates we generated for $n \le 64$; Section 6 describes certificates, checkers and the search; Appendix A has the search details.

## 2. The gluing lemma and the proof

**Definitions.** A *packing* of $N$ unit squares in a $W \times H$ rectangle is a family of $N$ closed unit squares contained in $[0, W] \times [0, H]$ with pairwise disjoint interiors; its *waste* is $WH - N$ (an integer when $W, H$ are). For $N$ unit squares and an integer side $n$, the *excess* is $j = N - (n^2 - n)$, and $n^2 - N = n - j$ is the waste in the nominal integer box $n \times n$. Following [AMS21], a packing in a $W \times H$ rectangle is *$\delta$-squeezable* ($\delta > 0$) if the same $N$ squares fit, as a packing, in $(W - \delta) \times (H - \delta)$; a $\delta$-squeezable packing is $\delta'$-squeezable for $0 < \delta' \le \delta$. The *integer block* $p \times q$ is the packing of $pq$ unit squares at the half-integer centres of a $p \times q$ rectangle. A *strict certificate* (Section 6) is a packing given by rational data in which the closed squares are pairwise disjoint and lie in the open box; it is a stronger, machine-checkable witness of the packing claim.

**Lemma 3 (gluing).** Let $a > b \ge 1$ and $a' \ge b' \ge 1$ be integers. Let $A$ be a $d$-squeezable packing of $ab - w_A$ unit squares in an $a \times b$ rectangle and $B$ a $d$-squeezable packing of $a'b' - w_B$ unit squares in an $a' \times b'$ rectangle, where $0 < d \le 1/2$. Put $n = b + b'$ and $c = n - a'$, and assume $n \ge a$ and $0 \le c \le a - d$. Then $n^2 - w_A - w_B$ unit squares pack in a square of side $n - d$.

*Proof.* Put $L = n - d$. Place the four pieces in $[0, L]^2$:

| piece | box | size |
|---|---|---|
| $A$ (squeezed) | $[0, a - d] \times [b', L]$ | $(a - d) \times (b - d)$ |
| $D$, integer block $(n - a) \times b$ | $[a - d, L] \times [b' - d, L]$ | $(n - a) \times b$ |
| $C$, integer block $c \times b'$ | $[0, c] \times [0, b']$ | $c \times b'$ |
| $B$ (squeezed) | $[c, L] \times [0, b' - d]$ | $(a' - d) \times (b' - d)$ |

(using $n - b = b'$, $n - c = a'$; $D$ is empty if $n = a$ and $C$ if $c = 0$). The boxes lie in $[0, L]^2$: $a - d \le L$ since $a \le n$, $c \le a - d \le L$, $b' \le L$ since $b \ge 1 > d$, and $b' - d \ge 0$. Their interiors are pairwise disjoint: $A$ and $D$ are separated by $x = a - d$; $C$ and $B$ by $x = c$; $A$ and $C$ by $y = b'$; $D$ and $B$ by $y = b' - d$; $A$ and $B$ by the strip $b' - d < y < b'$; and $C$ lies left of $x = c$ while $D$ lies right of $x = a - d \ge c$. Squares in different pieces therefore have disjoint interiors, and within a piece they do by hypothesis. The count is $(ab - w_A) + (n-a)b + c\,b' + (a'b' - w_B) = nb + (c + a')b' - w_A - w_B = n(b + b') - w_A - w_B = n^2 - w_A - w_B$. $\square$

The four-piece arrangement follows [AMS21, Figure 1]. Their infinite family uses a nonsquare rectangle from Lemma 1. Here $B$ is a square packing, and the output of the lemma is itself a $d$-squeezable square packing with waste $n - j$, so the lemma can be iterated; the two rectangles below are the other input.

**Proposition 4 (the rectangles $R$ and $R'$).** There is a packing $R$ of $257$ unit squares in a rectangle $(17 - \delta_R) \times (16 - \delta_R)$ with $\delta_R \ge 0.0007436$ (certificate `r17x16_n0257.cert`: rational width $W \le 16.999256308447$, height $H = W - 1$, every square in the open box); its waste in $17 \times 16$ is $15 = b - 1$. There is a packing $R'$ of $443$ unit squares in $(22 - \delta_{R'}) \times (21 - \delta_{R'})$ with $\delta_{R'} \ge 0.0094396$ (certificate `r22x21_n0443.cert`, $W \le 21.990560384076$, $H = W - 1$); its waste in $22 \times 21$ is $19 = b - 2$. (Rounded down from the exact values.)

**Proposition 5 (base cases).** For $15 \le n \le 26$ there is a packing of $n^2 - n + 1$ unit squares in a square of side $< n$: $n = 15$ from [dW26] (exact certificate by Daniel, side $\frac{7498980352483680782590502239189}{5 \cdot 10^{29}} < 15$), $n = 16, 17$ from [AMS21]/[Ell26] (exact certificates in [Dan26]), $n = 18$ by deleting one square from our packing of $308$ squares in side $< 18$, and $19 \le n \le 26$ from Section 3. For $11 \le m \le 14$ there is a packing of $m^2 - m$ unit squares in a square of side $< m$: the packing of $110$ squares in side $10.99678\ldots$ recorded in [Ell26] and the packings of (1) for $m = 12, 13, 14$, all with exact certificates in [Dan26]. Every piece named in this proposition and in Proposition 4 is $d_0$-squeezable with $d_0 = 1/10000$ (the smallest squeeze among them, $0.000690\ldots$ for $N = 308$, exceeds $d_0$; all are tabulated in the bundle's `theorem/PIECES.md`).

*Proof of Theorems 1 and 2.* Call $j$ *attainable at $n$* if some packing of $n^2 - n + j$ unit squares in a square of side $n - d_0$ exists. By Proposition 5, $1$ is attainable at $15 \le n \le 26$. For $27 \le n \le 30$ apply Lemma 3 with $A = R$ ($a = 17$, $b = 16$, $w_A = 15$), $B$ the packing of $m^2 - m$ squares with $m = n - 16 \in \{11, \ldots, 14\}$ ($a' = b' = m$, $w_B = m$, $c = 16 \le 17 - d_0$, $n \ge 17$), and $d = d_0$: the composite has $n^2 - 15 - m = n^2 - n + 1$ squares in side $n - d_0$, so $1$ is attainable at $27 \le n \le 30$. Now let $n \ge 31$ and suppose $j$ is attainable at $m = n - 16 \ge 15$, by a $d_0$-squeezable packing $B$ whose waste in the nominal $m \times m$ box is $m - j$. Lemma 3 with $A = R$ gives $n^2 - 15 - (m - j) = n^2 - n + 1 + j$ squares in side $n - d_0$, so $j + 1$ is attainable at $n$. Likewise for $n \ge 36$ with $A = R'$ ($a = 22$, $b = 21$, $w_A = 19$, $c = 21 \le 22 - d_0$): if $j$ is attainable at $m = n - 21 \ge 15$ then $n^2 - 19 - (m - j) = n^2 - n + 2 + j$, so $j + 2$ is attainable at $n$. Given $p, q$ with $16p + 21q \le n - 15$, the side $r = n - 16p - 21q \ge 15$ is reached from $n$ by $p$ steps of $16$ and $q$ steps of $21$; if $r \ge 31$, reduce $r$ further by steps of $16$ (each only adds to the excess) until $15 \le r \le 30$, where $1$ is attainable; climbing back up gives attainability of $1 + p + 2q$ at $n$ (at least). Packings of side $n - d_0 < n$ give $s < n$ by definition, and deleting squares keeps a packing, so every integer $j'$ with $0 \le j' \le 1 + p + 2q$ is attainable too. $\square$

The construction is explicit and rational: every gluing is performed at the same $d = d_0$, so each intermediate composite is a closed certificate of side exactly $m - d_0$ (squares may touch), and a strict certificate of the final packing is obtained by scaling all centres and the side by $1 + \varepsilon_n$ with $\varepsilon_n = d_0/(2(n - d_0))$, giving side $n - d_0/2 < n$: a scaling by $\lambda > 1$ about the origin with the squares' sizes and orientations fixed turns every closed separation (a separating unit normal $u$ with $u\cdot(p_j - p_i) \ge r_i + r_j$) into a strict one by $(\lambda - 1)(r_i + r_j) > 0$, and every wall margin $\mu \ge 0$ into $\lambda\mu + (\lambda - 1) r_i > 0$, where $r_i$ is the half-width of square $i$ projected on the axis in question. We have carried this out for every $15 \le n \le 64$ (Section 5).

## 4. Further rectangles

Besides $R$ and $R'$ we hold squeezable packings with waste $b - 1$ in $(b+1) \times b$ for $b = 17, 18, 20, 21$: $290$ squares in $18 \times 17$ (squeeze $\ge 0.0024491$), $325$ in $19 \times 18$ ($\ge 0.0059513$), $401$ in $21 \times 20$ ($\ge 0.0117127$) and $442$ in $22 \times 21$ ($\ge 0.0027796$). They are not needed for Theorems 1–2 but improve the inventory of Section 5: Lemma 3 with two rectangles ($A$ of waste $b - 1$, $B$ of waste $b' - 1$; $c = b - 1 \le b + 1 - d$) gives $n^2 - n + 2$ squares directly at $n = b + b'$. A further rectangle with short side $b$ and waste $b - t$ adds the step "$+b$ to the side, $+t$ to the excess" to the induction; the explicit rate of Theorem 2 is $\max t/b$ over the rectangles one holds, $2/21$ here. With the zero-excess bases ($11 \le m \le 14$ from Proposition 5, and every $m \ge 15$ from Theorem 1 after deleting a square) a squeezable $(b+1) \times b$ rectangle of waste $b - 2$ gives $s(n^2 - n + 2) < n$ for all $n \ge b + 11$; a short side $b \le 13$ would cover the five open cases above, and $b = 19$ only $n = 30$.

## 5. Certificates generated for $15 \le n \le 64$

The *theorem inventory* of [Fan26] (`theorem/`) contains, for each $15 \le n \le 64$, the strict certificate produced by the proof of Theorem 2 with $(p, q)$ maximising $p + 2q$: a packing of $n^2 - n + F(n)$ unit squares in a square of side exactly $n - 1/20000$. Its values are $F(n) = 1$ for $15 \le n \le 30$, $2$ for $31$–$35$, $3$ for $36$–$51$, $4$ for $52$–$56$ and $5$ for $57$–$64$; counts run from $211$ at $n = 15$ to $4037$ at $n = 64$. A second table (`theorem/best.tsv`) ranges over a precisely defined larger inventory, every square packing obtainable from one base piece (a square piece, or two rectangle pieces glued) by a sequence of Lemma 3 steps at squeeze $d_0$ using the pieces listed in `theorem/PIECES.md` (which adds the $n^2 - n + 2$ squares of Section 3 and the rectangles of Section 4); its maximum excess exceeds $F(n)$ at $n = 18$–$23$, $28$, $29$ ($2$), $34$, $35$ ($3$), $39$–$44$, $49$–$51$ ($4$), $55$, $56$ ($5$) and $60$–$64$ ($6$). The second table maximises the excess over the stated construction family. Both tables give lower bounds on $J(n)$. All $76$ certificates pass the three checkers of Section 6.

## 6. Certificates, checking and search

**Format.** A certificate is a text file with header `N S` (square container) or `N W H` (rectangle) and one line `x y t` per square, all rationals: centre $(x, y)$ and rotation $\theta = 2\arctan t$, so $(\cos\theta, \sin\theta) = ((1 - t^2)/(1 + t^2),\ 2t/(1 + t^2))$ exactly and the four vertices are rational. *Strict validity*: every vertex lies in the open box, and for every pair of squares some edge normal of one of them strictly separates the projections of the two (separating axis theorem; for convex polygons this is equivalent to disjointness of the closed squares). *Closed validity* allows equality in both conditions and is exactly the packing definition.

**Checkers.** Three exact programs were used, all in rational arithmetic with Python's `fractions`. (i) `verify.py` in [Fan26] and the package verifier `squarepack.verify` behind it (separating axes over the four edge normals; pairs whose centres are more than $\sqrt 2$ apart are skipped by a lemma; the public `verify.py` reads square headers only, the package verifier also rectangles). (ii) `verify_cert.py` of Daniel [Dan26] (commit `29d0cee`), unchanged, independently written, same representation and test; on a rectangle certificate it reads only the first two header fields, so it checks the width only. (iii) `tools/rectcheck.py` in [Fan26], a short program importing nothing from the package, checking both dimensions of the box and every pair (with the same $\sqrt 2$ shortcut). Every square certificate cited here passes (i), (ii) and (iii); every rectangle certificate passes `squarepack.verify` and (iii) in both dimensions and (ii) as a width check; Ellsworth's decimal checker [Ell26b] accepts the direct square packings in his format. The two reviews of this draft re-verified all certificates with their own freshly written exact checkers (395 and 254 files respectively, no failures). The checkers use the same separating-axis test but have separate implementations. These computations are not an external human review.

**Formal verification.** Lean 4 (v4.33.1, Mathlib v4.33.1) formalises Theorem 1 and the maximum of the two unmixed bounds in Theorem 2, including kernel checks of all 18 input pieces; the mixed-step bound is proved here [FanL26]. With Daniel's formal specification of unit-square packings and $s(N)$ [Dan26, `Spec.lean`, vendored unchanged], the Lean statements are

```
theorem theorem1 (n : ℕ) (h : 15 ≤ n) : minSide (n ^ 2 - n + 1) < n
theorem main     (n : ℕ) (h : 15 ≤ n) : Packs (n ^ 2 - n + 1) ((n : ℝ) - 1 / 10000)
theorem theorem2 (n : ℕ) (h : 15 ≤ n) : minSide (n ^ 2 - n + jExc n) < n
```

where `jExc n = max ((n + 1) / 16) (1 + 2 * ((n - 15) / 21))` (the maximum of the two single-step bounds; the mixed-step form of Theorem 2 is not formalised). Lemma 3 is the Lean theorem `glue` (rectangular containers `PacksR`), and the $18$ certificates of Propositions 4 and 5 are checked by `decide +kernel` through an integer-encoded checker whose soundness (`check_sound`) is proved from Daniel's `packs_of_cert`; the $\sqrt 2$ far-pair shortcut is the lemma `disjoint_of_far`. `#print axioms` for all three theorems lists only `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`; there is no `sorry`, `native_decide` or added axiom. Kernel time for the certificates is about three CPU-minutes in total; the README of [FanL26] reports a clean build at 34 minutes of wall time on a loaded machine. Repository [FanL26], public, commit `947bdae`; the proof sources are those of commit `837eca1`.

**Rounding.** Displayed certificate side bounds are rounded up; certified squeezes and margins are rounded down.

**Search (summary; details in Appendix A).** The square packings and the rectangles were found by a penalty method with basin hopping from "two-wedge" seeds, the structure of [AMS21]'s packings: two groups of squares tilted by about $\mp 15$–$20°$ along the bottom and left walls in opposite corners, the rest axis-parallel. Float solutions were turned into certificates by rounding to rationals, scaling centres apart by the smallest factor clearing every overlap, taking the exact extent as the side, and adding $2^{-64}$ so all margins are positive. The searches are time-budgeted and not bit-reproducible; the certificates are checked independently of how they were found.

**Availability.** All author-produced certificates (the $17$ direct square packings, the $6$ rectangles, the theorem inventory and the best-excess inventory of Section 5), Daniel's exact certificates of the known packings used as pieces (MIT), the three checkers, a standard-library implementation of Lemma 3 (`tools/glue.py`, with the `--d` option for the uniform squeeze), the tables, `VERIFICATION.md` and a SHA-256 manifest are in [Fan26] at commit 078789a4cfd36f915f1bfbe76d742413d79b2965. De Winter's $211$ carries no licence and is fetched by script from his repository at a pinned commit and checked by hash; the certificates built on it in the earlier composite table are regenerated by the supplied recipe rather than shipped.

## 7. Remarks

1. The proof uses only the base certificates and $R$ and $R'$. A squeezable $(b+1) \times b$ rectangle of waste $b - 3$, or of waste $b - 2$ with $b \le 13$, would strengthen the bound. An analytic family could replace the finite search inputs.
2. We prove no optimality result. The five remaining cases of the excess-two question are unresolved by these constructions.

## Appendix A. Search details

For $343$ and $344$, we inserted one line through each wedge of Ellsworth's $307$ packing. For $381, 421$, Couzo's $342$ (eight lines per wedge at about $-18°/+15°$) grown the same way; for $463$–$651$ and the $n^2 - n + 2$ packings at $n \ge 21$, wedges built from parameters (eight lines, lengths $(5, k-11, k-10, k-10, k-10, k-11, k-14, 1)$ and $(k-12, k-11, k-10, k-10, k-10, k-10, k-12, 3)$, rows at pitch $\cos\theta$ offset by $\sin\theta$, starting angles $18.2°/15.3°$, except $n = 24$ at $20.5°/16°$); $382$ from Daniel's improved $343$. The waste-$(b-1)$ rectangles: a square two-wedge packing with one more square inserted into every row crossed by a vertical cut through the lower wedge, then both sides of the box shrunk by a common amount. $R'$: a rectangle seed built from parameters (eight-line wedges, lower rows one longer than the square schedule, $17.2°/14.3°$).

Penalty: sum of squared penetration depths (separating-axis overlap on the best edge normal, plus wall penetration), minimised by L-BFGS at fixed side; the side is shrunk while the penalty stays below $10^{-14}$; Gaussian kicks of the best state for a fixed budget of $10$–$60$ minutes on a $10$-core machine.

Best attempts at the open $n^2 - n + 2$ cases (sides above $n$ by): $24$: $3.7 \cdot 10^{-8}$; $25$: $1.2 \cdot 10^{-2}$; $26$: $7.5 \cdot 10^{-3}$; $27$: $1.9 \cdot 10^{-3}$; $30$: $3.5 \cdot 10^{-3}$. The wedge heuristic: a wedge of $w$ tilted lines at angle $\theta$ has projected width $w\cos\theta + \sin\theta$, so it saves space on that line only if $w(1 - \cos\theta) > \sin\theta$; this guided the seeds and is not part of any proof.
