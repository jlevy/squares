## The Wall-Aware Footprint

For every retained signed frame $r,Jr$ in a coarse class, use

$$Z_r=K_r\cap\bigl(m+[0,h]r+[0,h]Jr\bigr).$$

For nonempty $Z_r$, an oriented core centred at $z$ has support interval
$[r\cdot z-h,r\cdot z+h]$ along $r$. Intersecting these intervals over all allowed
centres gives

$$
\max_{z\in Z_r}r\cdot z-h\le r\cdot x
\le\min_{z\in Z_r}r\cdot z+h.
$$

The identical pair along $Jr$ defines $R_r$. This proves

$$
R_r=\bigcap_{z\in Z_r}(z+S_r),\qquad
A_{\mathrm{wall}}=\bigcap_{r:Z_r\ne\varnothing}R_r.
$$

All extrema occur at rational vertices.
The formula is exact for the declared finite owner-frame family.
It needs no 256-class split.
The actual selected owner is one of these frames with a centre in $Z_r$, so its core
contains $A_{\mathrm{wall}}$.

**The inclusion is always weak; strict enlargement is unmeasured.** Removing candidate
centres cannot shrink an intersection of their cores.
Without the container restriction, intersecting the centres in $m+[0,h]r+[0,h]Jr$ gives
that same anchored half-side square; intersecting the retained frames gives the old
endpoint footprint.
Thus $A_{\mathrm{old}}\subseteq A_{\mathrm{wall}}$. However, clipping
a corner of $Z_r$ may leave all four projection extrema unchanged, and an enlargement in
one frame may be removed by the intersection with another frame.
Area gain must be computed, not inferred from the existence of wall clipping.

Keep every retained signed owner frame, rather than the sparse residual screen.
Keep nonempty segments and points as possible-centre sets.
Using the closed container is conservative.
If all $Z_r$ are empty, record class impossibility; do not construct the intersection of
an empty family as an occupied region.
Every nonempty $R_r$ contains the owned mark, a useful invariant.

The independent union clipper intentionally discards zero-area intersections.
A constructor promising to retain degenerate $Z_r$ must use clipping that preserves
segments and points.
