Review candidate for version 2 of the 1..324 video.
Everything here is generated by `build_candidate.py` from the repository’s witnesses,
manifest, per-n renderings and composite record; nothing in the worktree was modified.

Correspondence rule through revision 4 (revision 5 replaces the assignment step with the
block-aware matching described under Revision 5; the prefix and shared-picture rules
stay): exact prefix for grid→grid pairs (the first n poses of n+1 are byte-identical to
n’s), the recorded removal index for the five shared-picture pairs (the manifest names
`source_n = n+1` with `n` in `listed_n`, and the geometry is checked to be n+1 with
exactly one square removed, order preserved), and otherwise a minimum-cost assignment
(`scipy.optimize.linear_sum_assignment`, scipy 1.17.1 is in the venv) on an n × (n+1)
matrix. The cost is the squared centre distance in coordinates normalised by the larger
of the two sides, plus `ANGLE_WEIGHT × (Δθ/45°)² / side²` with Δθ the shortest turn
modulo 90°. `ANGLE_WEIGHT = 1.0`, i.e. a full 45° turn costs the same as sliding one
square width; see the sweep below for why.

The page draws each square as a unit `<rect>` centred at the origin inside
`<g transform="translate(x y) rotate(deg)">`, in a world group flipped with
`scale(1 -1)` so the witnesses’ y-up coordinates are used as they are.
The container rect and the viewBox interpolate with the same ease-in-out curve as the
squares; the viewBox is always `side × (1 + 2 × 0.045)` centred on the container, so the
container holds a constant screen size and the squares shrink as it grows, as in the
atlas.
Angles interpolate by the shortest path modulo 90°, with an exact 45° tie resolved
counter-clockwise. The new square fades in over the last third of the move, scarlet-
outlined and with a scarlet-tinted fill, and the facts panel switches from n to n+1 from
the same instant; the details are under Revision 2.

Timeline per pair: dwell 1.0 s, move 1.4 s, settle 0.4 s (2.8 s); from revision 5 the
move is staged, the new square arriving over its first 30% and the blocks moving over
the rest. The clock is virtual; during play it advances only by `requestAnimationFrame`
timestamp deltas, capped at 0.1 s. There is no `Date.now` and no `Math.random` in the
page. `window.atlasTransitions` exposes `seek(seconds)`, `duration()`, `play()`,
`pause()`, `select(pairIndex)`, `setTiming({dwell, move, settle})`, plus `setColorRule`,
`setPhase`, `setOverlay`, `setCapture`, `setAutoAdvance`, `pairs()`, `state()`,
`sequenceDuration()`, `seekSequence(seconds)`, and from revision 2 `progress()` (the
bar’s position and the n it shows) and `schedule()` (the pair’s instants).
The smoke run confirmed that `seek` is idempotent (same DOM for the same instant after
seeking elsewhere and back) and that the clock stops when paused.

Review controls: pair selector, previous/next (arrow keys), play/pause (space), a
scrubber, colour-rule toggle (k), motion phase toggle (m: simultaneous, rotate first,
slide first), correspondence overlay (l), auto-advance through consecutive pairs, timing
inputs, and a capture-preview toggle (c) that hides the controls.
An `aria-live` region announces `n = …` whenever the displayed n changes.

Fonts: PT Serif 400, 400 italic and 700, and Source Sans 3 (variable weight) from
`vendor/kpress/src/kpress/format/static/fonts/`, and from revision 3 KaTeX_Main Regular
from `…/static/katex/fonts/` as the symbols face, embedded as base64 data URIs (205,013
bytes of the page, of which the symbols face is 35,030). The facts panel sets s(n) in PT
Serif with an italic s, the way the site’s math text face does; labels are Source Sans
3; `≤ ≥ √` are KaTeX_Main’s. A system stack would save that 205 KB but the page would
then look different on every machine, which defeats a review of visual quality.

### What changed, per request

1. **Scarlet means new.** The `--accent` token and the scarlet “proved optimal” line are

   (`#mark`) drawn above every square, so a neighbour’s stroke never covers it; to draw
   it in the dwell each pair carries `prev_new`, the previous pair’s arrival, known for

   (Revision 3 replaces the tildes with KaTeX_Main’s outline.)
   Glyph baselines come from the font: letters on the cap height (14.45 units), `=` and
   `?` on the middle of their own ink (14.45 and 14.53).

### Choices made

- The lower line goes under the exact line, not directly under the side line, so the
  closed form or degree note stays on the value it annotates; this is the slideshow’s
  order. For n = 101 the block reads side, `= 7 + (5⁄2)√2`, `s(101) ≥ 10.055385`, note.
- The lower line is 40 px, the exact line’s size, rather than the side line’s 54: an
  upper bound is what the picture shows and the lower bound is what is proved about it.
- The symbols face is added to the sans stack as well as the serif stack.
  Nothing in the panel’s sans text uses those code points today; it costs nothing and
  matches the slideshow.
- The build’s wall time on this host varied from 16 s to 49 s during the session against
  a constant 7.4 s of CPU, with the load average between 47 and 79 from other work; the

  load. The 7.5 s in the file table is CPU. The full test took 77 s of wall time for 18 s

- The font-source checks use CDP’s `CSS.getPlatformFontsForNode` on a page of their own,

  Measured: the page logs nothing on load or after `DOM.enable`, and that one line after

     rotation for them.

     blocks’ mean tilt difference, every source–target offset votes into a cell of 0.35

## Styles B and C: physics, bodies

Added on 2026-09-08: the physics spike’s style B, merged into this revision so that it
drives the identity pool, and a third style, C, after the owner asked for “the blocks as
a whole, each of identities, move according to being shaken about”, “with a little bit
of attraction to the right orientations”.
Style A (tween, the block tween of revision 5) is unchanged.
The choice is the Style select in the controls, the `p` key (tween → physics → bodies →
tween) and `setStyle('tween' | 'physics' | 'bodies')` on `window.atlasTransitions`;
`state().style` reports it, `styles()` lists them, `physics(index, style)` returns a
cached trajectory’s cost and final poses, and a second legend line under the panel names
the active style. The panel, badges, open group, progress bar, colour rules, title and
timing controls are shared and untouched, and no scarlet outline is drawn in any style.
The two physical styles ignore the motion mode (`m`): the panel’s roll follows the
mode’s `arrive` instant, which in the default staging is the move’s start, where B and C
make the new square appear.

### The model

One simulator serves both styles, and a style is what a body is.
In B every square is its own body, which is the physics spike’s model to the constant.
In C every matched block of the pair is one rigid body whose rigid members are fixed in
the body frame (their poses at n relative to the block’s centroid); riders, squares that
move alone and the new square are single-square bodies.
The trajectory is a deterministic fixed-timestep simulation, precomputed on the frame
that first needs it and cached per style, pair and move length (the 16 most recent);
`seek(t)` interpolates linearly between cached states, so a frame is a pure function of
t and identical in every run.
The step is 1/120 s of animation time, 168 steps at the default 1.4 s move; the
simulation’s clock is the move, so every constant is per move length and a longer move
plays the same trajectory more slowly.
Semi-implicit Euler, angles in radians and continuous inside.
Every square is drawn by the pool’s element for its identity, so a square keeps its
element across styles and pairs; the targets are the pair’s correspondence (square i of
n to `B.squares[map[i]]`, the new square identity n + 1 at its own pose).

- **Bodies.** Mass is the member count; inertia is the members’ own (1/6 each) plus
  their centre distances from the centroid squared.
  A body starts at its centroid with orientation 0.
- **Spring.** On the centroid toward the centroid of the members’ targets, and on the
  orientation toward the block’s turn as the record states it (within 60°, so the
  shortest arc for the block’s pattern); a single square’s angle target is the shortest
  arc modulo 90, a rider’s the turn its block takes plus its own correction, the way
  round that keeps the total smallest (`memberTurn`). Natural frequency 10 per move (k =
  100), damping ratio 0.85, the stiffness rising as a smoothstep over the first 25% of
  the move, as in B. Both springs act as accelerations, so a block arrives at a single
  square’s pace rather than √mass slower.
- **Push-apart and walls.** The separating-axis test on the four edge normals of each
  pair of squares, the push along the axis of least penetration (stiffness 2,500 per
  unit, not growing past 0.15, damping 20 on the closing speed) at the incident square’s
  deepest corner, and the walls at each corner outside the interpolating container
  (2,500 per unit, capped at 0.25), all exactly as in B; the grid broad phase of
  1.5-unit cells is the same.
  Each push is applied to the owning body as a force and a torque about its centroid,
  over the body’s mass and inertia.
  A single-square body takes B’s 15% of the torque; a block takes the whole of it.
  Two members of one body never push each other.
- **Jiggle.** Per body and axis, a sinusoid of 2.5–4 cycles per move at a random phase
  from the same LCG seeded by n, applied as an acceleration (the same for every body
  whatever its mass, as a shaken table gives), decaying as (1 − τ)^1.5. B’s amplitudes
  are the spike’s, 20 per move² and 12 rad per move²; C’s are twice that (`BODIES`), the
  shake being the point of the style.
- **Caps, lock-in, blend.** 40 units per move on a body’s speed, 20 rad per move on its
  spin. From 70% of the move the push-apart fades, gone at 88%; over the last 12% every
  square eases (smoothstep) onto its exact target.
  For a block’s member that is the residual its target keeps from the block’s rigid
  image (at most 0.35 on the record) blended in together with whatever the body’s spring
  has not yet closed, and the final cached state is the target itself.
  Measured on 100→101 for both styles: zero error against the frame data at a tolerance
  of 1e-6.
- **The new square.** Its own body from the start of the move, inflating in place from
  0.3 of its side to full over the first 15% while it fades in, tinted scarlet as in A,
  the tint fading over the settle.
  `PHYS.drop` stays at 0 for the reason the spike gave.
- **The view.** Held at n’s scale through the move (the container visibly grows, no
  square changes size), eased down to n + 1’s fit over the settle; where n + 1’s
  container would not fit at that scale the view grows just enough (the tiny pairs).
  Measured on 100→101 under both styles: 10.9 wide mid-move, 11.4837 at the end of the
  settle.

Four things the owner asked for after watching the styles, added on 2026-09-08 in the

### 1. Desaturate while moving, lock the colour in at the end

“When everything’s in transition, we make all the boxes somewhat less saturated and then
lock in the colors at the end.”
Every square’s fill keeps its OkLCh lightness and hue and loses half its chroma
(`DESAT`, 0.5) while the pair is in motion: the drain rises over the first 15% of the
move (`DESAT_IN`, smoothstep), holds through the motion, and eases back to nothing over
the settle, so the frame the pair rests on is exactly the retained packing’s colours.
Scaling the OkLab a and b by one factor is exactly scaling C, so the hue never drifts
and no second colour space enters the page.
It applies under all three styles, to both colour rules, and to every square but the
arriving one, which keeps the scarlet arrival (`TINT = 1.0`) and settles to its own
colour as before. `setDesaturate(bool)`, the “desaturate while moving” box and the `d`
key; on by default; `state().desaturate` reports it.

Measured, not asserted: `dump_fills.py` reads every visible square’s fill at three
resting instants (the dwell at 0.5 s, the start, the end of the settle) over 9 pairs × 3
styles × 2 colour rules — 162 instants, 20,754 fills — and all 20,754 are byte-identical
to the build before this revision.
Mid-move the mean sRGB chroma of `100 → 101` falls from 0.30 to 0.13 under every style.
A zero-length move (a static append under continuous play) never drains at all, which
falls out of the curve rather than needing a case.

### 2. Snap to best known, on or off

“I want to see whether the physics lands on the record by itself.”
A trajectory is now computed in one of three modes rather than one: `snap` is the
shipped behaviour (the last `PHYS.blend` of the move eases every square onto the record
and the final state *is* the record); `free` runs the same simulation to the end of the
move with no blend and no snap; `blind` is feature 3. The cache is keyed by mode as well
as style, pair and step count, so the two runs coexist.
Under `free` the push-apart no longer fades from `PHYS.lockIn`: that fade exists only
because the blend is about to take over, and with nothing to hand over to the contacts
stay firm to the last step.
With the snap off the pair also comes to rest where the physics left it — `seek` reads
the trajectory through the settle, not only while the squares are moving — so the frame
the viewer is left with is the physics’ own answer, not the record.
Style A is untouched: it is an interpolation, not a simulation, and the panel says so.
`setSnap(bool)`, the “snap to best known” box and the `s` key; on by default.

The panel’s third legend line, in the same muted grey as the other two, reports the
miss: the largest distance a square’s centre ends from the record’s, the largest angle
error (modulo 90°, since a square that lands a quarter turn round is the same square),
and the side of the smallest axis-aligned square box that holds the poses reached,
against the record’s side.

### 3. Blind mode: no cheating

“We could see if we can tune the physics settings so that it does match the best known,
or we could see if it doesn’t… if we didn’t sort of cheat and know where it needs to
be.” In blind mode the simulation is told nothing about the target poses.
It starts from the packing of n, centred in a container inflated to
`BLIND.inflate = 1.12` times the record’s side; the new square is dropped, upright, at
the emptiest place a coarse grid finds (`emptiestSpot`: candidate centres every
`BLIND.gridStep = 0.25` inset half a side from the walls, scored by the distance to the
nearest square’s centre, ties to the first cell in scan order); and then the same
contact, wall and jiggle physics runs with the target springs switched off entirely
(`ks = 0`, both position and angle) while the walls close in.
The container holds until `BLIND.hold = 0.2` of the move while the new square inflates,
then contracts toward the record’s side, reaching it at `BLIND.close = 0.9` if nothing
jams; the contraction clock only advances while the deepest overlap of two full-size
squares is within `BLIND.overlapTol = 0.08`, so “reached the record” and “could not
close any further” are one test on one variable.
The walls close on the packing rather than on the origin: every body is shifted by half
the change in side each step, which keeps the contents centred and squeezes from all
four sides. The picture opens into that box over the first `BLIND.open = 0.12` of the
move, easing from the record of n to the run’s own first state, so the dwell still shows
the record and nothing pops.
Deterministic: the jiggle draws from the existing LCG seeded by n and the placement is a
scan, so a pair blinds the same way in both pages and on every run; cached per pair,
style and inflation factor.
`setBlind(bool)` (the `b` key, the “blind, no target” box) and `setBlindInflate(factor)`
(the “inflate” box); off by default.

### 4. Continuous play, and the jump to n

“Like to be able to see any place as the whole movie for all 300 with a given set of
settings.” “Play all” and the `a` key run every pair from the current one to the last,
back to back, keeping the style, the colour rule, the motion mode, the snap, the blind
run and the desaturation; the progress bar was already a function of n and the clock, so
it advances 1 → 324 across the run without changing.
The beat is the sequence’s own rather than the controls’: `CONTINUOUS` is 0.8 s dwell,
1.2 s move, 0.3 s settle, and the 165 static appends (the 160 grid prefixes and the 5
shared pictures, where nothing moves) get 0.5 s of dwell and no move at all, which the
“full beat for static appends” box evens out.
A zero-length move is a real zero here — `schedule()` and every ramp already collapse
correctly — so a static pair costs no simulation, no roll and no blend, and the appended
square simply arrives at the cut.
`timing(pairIndex)` is the one place the beat is decided, and `duration`, `schedule`,
`physicsSteps` and `sequenceDuration` all read it, so nothing has two opinions about how
long a pair lasts. `playAll()`, `stopAll()`, `setContinuous({fullBeat, prefetch})` and
`continuous()` on the API; `goTo(n)` and the “jump to n” box select the pair starting at
n, clamping to the nearest pair the page carries.
In `index-all.html` that is all 323 pairs, 641 s at the default beat; in `index.html` it
is the 25 embedded ones, and the run steps over the gaps.

Two things pay for the boundary.
The next pair is simulated during this pair’s dwell (`maybePrefetch`, once per pair,
between 0.1 s in and the move’s start, only while playing continuously and only when the
next pair actually moves), and the correspondence lines — one `<line>` per square,
rebuilt at every boundary — are now built only when the overlay is on, `setOverlay`
rebuilding the pair when it is turned on.

`measure_stall.py` probes `requestAnimationFrame` over a 30-pair run of `index-all.html`
from n = 100 and classifies every frame delta by what happened across it: the pair
changed, the move began, or neither.
The headless shell runs at a median 8.3 ms frame.

| style | prefetch | worst frame at a pair change | at a move start | elsewhere |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| B, physics | on | 8.8 ms | 14.6 ms | 19.7 ms |
| C, bodies | on | 10.5 ms | 9.4 ms | 24.9 ms |
| B, physics | off | 17.1 ms | 36.1 ms | 28.3 ms |
| C, bodies | off | 9.3 ms | 25.2 ms | 33.4 ms |

So the worst boundary stall over 30 pairs is 8.8 ms under B and 10.5 ms under C — a
single frame period, which is to say no stall at all.
Without the prefetch the cost does not appear at the pair change but at the *move’s*
start, where the trajectory used to be built: 36.1 ms under B, three dropped frames.
With the prefetch that work moves into the dwell, which is what the “elsewhere” column
picks up: 19.7 and 24.9 ms while nothing on the stage is moving.

## Colour rule

Both rules are implemented; the default is **rule B, continuous angle**.

Rule A, house colours, reads each square’s fill from the atlas rendering (`data-square`
index) for both frames and cross-fades in OkLab during the move.
It is faithful to the poster, but the house assignment is class-based and re-ranked per
frame: an unpinned class takes its hue from its size rank, and a block whose tilt is 45°
in one frame and 44.9° in the next moves from the pinned citron to a ranked hue.
The 260→261 still (`frames/260-2.00s-house.png`) shows the effect: the central block
cross-fades from citron to salmon while rotating by a fraction of a degree.
Across a sequence this reads as flicker without a geometric cause.

Rule B makes fill a function of the tilt modulo 90° and the frame’s full-side contact
count. From revision 3 the hue is a symmetric sweep in OkLCh: with d = min(θ, 90° − θ)
the tilt’s distance from the grid, hue runs from teal (house slot 0, 174.6°) at d = 0 to
citron (house slot 1, 109.4°) at d = 45° along the short arc between them, 65° of hue,
so a 20° tilt and its mirror at 70° are the same green and no fill is ever red, orange
or blue.
(Revisions 1 and 2 went the long way round the hue circle from 45° to 90°, which
kept mirror tilts distinguishable but painted 58°–65° tilts salmon and orange beside the
scarlet mark; scarlet is reserved for the new square.)
Lightness and chroma follow the house’s own perceptual shade ramps (teal 0.50–0.70 at
chroma 0.080, citron 0.68–0.862 at chroma 0.150, four contacts darkest) interpolated
along the sweep, so at 0° and 45° the fills are exactly the house families and every
grid frame looks as it does in the atlas.
Chroma is clipped to the sRGB gamut by bisection.
The result: a square only changes colour when it turns, a turning square visibly sweeps
from teal through green to citron, and the tilt’s size is legible; its handedness is
not, which the symmetric sweep gives up on purpose.
The cost is that two distinct classes at nearby angles (say 20° and 22°) are no longer
distinguishable, which the house rule guarantees; for a video, where the eye tracks
motion rather than reading a legend, continuity wins.

## What the Hungarian matching gets wrong visually, and what would fix it

Written at revision 4. Revision 5 built the fix for items 1, 2, 4 and 6 (the block-aware
matching, the new-square rule and the shared block turn) and the staging of item 5; item
3 is now the opposite problem, close passes from rigid turns, tabulated under Revision
5\.

1. **It knows nothing about blocks.** The cost is per square, so a tilted block that
   turns back into grid rows is matched as many independent one-unit hops (the
   derived→grid collapses: 110→111 moves 12 squares more than a unit, 307→308 moves 23).
   The motion is legal but it reads as a shiver, not as a block turning.
   Fix: a two-level matching — cluster each frame by angle class and full-side-contact
   adjacency (the atlas already computes both), match clusters by centroid and size,
   then match squares within matched clusters.
   Blocks would then rotate rigidly about their centroid.
2. **Rotate in place versus slide to a same-angle slot is a free parameter.** The sweep
   shows the total rotation is essentially fixed by the tilt census, so the choice only
   moves a few squares, but those few are visible (4→5). A weight of 1 is defensible and
   explainable; a cluster-level matching would make it moot.
3. **Close passes.** Five pairs at weight 1 have two squares overlapping to a centre
   distance of about 0.62 during the move.
   A post-pass that swaps the two assignments of any crossing pair when the swap does
   not raise the cost by more than a threshold (2-opt on crossings) would remove these;
   alternatively, curved (quadratic Bézier) trajectories that bow away from the other
   square.
4. **The new square is whoever is left over.** For 100→101 it is a square of the tilted
   block, which is fine; but in general the unmatched square is decided by cost residue,
   not by narrative. A rule such as “the new square is the one farthest from any square
   of n” or “the one with the fewest full-side contacts” would be more consistent.
5. **Everything moves at once.** With simultaneous timing all squares start and stop
   together, so a chaotic pair is uniformly busy for 1.6 s. Staggering start times by
   displacement (far movers first, or a wave from the container’s lower-left) and giving
   rotation and translation different phases (the `rotate first` / `slide first` options
   are already in the page for judging this) both spread the motion out.
   The page’s `setPhase` already shows the two-stage variant.
6. **Rotation direction at exact ties** is counter-clockwise by rule (with the symmetric
   sweep the colour no longer depends on it), but a block whose squares are matched to
   targets at +44.9° and −45° (the same pose modulo 90) will have its members spin in
   opposite directions.
   Cluster-level matching, again, fixes it by giving the block one rotation.

## Capture pipeline

Recommended, all from what is already on the host: the repository’s pinned
`playwright==1.62.0` in `packing/.venv`, whose default launch is
`chromium_headless_shell-1234` (already in `~/Library/Caches/ms-playwright`), and
`/opt/homebrew/bin/ffmpeg` 7.1.1. Nothing was installed.
`smoke_capture.py --video N` is the prototype and it worked first time:

- Load `index-all.html` via `file://`, viewport 1920×1080, `device_scale_factor=1`, wait
  for `document.fonts.ready`, call `setCapture(true)`.
- For each pair: `select(i)`, then for k = 0..round(duration × fps): `seek(k / fps)`,
  `page.screenshot(type="png")`. Because the clock is virtual, frame k is rendered at
  exactly k/fps seconds regardless of screenshot latency.
- Measured: 74 ms per 1920×1080 PNG frame in the headless shell (100 frames of 100→101
  in 7.4 s); ffmpeg `libx264 -crf 18 -pix_fmt yuv420p` encoded them in 3.5 s to a 522 KB
  file (`frames/transition-100.mp4`, 100 frames, 3.33 s).
- The same frame from two independent browser launches was byte-identical (sha256
  `fdd2d01e11eaa355…`), so a receipt of the page’s sha256 plus the frame index is a
  sufficient determinism check, in the style of the atlas’s PNG receipts.
- The full run at 30 fps is 32,013 frames: about 40 min of capture at the measured rate,
  and roughly 19 min of encoding at the measured 35 ms per frame; 60 fps doubles both.
  Pairs are independent, so capture parallelises across browser contexts; encode per
  pair and join with ffmpeg’s concat demuxer, or stream all frames to one encode.
- Prefer capturing pair by pair (select + seek within the pair) over `seekSequence`,
  because the per-pair loop is what the receipts and any retry naturally key on.

## Revision 7: bar scale, annealing dial, live gap

Landed by a delegate that stalled before it could measure or document; the constants
below are read from the shipped `template.html` and the table was measured afterwards by
the coordinator with the pinned headless shell.

**The bar carries a scale.** `SCALE_MAJOR`, `SCALE_MINOR` and `SCALE_GAP` draw numbered
major ticks and unnumbered minor ticks across the whole 1 to 324 span, with the riding
`n` suppressing the numeral it would collide with.

**The annealing dial** is `setAnneal(level)`, 0 to 10, default 3, which is the previous
behaviour exactly. Three things scale together, so a higher level shakes harder, keeps
shaking longer, and is given more simulated time to do it in:

|  | level 0 | level 3 (default) | level 10 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| `amplitude` | 0 | 1.0 | 3.0 |
| `decayPower` | 1.5 | 1.5 | 0.35 |
| `span` (simulated time) | 1.0 | 1.0 | 1.7 |

The level is part of the trajectory cache key, and the jiggle stays the seeded
generator, so a level is reproducible.

**The live gap** is `#gap-a` and `#gap-b`, updated every frame: the worst centre
distance and worst angle error against the record, and the container side the current
arrangement would need against the record’s, with the excess.
A sparkline (`#gap-trace`, `#gap-head`) traces the gap over the move.
In blind mode only the side comparison is shown, because without a correspondence to the
record’s labelling the per-square errors mean nothing.

### Does shaking harder get closer?

Measured at the end of the move with the snap off, so the physics rests where it lands.

| pair | style | level | centres | angles | side | excess |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 100→101 | physics | 0 | 1.134 | 42.9° | 10.658 | +1.16% |
| 100→101 | physics | 3 | 1.149 | 43.0° | 10.619 | +0.79% |
| 100→101 | physics | 6 | 1.127 | 37.9° | 10.627 | +0.86% |
| 100→101 | physics | 10 | 1.235 | 19.9° | 10.624 | +0.84% |
| 100→101 | bodies | 0 | 1.129 | 28.5° | 10.602 | +0.63% |
| 100→101 | bodies | 10 | 1.917 | 42.2° | 10.705 | +1.60% |
| 110→111 | physics | 0 | 1.547 | 17.3° | 11.110 | +1.00% |
| 110→111 | physics | 10 | 1.487 | 7.6° | 11.026 | +0.24% |
| 110→111 | bodies | 10 | 1.530 | 21.0° | 11.209 | +1.90% |
| 272→273 | physics | 0 | 0.995 | 16.1° | 17.006 | +0.11% |
| 272→273 | physics | 10 | 1.032 | 13.5° | 17.026 | +0.22% |
| 272→273 | bodies | 10 | 1.315 | 24.5° | 17.510 | +3.07% |

**Annealing fixes orientations, not positions.** Under `physics` the worst angle error
falls steadily with the level, by more than half at 100→101 (42.9° to 19.9°) and by more
than half again at 110→111 (17.3° to 7.6°). The worst centre distance barely moves at
any level, staying near one unit throughout, and the container side is flat to within a
few tenths of a per cent.
The best result anywhere in the table is 110→111 at level 10, still 0.24 per cent above
the record.

**Under `bodies` a hard shake is actively harmful**: a rigid block shaken at level 10
loses its seating and the side degrades, worst at 272→273 where it reaches 3.07 per cent
above the record against 0.11 per cent for an unshaken per-square run.

This is what the annealing survey predicts.
The objective responds to the two to four squares that set the extremes and is nearly
flat in the rest, so shaking rearranges tilts freely while the container side hardly
notices. Getting the side down needs pressure on the walls or an inflate-and-contract
cycle, not a bigger jiggle.

`index.html` grew from 561,629 to 588,299 bytes and `index-all.html` / `workbench.html`
from 2,917,284 to 2,943,954.

### 1. One view, and the range as the spine

The owner is right that the two tabs were one operation: stepping one *n* and running a
range differ only in the span.
So the tab bar, the two `role="tabpanel"` panels and the roving tabindex are gone, and
the **range is the single spine**.

- **`from` and `to` are values of *n* stepped into**, which is the unit the chooser and
  the chips always used: 17 to 17 is the one step 16 → 17, and 2 to 324 is the whole
  corpus. That is a change of meaning for `setRange`, which in revision 8 took the *n* a
  step starts at (1 to 100 was the 99 steps arriving at 2..100). `setRange(1, 100)`
  still gives 99 steps, because it clamps to 2..100 — a coincidence worth knowing about
  rather than relying on.
- **Setting them equal is the old Single step tab.** A one-step range turns continuous
  play off, so the three timing boxes govern again; a wider range plays on the
  sequence’s beat. The page opens on **17 to 17**, and a one-step range snaps to a step
  the page actually carries, so a chip for 272 in the 25-pair demo collapses onto the
  nearest step it has rather than pointing at nothing.
- **The chips set both ends.** `whole corpus (2 → 324)` is the one click that widens it.
- **One transport.** `transport()` plays whatever the range is: one step on the
  controls’ beat, the range end to end on the sequence’s, resuming a paused range run
  where it stands and restarting one that has reached its end.
  The `Play the range`, `Play on from here` and `Stop` buttons are gone; `playRange`,
  `playAll` and `stopAll` stay on the API.
- **The bar’s scale spans `from - 1` through `to`**, so the corpus range is exactly the
  shipped 1..324 bar and a one-step range is its own two numerals with the fill sweeping
  the whole track. `check_revision7.py` and `check_legend.py` now set the corpus range
  before reading the scale.
- **Retired from the page, kept on the API**: `setTab` (a no-op returning `'single'`),
  `tab()` (always `'single'`), `state().tab` (always `'single'`), `goTo` (the
  jump-to-*n* box is gone), `setAutoAdvance` (a one-step range has nothing to advance
  into, so the checkbox was noise), and the pair `<select>` (323 options duplicating the
  *n* boxes).

The run duration readout prices one step at the controls’ beat and a range at the
sequence’s; the `step k of m` position readout is drawn only when the range spans more
than one step.

### 2. Nothing on the stage explains the stage

Removed: the legend line naming the scarlet convention, the line naming the active
style, the line narrating what a free or blind run was doing, the sentence under the
stage narrating the block matching (`#pair-info`), and the keyboard-hint line.
Kept: the numeral, the `n =` line, both side lines, the badges, the Open group with its
question-mark badges, the gap bar and the live numeric readouts.
`review/r9-capture-clean.png` is the proof — a capture frame at the loudest settings
(style C, no snap, annealing 10, mid motion) carrying the picture and its facts and not
one instruction.

Where the knowledge went, rather than being deleted:

- **The scarlet convention** — the arriving square is tinted scarlet as it appears and
  settles to its own colour — is revision 5, item 4, and the constants `TINT`,
  `MARK_FADE`, `MARK_WIDE` and `MARK_THIN` in the template.
- **What each style is** — A tween, B per-square physics, C rigid blocks shaken — is the
  “Styles B and C” section above, and the Style select still names them (`A · tween`,
  `B · physics`, `C · bodies`).
- **What a free or blind run reached** is `physics(index, style, mode).miss` on the API
  and the measured tables in revisions 6 and 7. The two live readout rows carry the same
  numbers frame by frame, which is what made the sentence redundant.
- **The block matching’s statistics** — how many blocks carry how many moving squares,
  the residual, which square is new and by what rule, the arrival overlaps — are
  `blocks()`, `blockOf()`, `newSquare()` and `pairs()` on the API, the record itself,
  and the revision-5 tables.
  The `kind-tag` at the top left of the stage still names the pair and its headline
  statistics, and it is chrome: capture preview hides it.
- **The keys** are unchanged and listed here: space play/pause, left/right step,
  home/end, `p` style, `k` colour rule, `m` motion, `d` desaturate, `s` snap, `b` blind,
  `a` play all, `l` correspondence, `c` capture preview.
  `t` is gone with the tabs.

`check_legend.py` was rewritten around this: it was the check that three legend lines
fitted, and it is now the check that they, and their wording, are gone — over 108
combinations of pair, style, mode, annealing level and instant, plus an open-ended run
from each of the three starts.

### 3. Transport glyphs

The play control is one button carrying two inline SVG glyphs, a right-pointing triangle
and two vertical bars, swapped by a class (`.transport.is-playing`) rather than by
rewriting the button’s contents; both are `fill: currentColor`, so they take the
button’s ink in either state.
The button has no text, so its accessible name is set from the script beside the class.
Previous and next are **&minus;** and **+**: they were solid triangles, which on a
transport row read as play.
Both carry an `aria-label`; every keyboard binding is unchanged.

### 4. The gap bar stops animating

The owner’s reason: the motion is already visible in the packing, so a second animated
readout of it is noise.
The hand now redraws only when the picture is not moving — through the dwell and from
the instant the settle ends — at a step boundary, whenever a setting that changes the
answer is touched (style, phase, colour, desaturation, snap, blind, inflation,
annealing, pause, seek, a drop), and on demand: `refreshGap()` on the API and an
`update gap bar` button.
The per-frame sparkline (`#gap-spark`, `#gap-trace`, `#gap-head`, `ensureTrace`) is gone
entirely, and with it the `sceneU` clock that only the trace’s head rode.
The bar’s static content is untouched: the proved lower bound, the record tick, the
shaded open span, the excess and the green check.

Measured, not asserted: over a 2.5 s move at n = 101 under style C with the snap off,
sampled every 60 ms, the hand takes **one** position across 34 mid-move samples while
the live readout below it takes 28 distinct values; the hand catches up at the settle.
`check_workbench.py` drives that.

### 5. Speed, 0.05x to 2x

`setSpeed(multiplier)` and `speed()`, a logarithmic slider
(`SPEED = {min: 0.05, max: 2}`) so the slow end has as much travel as the fast one, with
the multiplier printed beside it to two places — the value shown is the value in use,
because the setter rounds to two places.
It multiplies the wall-clock delta the animation clock is advanced by, and the simulated
seconds an open-ended run covers per frame, and nothing else: `seek(t)` is still a pure
function of its argument, so every instant renders identically and every capture is
unaffected. Measured: 1.0 s of wall clock advances the clock 0.992 s at 1x, 0.250 s at
0.25x and 1.986 s at 2x.

### 6. Initial conditions, and Optimize

`setInitial('previous' | 'random' | 'grid')` and `initial()`, as a three-way segmented
control:

- **previous packing** (the default) — the retained packing of *n* − 1 with the new
  square added. This *is* the timeline’s own start, so choosing it puts the step
  animation back on the stage.
  With the blind box off it keeps the record’s poses as spring targets and the walls at
  the record’s side; with blind on it is revision 6’s blind start (the packing centred
  in a box inflated by `BLIND.inflate`, the new square dropped upright at
  `emptiestSpot`) and the walls close.
- **random** — *n* squares at centres drawn uniformly from the page’s own LCG, seeded by
  *n* and the kind, inset half a diagonal from the walls so a square at any angle starts
  inside, and angles uniform on 0..90°, in a box `OPT.randomInflate = 1.25` times the
  record. Starting overlaps are expected and are what the run has to clear.
- **ordered fill from the bottom** — *n* squares axis-aligned in rows from the
  bottom-left, the trivial grid, in the grid’s own box of side `ceil(sqrt(n))`.

Choosing random or grid puts the arrangement on the stage at once, paused, because
neither has anything to do with the step animation; **Optimize** is what sets it going,
from whichever of the three is chosen.

**Optimize is an open-ended run.** It is a separate stepper (`optAdvance`), not a
generalisation of `simulate`, and the reason is deliberate: `simulate` is a closed
precompute whose output is cached, keyed, and byte-compared by four checkers, and
opening it up to continuation would have put every one of those measurements at risk for
no gain. The physics is the same physics — the same `PHYS` constants, the same
separating-axis push-apart at the incident corner, the same wall springs, the same
seeded jiggle — with one difference in the clock: the optimizer’s time unit is **one
second of simulated time** where `simulate`’s is one move, so `PHYS`’s per-move
constants read directly as per-second ones.
`optimizeAdvance(dt)` takes `round(dt x speed x 120)` fixed steps a frame, capped by an
EMA of the measured cost against `OPT.budgetMs = 9`, and state advances rather than
being re-read from a cache.

- **Bodies are one per square, always.** A block is a fact about one step’s
  correspondence, not about an arrangement being shaken open-endedly, and the random and
  grid starts have none.
  What the style select does reach is the shake: style C’s jiggle is twice style B’s, as
  in the step animation.
- **Springs iff there is a target**: the previous packing with blind off.
  Otherwise the walls close while the packing is clean (`pen <= OPT.squeezeTol = 0.004`,
  at `OPT.squeezeRate` 3% of the side a second) and ease back out while it is jammed
  (`pen > OPT.jamTol = 0.06`, at `OPT.relaxRate`), which is the inflate-and-contract
  cycle revision 7’s annealing survey said the side actually responds to.
  Revision 6’s blind run gated on 0.08 and its packings sat at 0.08 for exactly the
  reason a squeeze allowed up to a tolerance will use all of it; gating at 0.004 puts
  the equilibrium at a few thousandths instead.
- **The shake anneals to a floor** (`OPT.floor = 0.15`, `OPT.tau = 2 s`) rather than to
  nothing: the run never ends, so it must not go dead either.
- **It reports continuously**, on two rows of the panel: the start, the elapsed
  simulated time and the step count on one; the smallest box the run has held the
  squares in, **the deepest overlap that box carried**, and the record on the other.
  The overlap is printed beside the box on purpose.
  With soft contacts and a shake that never dies there is no instant at which the
  overlap is exactly nothing, so a box below the record is not a record and the two
  numbers together say so — at n = 100 the grid start reaches a box of 9.98 with an
  overlap of 0.005, and 0.005 across a row of ten squares is 0.045, more than the 0.018
  the box appears to have gained.
- **Play resumes, pause stops it where it stands.** Seeking is how the timeline is come
  back to, and changing *n* ends the run.

**The snap checkbox is gone from the page**, replaced by this: an open-ended run has no
target to snap to, and the honest readout is the live gap.
`setSnap` stays on the API and still governs the step animation, which is where a snap
means anything.

What it achieves, from `measure_optimize.py` (the tool, not a one-off), on the pinned
headless shell:

| n | start | steps/s at 2x | ms/step | start side | smallest box after 2400 steps | overlap there | record | excess |
| ---: | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 17 | previous | 241 | 0.003 | 4.676 | none clean | 0.097 | 4.676 | — |
| 17 | random | 240 | 0.009 | 5.550 | 4.987 | 0.006 | 4.676 | +6.7% |
| 17 | grid | 239 | 0.023 | 5.000 | 4.990 | 0.005 | 4.676 | +6.7% |
| 100 | previous | 241 | 0.107 | 10.000 | 10.005 | 0.003 | 10.000 | +0.05% |
| 100 | random | 240 | 0.062 | 12.242 | 11.869 | 0.005 | 10.000 | +18.7% |
| 100 | grid | 240 | 0.047 | 10.000 | 9.984 | 0.005 | 10.000 | −0.16% |

Two figures, because they answer different questions.
**Throughput** is 120 fixed steps per simulated second times the speed multiplier — 240
a second at 2x at both n = 17 and n = 100, and the frame budget never binds at 2x (0.107
ms a step at n = 100 leaves room for 84 steps a frame against the 4 the clock asks for).
**Progress** is deterministic in the step count and the same on any host.
The answer to “does it get anywhere” is: it closes a grid or random start by six or
seven per cent at n = 17 and stalls seven per cent above the record, which is the same
wall revision 6’s blind runs hit and for the same reason — the objective is nearly flat
away from the two or three squares that set the extremes.
n = 17 from the previous packing never has a clean moment at all: at annealing 3 the
record packing of 17 is not a fixed point of this physics, and it sits at a 0.097
overlap.

### 7. The hand

Press picks the topmost square under the cursor (drawing order is birth order, so the
last match is the one on top), drag moves it, shift turns it about its own centre,
release drops it. Client coordinates go through `#world`’s own screen matrix, so the y
flip, the viewBox and the stage’s CSS scale are all accounted for in one step; pointer
capture keeps a drag alive when the cursor leaves the square or the stage.

- **Pinned while held**: the run writes the cursor’s pose into the square every step,
  zeroes its velocity and gives it no force at all, so its neighbours are pushed aside
  by it rather than the other way round.
  Measured: with a square held 0.7 units into its neighbour at n = 17 from the grid
  start, 300 steps later the held square is exactly where the cursor left it and the
  neighbour is not where it was.
- **A drag needs a live simulation to push anything**, so grabbing a square while the
  timeline is playing or paused hands the picture to an open-ended run seeded from the
  poses on the stage — no jump, and if it was playing it keeps playing.
- **The readout follows at once**: `dragTo` re-measures and re-renders, so a square
  dragged out of the box takes the required side with it (5.000 to 8.000 in the check).
  The view is sized on the union of the walls and the squares, so a square held outside
  the box stays on the stage instead of vanishing off the edge of it.
- `pickAt(x, y)`, `grab(index, x, y)`, `dragTo(x, y, rotating)`, `release()` and
  `hand()` are on the API in world coordinates, which is what the pointer handlers turn
  client coordinates into — so a test drives a drag without a mouse.

**The determinism caveat.** An un-dragged run is deterministic in its step count: the
start is a seeded arrangement, the jiggle draws from the same LCG the cached simulator
uses, the step is fixed, and `optimizeStep(k)` reaches the same state every time.
It is *not* deterministic in wall time — how many steps a second buys depends on the
frame budget — and a **dragged** run is not reproducible from the seed at all, because
the owner’s hand is an input the seed does not carry.
So a touched run is marked `hand-edited` on the panel, beside the clock and the step
count, and the mark stays for the life of the run: a screenshot of a dragged run cannot
be mistaken for a clean one.
`seek(t)` is untouched by any of this and remains a pure function of its argument.

### What reads badly

- **The optimizer duplicates the collision code.** `optCollide` and `optSupport` are
  `simulate`’s separating-axis test written again for a live state rather than a
  precomputed one. That was the deliberate trade — see step 6 — but it is two copies of
  the most delicate arithmetic on the page, and a change to one has to be made to the
  other. A shared `collide(ctx, i, j)` taking a context object is the fix, and it should
  be made with the cached trajectories byte-compared either side.
- **The walls can end up inside the packing.** The squeeze closes the box while the
  deepest overlap is small, but the wall spring is capped, so squares poke a few
  hundredths outside their own container and `review/r9-optimize-running.png` shows a
  border cutting through a row.
  Revision 6 noted the same thing about the blind run.
  It reads as “this is not a packing”, which is true, but it is not deliberate.
- **A one-step range gives the progress bar a two-numeral scale**, and the riding *n*
  then prints the same value as the scale’s right end.
  Honest, but it looks like a duplicate.
- **Style C’s rigid blocks do not reach Optimize.** The style select changes the shake’s
  amplitude and nothing else there.
  For the previous-packing start the pair’s blocks do exist and could be bodies; for
  random and grid they do not exist at all, and after a few seconds of shaking they
  would not be the arrangement’s blocks anyway.
- **`best` is a box, not a packing**, and the readout has to carry the overlap beside it
  to say so. A better figure would be the smallest box the arrangement fits in after the
  overlap is genuinely resolved, which needs a projection step the simulator does not
  have.
- **The shaded open span still reads a little like a progress fill**, as revision 8
  recorded. The gap bar is otherwise unchanged.
- **Nothing anneals the optimizer’s own schedule.** `OPT.squeezeRate`, `relaxRate`,
  `squeezeTol`, `jamTol`, `tau` and `floor` are constants tuned by hand over about six
  measured runs; there is no sweep tool for them the way
  `experiment_block_matching.py --sweep` sweeps the matching.
