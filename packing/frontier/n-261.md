# `s(261)` — open

**Hiroaki Hosono’s closed form, 14 October 2026.** Hiroaki Hosono’s closed form
(wand125, 13 October 2026, T-180) states $s(N) \ge a(N)$ for every non-square
$N \ge 49$, from a measure of line segments on 34 intervals of the fractional part.
At $n = 261$, $k = 16$ and interval 5 give
$a(261) = 16.192480533769752717717472451742\ldots$, so the reported lower bound here is
now $s(261) \ge 16.192480533769$, rounded down, above a smaller count’s bound by
monotonicity, $16.16637581635$. The source replayed its certificate with its own
checker; no record of it has been replayed here, so the verified lower bound is
unchanged. The source states that its search, certificates, checkers and text were
produced with AI assistance (Claude and Codex), under the direction of and reviewed by
Hiroaki Hosono.

**House adoption, 15 October 2026.** PingYou Ltd’s exact re-solve of Robert Barnett’s
refinement of Ryan Xu’s packing (T-169) is now this case’s house: its exact certificate
proves $s(261) \le 16.6787798753429099$, the reported and the verified upper bound,
`2.98e-13` below the ceiling the case held until then, $16.6787798753432078$ from Ryan
Xu’s packing, improved by Robert Barnett (T-150), which stays retained as history
([the certificate](#pingyou-ltds-certificate-t-169)).

**House adoption, 11 October 2026.** Robert Barnett’s refinement of Ryan Xu’s packing
(T-150) was this case’s house until 15 October 2026: its exact certificate proves
$s(261) \le 16.6787798753432078$, the reported and the verified upper bound, `7.35e-11`
below the ceiling the case held until then, $16.6787798754166788$ from Ryan Xu’s packing
(T-125), which stays retained as history
([the certificate](#robert-barnetts-certificate-t-150)).

**Exact certificate, 2026-10-06.** Evan Daniel’s
[`square-packing`](../resources/web/evand-square-packing-2026-10-05/README.md) published
on 5 October 2026 an exact rational certificate of this packing, decided here over
$\mathbb{Q}$ by two exact checkers that share no code with each other or with the
source, and by the source’s own two run here: it previously proved
$s(261) \le 16.68292682926830$, the verified upper bound (T-101). Until then the
verified upper bound was the trivial grid bound $17$.

Previously, the packing gave $s(261) \le 16.68292683$, and the strongest lower bound
independently verified here is $16.160459$ from Karakuş’s general theorem, leaving a gap
of $0.5225$. General bound: s(N) >= 1/2 + sqrt(N - floor(sqrt(N)) + 1/4).

## The earlier verified ceiling

The verified ceiling at that intake was $16.68292682926830$, proved by Evan Daniel’s
exact rational certificate of this packing
(`E-evand-exact-ceilings-2026-10-05-exact-replay`, T-101). It was **larger** than the
then-reported $16.68292682926829$, by `1e-14`.

It is not the value of $s(261)$ and not a different packing: it is the certificate’s own
side, $16.6829268292682926831\ldots$, rounded up at the fourteen decimals the catalogue
prints. The catalogue gives the packing’s side exactly, as $16 + (28/41)$, and the
certificate’s side lies `1.7e-19` above that: its squares are rational and each is kept
clear of its neighbours and the walls, so it bounds the exact side from above and does
not reach it. That side is rational, so an exact certificate of the packing at that side
would reach it, a rational one where the packing’s exact point is rational.
That certificate gap was the upper-bound blocker at that intake.
Read `reported_upper_bound` for the best known side length.

## The exact certificate

Evan Daniel’s
[`square-packing`](../resources/web/evand-square-packing-2026-10-05/README.md) published
on 5 October 2026 exact rational certificates of the packings this register then listed
as best known, solved from its own witnesses, and offered those that do not lower a
printed side as a replay of the existing bounds, asking for nothing to be registered
from them. The certificate for this count holds the same 261 squares, each a rational
centre and a rational $t = \tan(\theta/2)$, in a square of side
$16.6829268292682926831\ldots$, `2.7e-15` above the side the Kingbird catalogue prints,
$16.68292682926829$. Square for square, its pose lies within `2.9e-4` of the binary64
pose the atlas then pictured for this count.
6 squares move by more than `1e-8`, all of them squares the source lists as carrying no
force; every other square’s centre rounds to the witness’s. Evan Daniel’s solver moves
the binary64 pose to a nearby exact KKT point of the problem of minimizing the side
under non-overlap, computed at 80 digits, and rounds it outward to rationals.

This repository decides the certificate exactly.
Converted without rounding, every pair and every wall is decided over $\mathbb{Q}$
twice, by `sqpack`’s exact separating-axis test and by an independent checker that
shares no code with it, and the source’s own two checkers, run here as retained, accept
it as well
([receipts](../resources/web/evand-square-packing-2026-10-05/README.md#replayed-here)).
That previously proved $s(261) \le 16.68292682926830$, the verified upper bound at that
intake: the certificate’s side rounded up at the fourteen decimals the catalogue prints,
as the record writes any certificate of a printed side.

## The lower bound

The strongest lower bound independently verified in this record is Karakuş’s general
bound, which applies to every nonsquare $N \ge 8$:

```
s(N) ≥ ½ + √(N − ⌊√N⌋ + ¼)
```

**Correction, 2 October 2026.** Until that date the verified lower bound here was
Nagamochi’s general closed form,
$s(N) \ge \min(\lceil\sqrt{N}\rceil, \sqrt{N - 2\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor + 1} + 1)$, which
is stronger at this $n$ and is now recorded as a reported bound.
Its published proof rests on Nagamochi’s Lemma 1, which Karakuş showed false; nothing is
disproved, and no packing beating it is known
([review of 2 October 2026](../../docs/project/reviews/review-2026-10-02-nagamochi-lemma1-karakus.md)).
This register had recorded that proof as verified, its own error, logged as defect
[D-516](../../defects.md).

Source-reported bounds are recorded separately from this independently verified theorem.
