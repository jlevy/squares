# `s(232)` — open

**House adoption, 11 October 2026.** Nate Chaoweeraprasit’s SQUISH packing (T-136) is
now this case’s house: its exact certificate proves $s(232) \le 15.7674283499418417$,
the reported and the verified upper bound, `1.07e-02` below the ceiling the case held
until then, $15.77817459305202$ from the catalogue’s packing (T-101), which stays
retained as history ([the certificate](#nate-chaoweeraprasits-certificate-t-136)).

**Exact certificate, 2026-10-06.** Evan Daniel’s
[`square-packing`](../resources/web/evand-square-packing-2026-10-05/README.md) published
on 5 October 2026 an exact rational certificate of this packing, decided here over
$\mathbb{Q}$ by two exact checkers that share no code with each other or with the
source, and by the source’s own two run here: it proved $s(232) \le 15.77817459305203$,
the verified upper bound until 11 October 2026 (T-101). Until then the verified upper
bound was the trivial grid bound $16$.

Open. Previously, the best known packing gave $s(232) \le 15.7781746$, and the strongest
lower bound independently verified here is $15.239402$ from Karakuş’s general theorem,
leaving a gap of $0.5388$. General bound: s(N) >= 1/2 + sqrt(N - floor(sqrt(N)) + 1/4).

## The earlier verified ceiling

Until 11 October 2026 the verified upper bound here was $15.77817459305203$, proved by
Evan Daniel’s exact rational certificate of this packing
(`E-evand-exact-ceilings-2026-10-05-exact-replay`, T-101). It was **larger** than the
catalogue’s printed side $15.77817459305202$, then the best known, by `1e-14`.

It was not the value of $s(232)$ and not a different packing: it is the certificate’s
own side, $15.7781745930520227686\ldots$, rounded up at the fourteen decimals the
catalogue prints.
The catalogue gives the packing’s side exactly, as `8 + (11/2)sqrt(2)`,
and the certificate’s side lies `1.6e-19` above that: its squares are rational and each
is kept clear of its neighbours and the walls, so it bounds the exact side from above
and does not reach it.
That side is irrational, so no rational certificate reaches it.
A `mathematics` blocker in the frontmatter recorded the difference until Nate
Chaoweeraprasit’s certificate (T-136) replaced both lanes.

## The exact certificate

Evan Daniel’s
[`square-packing`](../resources/web/evand-square-packing-2026-10-05/README.md) published
on 5 October 2026 exact rational certificates of the packings this register lists as
best known, solved from its own witnesses, and offered those that do not lower a printed
side as a replay of the existing bounds, asking for nothing to be registered from them.
The certificate for this count holds the same 232 squares, each a rational centre and a
rational $t = \tan(\theta/2)$, in a square of side $15.7781745930520227686\ldots$,
`2.8e-15` above the side the Kingbird catalogue prints, $15.77817459305202$. Square for
square, its pose lies within `1.5e-4` of the binary64 pose the atlas pictures for this
count. 4 squares move by more than `1e-8`, all of them squares the source lists as
carrying no force; every other square moves by at most `1.8e-15`. Evan Daniel’s solver
moves the binary64 pose to a nearby exact KKT point of the problem of minimizing the
side under non-overlap, computed at 80 digits, and rounds it outward to rationals.

This repository decides the certificate exactly.
Converted without rounding, every pair and every wall is decided over $\mathbb{Q}$
twice, by `sqpack`’s exact separating-axis test and by an independent checker that
shares no code with it, and the source’s own two checkers, run here as retained, accept
it as well
([receipts](../resources/web/evand-square-packing-2026-10-05/README.md#replayed-here)).
That proved $s(232) \le 15.77817459305203$, the verified upper bound until 11 October
2026: the certificate’s side rounded up at the fourteen decimals the catalogue prints,
as the record writes any certificate of a printed side.
It says nothing about optimality.
On jlevy/squares#375 its author wrote that the solver, its checkers and the batch “were
written with Claude (Anthropic) as a coding and research agent, directed and reviewed by
me.”

## The lower bound

The strongest lower bound independently verified in this record is Karakuş’s general
bound, which applies to every nonsquare $N \ge 8$:

```
s(N) ≥ ½ + √(N − ⌊√N⌋ + ¼)
```

**Correction, 2 October 2026.** Until that date the verified lower bound here was
Nagamochi’s general closed form,
$s(N) \ge \min(\lceil\sqrt{N}\rceil, \sqrt{N - 2\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor + 1} + 1)$, which
is stronger at this $n$ and is now recorded as a reported bound.
Its published proof rests on Nagamochi’s Lemma 1, which Karakuş showed false; nothing is
disproved, and no packing beating it is known
([review of 2 October 2026](../../docs/project/reviews/review-2026-10-02-nagamochi-lemma1-karakus.md)).
This register had recorded that proof as verified, its own error, logged as defect
[D-516](../../defects.md).

Source-reported bounds are recorded separately from this independently verified theorem.

## Nate Chaoweeraprasit’s certificate (T-136)

Nate Chaoweeraprasit’s SQUISH packing, reported on
[jlevy/squares#481](https://github.com/jlevy/squares/issues/481), at an exact rational
side, displayed upward as $15.7674283499418417$ (T-136). The certificate gives a
rational side and, for each of the 232 unit squares, a rational centre and the tangent
of its half-angle; the
[retained packet](../resources/web/squish-481-third-request-2026-10-09/README.md) holds
it with the complete native jobs that decided it.

Both maintained exact routes decided it here, `sqpack`’s exact separating-axis verifier
and the independent rational corner checker, with its duplicate-square and
outside-container controls, and a third exact route, half-extent containment and exact
intersection area, decided it again.
No route here is the source’s code, so the replay is independently re-implemented.
The
[review of 10 October 2026](../../docs/project/reviews/review-2026-10-10-upper-bound-imports-476-481-483-484.md)
and the
[final review of 11 October 2026](../../docs/project/reviews/review-2026-10-11-final-upper-bounds.md)
accepted the replay for finite feasibility.
That proves $s(232) \le 15.7674283499418417$, the verified upper bound: confirmed,
independently re-implemented.
It says nothing about optimality, rigidity or a local minimum.

The source states that SQUISH’s solver, its verification and the issue were written with
Claude Opus 5.5 under the author’s direction.

The house it replaces, $15.77817459305202$ from the catalogue’s packing (T-101), stays
retained with its own replay as history.
