# `s(297)` — open

**Hiroaki Hosono’s closed form, 14 October 2026.** Hiroaki Hosono’s closed form
(wand125, 13 October 2026, T-180) states $s(N) \ge a(N)$ for every non-square
$N \ge 49$, from a measure of line segments on 34 intervals of the fractional part.
At $n = 297$, $k = 17$ and interval 6 give
$a(297) = 17.287171021464262610446436903850\ldots$, so the reported lower bound here is
now $s(297) \ge 17.287171021464$, rounded down, above Nagamochi’s closed form,
$17.24807680927$. The source replayed its certificate with its own checker; no record of
it has been replayed here, so the verified lower bound is unchanged.
The source states that its search, certificates, checkers and text were produced with AI
assistance (Claude and Codex), under the direction of and reviewed by Hiroaki Hosono.

**House adoption, 14 November 2026.** Joost de Winter’s packing with a double step in
each staircase of Francisco Couzo’s layout, below Couzo’s packing as Evan Daniel and
PingYou Ltd improved it (T-299) is now this case’s house: its exact certificate proves
$s(297) \le 17.7394555582889518$, the reported and the verified upper bound, rounded
outward from its exact side.
The exact certificate side improves the previous verified exact side by about
`9.62e-04`. The earlier ceiling, displayed outward as $17.740417287543801851$ from
Francisco Couzo’s packing, improved by Evan Daniel and PingYou Ltd (T-174), applied
until this adoption and stays retained as history
([the certificate](#joost-de-winters-certificate-t-299)).

**House adoption, 17 October 2026.** PingYou Ltd’s refinement of Evan Daniel’s exact
optimum of Francisco Couzo’s packing (T-174) was this case’s house until 14 November
2026: its exact certificate proves $s(297) \le 17.740417287543801851$, the reported and
the verified upper bound, rounded outward from its exact side.
The exact certificate side improves the previous verified exact side by about
`1.77e-19`. The earlier ceiling, displayed outward as $17.74041728754380185115320576776$
from Francisco Couzo’s packing, improved by Evan Daniel (T-098), applied until this
adoption and stays retained as history
([the certificate](#pingyou-ltds-certificate-t-174)).

**Held for the owner, 15 October 2026; lifted 16 October 2026.** T-174 at
$17.7404172875438019$, `1.77e-19` below this case’s verified ceiling, is confirmed here
and accepted by the
[review of 14 October 2026](../../docs/project/reviews/review-2026-10-14-upper-bound-imports-574.md).
From 15 October 2026 until the owner’s house rule of 16 October 2026 lifted the hold, PR
#574’s houses were held for the owner, since their witnesses would add about 18.5 MB to
a checkout and their custody inputs about 5.8 MB to every worker’s snapshot.

Open. Previously, the best known published packing, Francisco Couzo’s at Evan Daniel’s
exact optimum (T-098), gives $s(297) \le 17.7404172875438018512\ldots$, and the
strongest lower bound independently verified here is $17.240669$ from Karakuş’s general
theorem, leaving a gap of $0.4997$. General bound: s(N) >= 1/2 + sqrt(N - floor(sqrt(N))
\+ 1/4).

## The exact optimum

Evan Daniel’s
[`square-packing`](../resources/web/evand-square-packing-2026-10-05/README.md) published
on 5 October 2026 an exact rational certificate of this packing at its exact optimum
(T-098): the same 297 squares in a square of side $17.7404172875438018512\ldots$,
`4.5e-12` below the side Francisco Couzo prints.
Square for square, its pose lies within `1.5e-4` of the binary64 pose the atlas pictures
for this count. 21 squares move by more than `1e-8`, 18 of them squares the source lists
as carrying no force and the other 3 by at most `3.8e-7`; every other square moves by at
most `2.2e-9`. The known-best witness this record lists is that binary64 pose, posed at
its finder’s larger side; the side above is witnessed by the certificate itself.
Evan Daniel’s solver moves the binary64 pose to a nearby exact KKT point of the problem
of minimizing the side under non-overlap, computed at 80 digits, and rounds it outward
to rationals, each square a rational centre and a rational $t = \tan(\theta/2)$.

This repository decides the certificate exactly.
Converted without rounding, every pair and every wall is decided over $\mathbb{Q}$
twice, by `sqpack`’s exact separating-axis test and by an independent checker that
shares no code with it, and the source’s own two checkers, run here as retained, accept
it as well
([receipts](../resources/web/evand-square-packing-2026-10-05/README.md#replayed-here)).
That proves $s(297) \le 17.7404172875438018512\ldots$, the verified upper bound until 17
October 2026; it says nothing about optimality.
The source reports the exact point as a KKT local minimum: multipliers that keep it in
equilibrium, a reduced Hessian positive definite once its exact flat motions are set
aside (its per-count report), and no first-order descent across corner-to-corner
contacts, each computed numerically at that point; none of that is verified here, and it
bears on this packing alone, not on $s(297)$. On jlevy/squares#375 its author wrote that
the solver, its checkers and the batch “were written with Claude (Anthropic) as a coding
and research agent, directed and reviewed by me.”

## The lower bound

The strongest lower bound independently verified in this record is Karakuş’s general
bound, which applies to every nonsquare $N \ge 8$:

```
s(N) ≥ ½ + √(N − ⌊√N⌋ + ¼)
```

**Correction, 2 October 2026.** Until that date the verified lower bound here was
Nagamochi’s general closed form,
$s(N) \ge \min(\lceil\sqrt{N}\rceil, \sqrt{N - 2\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor + 1} + 1)$, which
is stronger at this $n$ and is now recorded as a reported bound.
Its published proof rests on Nagamochi’s Lemma 1, which Karakuş showed false; nothing is
disproved, and no packing beating it is known
([review of 2 October 2026](../../docs/project/reviews/review-2026-10-02-nagamochi-lemma1-karakus.md)).
This register had recorded that proof as verified, its own error, logged as defect
[D-516](../../defects.md).

Source-reported bounds are recorded separately from this independently verified theorem.

## Joost de Winter’s certificate (T-299)

Joost de Winter’s packing with a double step in each staircase of Francisco Couzo’s
layout, below Couzo’s packing as Evan Daniel and PingYou Ltd improved it, reported on
[jlevy/squares#977](https://github.com/jlevy/squares/issues/977), at an exact rational

[retained packet](../resources/web/de-winter-s297-upper-bound-2026-11-14/README.md)

Both maintained exact routes decided it here, `sqpack`’s exact separating-axis verifier
and the independent rational corner checker, with its duplicate-square and
outside-container controls, and the 14 November review’s clean-room checker, frozen
before any producer or witness code was read, decided it and its Witness/v2 twin again
by separating axes in integers and by exact clipped areas in GMP rationals over every
square, wall and pair; its 19 controls, each stated beforehand, were each decided as
stated. No route here is the source’s code, so the replay is independently
re-implemented. The
[review of 14 November 2026](../../docs/project/reviews/review-2026-11-14-de-winter-977-979.md)
accepted the replay for finite feasibility.
That proves $s(297) \le 17.7394555582889518$, the verified upper bound: confirmed,
independently re-implemented, by a Claude agent, a system family that also produced the
certificate. It says nothing about optimality, rigidity or a local minimum.

The issue and the source’s README say the construction, optimization, high-precision
solution, rational certificate, checks and figures were produced with Claude Fable 5.2
(Anthropic) under Joost de Winter’s direction.

The issue says the packing keeps the layout of T-174’s packing, Francisco Couzo’s as
Evan Daniel and PingYou Ltd improved it: two blocks of axis-aligned squares with
staircase edges, two corner blocks of 10 and a diagonal band of 18 columns of tilted
squares. Two things are new: each staircase has two steps of double size, taken from the
staircases of his own pinwheels at 132 and 157, between which the band lies one step
closer to the upper left block, and the band’s columns change their lean once, where
T-174’s change three times.
Against T-174’s packing, this case’s house best packing before this adoption, it is a
new arrangement: no symmetry pairs nearest centres one to one, and the optimal
assignment moves 55 squares beyond a tenth, at most 0.700; its 110 tilted squares lean
61 one way and 49 the other.
So Joost de Winter is the packing’s finder, in Couzo’s layout, below the record it
improves, as the issue asks.

The house it replaces, $17.740417287543801851$ from Francisco Couzo’s packing, improved
by Evan Daniel and PingYou Ltd (T-174), stays retained with its own replay as history.
