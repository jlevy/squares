# `s(21)` — open

Open. The best known packing gives `s(21) ≤ 5`. The verified lower bound is
`s(21) ≥ 24/5 = 4.8`, from this repository’s weighted fractional unavoidable-set
certificate at that side (`T-020`, 2026-09-04, `V4/C4`), leaving a gap of `0.2`. It
improves the previously verified Nagamochi bound `1 + √14 ≈ 4.741657` by `0.058343`.
Friedman’s DS7 survey had already reported `≈ 4.7438` by 2000, without an exact form,
figure, or proof in the retained source; against that strongest located report, the
improvement is approximately `0.0562`.

## The packing

The retained catalogue records the trivial `⌈√21⌉ = 5` grid and no pictured
improvement. That is a catalogue report, not a completeness proof.

## The lower bound

The operative bound is a weighted fractional unavoidable-set certificate at side `24/5`:
2260 rationally weighted atoms on a `D4`-symmetric site set, total mass
`946131/50000 < 21`, every closed `B`-square at every net direction capturing mass at
least one, the least being `50007/50000`. It is decided from its own bytes by an exact
event-cell sweep and by an interval branch and bound over centre boxes, which agree on
that least value to the digit.
The certificate was generated for `n = 20`, and it carries this case with no
monotonicity step: of the five conditions only **Condition 2**, total mass below `n`,
mentions `n`, so an atom set
certifies its side for every integer strictly above its own mass, which is 19 and
upward. Before T-020, Nagamochi supplied the strongest verified bound here, while DS7’s
slightly larger four-decimal value remained a report without a located proof. Nagamochi’s
closed form applies to every `N ≥ 4`:

```
s(N) ≥ min{ ⌈√N⌉,  √(N − 2⌊√N⌋ + 1) + 1 }
```

In the current verified register, Nagamochi supplies the lower bound for 58 of the 65
open cases at `n ≤ 100`.

## What the method can still add here

A certificate for `n` cannot exist above `⌈√n⌉·B`, which at `B = 9977/10000` is
`4.9885`, so a single certificate on this fixed 181-direction net cannot go farther.
That is a necessary ceiling, not evidence that `4.9885` is attainable. Unlike `n = 19`,
no known packing binds first: the retained catalogue gives the trivial grid at `5`.
Refining the direction net changes `B`, however, so this finite-net ceiling does not rule
out a proved family of certificates and a limiting argument at `5`.
