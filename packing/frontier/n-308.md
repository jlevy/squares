# `s(308)` — open

**House adoption, 11 October 2026.** Evan Daniel’s move of Kevin Fang’s two-wedge
construction (T-141) is now this case’s house: its exact certificate proves
$s(308) \le 17.9982698795262559$, the reported and the verified upper bound, `1.73e-03`
below the ceiling the case held until then, $18.0$ from the trivial grid, which stays
retained as history ([the certificate](#evan-daniels-certificate-t-141)).

Open. Previously, the best known packing gave $s(308) \le 18$, and the strongest lower
bound independently verified here is $17.566048$ from Karakuş’s general theorem, leaving
a gap of $0.434$. General bound: s(N) >= 1/2 + sqrt(N - floor(sqrt(N)) + 1/4).

## The packing

The record catalogue does not picture $n = 308$: until October 2026 no arrangement was
known that beats the trivial $\lceil\sqrt{308}\rceil = 18$ grid, and the catalogue’s
conjecture that the grid is optimal here was a statement about what had been searched,
not a proof. Kevin Fang’s two-wedge construction (T-135, issue 484) beat it first, and
Evan Daniel’s move of that construction (T-141, issue 489) is smaller again; the grid
stays retained as history.

## The lower bound

The strongest lower bound independently verified in this record is Karakuş’s general
bound, which applies to every nonsquare $N \ge 8$:

```
s(N) ≥ ½ + √(N − ⌊√N⌋ + ¼)
```

**Correction, 2 October 2026.** Until that date the verified lower bound here was
Nagamochi’s general closed form,
$s(N) \ge \min(\lceil\sqrt{N}\rceil, \sqrt{N - 2\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor + 1} + 1)$, which
is stronger at this $n$ and is now recorded as a reported bound.
Its published proof rests on Nagamochi’s Lemma 1, which Karakuş showed false; nothing is
disproved, and no packing beating it is known
([review of 2 October 2026](../../docs/project/reviews/review-2026-10-02-nagamochi-lemma1-karakus.md)).
This register had recorded that proof as verified, its own error, logged as defect
[D-516](../../defects.md).

Source-reported bounds are recorded separately from this independently verified theorem.

## Evan Daniel’s certificate (T-141)

Evan Daniel’s move of Kevin Fang’s two-wedge construction, reported on
[jlevy/squares#489](https://github.com/jlevy/squares/issues/489), at exact side
$17998269879526255875387992744507/1000000000000000000000000000000$, displayed upward as
$17.9982698795262559$ (T-141). The certificate gives a rational side and, for each of
the 308 unit squares, a rational centre and the tangent of its half-angle; the
[retained packet](../resources/web/evand-record-hunt3-2026-10-10/README.md) holds it
with the complete native jobs that decided it.

Both maintained exact routes decided it here, `sqpack`’s exact separating-axis verifier
and the independent rational corner checker, with its duplicate-square and
outside-container controls, and a third exact route, half-extent containment and exact
intersection area, decided it again.
No route here is the source’s code, so the replay is independently re-implemented.
The
[review of 10 October 2026](../../docs/project/reviews/review-2026-10-10-upper-bound-imports-488-489.md)
and the
[final review of 11 October 2026](../../docs/project/reviews/review-2026-10-11-final-upper-bounds.md)
accepted the replay for finite feasibility.
That proves $s(308) \le 17.9982698795262559$, the verified upper bound: confirmed,
independently re-implemented.
It says nothing about optimality, rigidity or a local minimum.

The author says the search code and the request were written with Claude under his
direction.

The house it replaces, $18.0$ from the trivial grid, stays retained with its own replay
as history. The other certificates confirmed here at this count, T-135 at
$17.9993098551627480$, stay verified and are not selected.
