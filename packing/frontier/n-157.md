# `s(157)` — open

**House adoption, 11 November 2026.** Joost de Winter’s pinwheel grown from Samanyu-dev’s n = 109 packing, the first below the 13 × 13 grid (T-293) is now this case’s house: its exact certificate proves $s(157) \le 12.9933026113432237$, the reported and the verified upper bound, rounded outward from its exact side. The exact certificate side improves the previous verified exact side by about `6.70e-03`. The earlier ceiling, displayed outward as $13.0$ from the trivial grid, applied until this adoption and stays retained as history ([the certificate](#joost-de-winters-certificate-t-293)).

**Hiroaki Hosono’s closed form, 14 October 2026.** Hiroaki Hosono’s closed form
(wand125, 13 October 2026, T-180) states $s(N) \ge a(N)$ for every non-square
$N \ge 49$, from a measure of line segments on 34 intervals of the fractional part.
At $n = 157$, $k = 12$ and interval 29 give
$a(157) = 12.625379072825314661411226868861\ldots$, so the reported lower bound here is
now $s(157) \ge 12.625379072825$, rounded down, above Nagamochi’s closed form,
$12.57583690279$. The source replayed its certificate with its own checker; no record of
it has been replayed here, so the verified lower bound is unchanged.
The source states that its search, certificates, checkers and text were produced with AI
assistance (Claude and Codex), under the direction of and reviewed by Hiroaki Hosono.

Open. Joost de Winter’s packing (T-293) gives $s(157) \le 12.9933026113432237$, and the
strongest lower bound independently verified here is $12.551970$ from Karakuş’s general
theorem, leaving a gap of $0.4413$. Until 11 November 2026 the trivial grid gave the
ceiling, $s(157) \le 13$. General bound: s(N) >= 1/2 + sqrt(N - floor(sqrt(N)) + 1/4).

## The packing

The record catalogue does not picture $n = 157$. Until 11 November 2026 this case’s house
was the trivial $\lceil\sqrt{157}\rceil = 13$ grid, and the integer side was the
conjectured optimum. The house is now Joost de Winter’s pinwheel, the first packing of 157
squares below the grid on record, whose witness the atlas draws
([the certificate](#joost-de-winters-certificate-t-293)). Since
$157 = 13^2 - 13 + 1$, it shows $s(k^2 - k + 1) < k$ at $k = 13$; Kevin Fang’s Theorem 1
(T-163) gives it for every $k \ge 15$, and Evan Daniel’s packing of 183 (T-220) at
$k = 14$.

## The lower bound

The strongest lower bound independently verified in this record is Karakuş’s general
bound, which applies to every nonsquare $N \ge 8$:

```
s(N) ≥ ½ + √(N − ⌊√N⌋ + ¼)
```

**Correction, 2 October 2026.** Until that date the verified lower bound here was
Nagamochi’s general closed form,
$s(N) \ge \min(\lceil\sqrt{N}\rceil, \sqrt{N - 2\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor + 1} + 1)$, which
is stronger at this $n$ and is now recorded as a reported bound.
Its published proof rests on Nagamochi’s Lemma 1, which Karakuş showed false; nothing is
disproved, and no packing beating it is known
([review of 2 October 2026](../../docs/project/reviews/review-2026-10-02-nagamochi-lemma1-karakus.md)).
This register had recorded that proof as verified, its own error, logged as defect
[D-516](../../defects.md).

Source-reported bounds are recorded separately from this independently verified theorem.

## Joost de Winter’s certificate (T-293)

Joost de Winter’s pinwheel grown from Samanyu-dev’s n = 109 packing, the first below the 13 × 13 grid, reported on [jlevy/squares#958](https://github.com/jlevy/squares/issues/958), at an exact rational side, displayed upward as $12.9933026113432237$ (T-293). The certificate gives a rational side and, for each of the 157 unit squares, a rational centre and the cosine and sine of its rotation; the [retained packet](../resources/web/de-winter-s157-upper-bound-2026-11-11/README.md) holds it with the complete native jobs that decided it.

Both maintained exact routes decided it here, `sqpack`’s exact separating-axis verifier and the independent rational corner checker, with its duplicate-square and outside-container controls, and the 11 November review’s clean-room checker, frozen before any producer or witness code was read, decided it and its JSON twin again by separating axes in rationals and by exact clipped areas in GMP rationals over every square, wall and pair; its 16 controls, each stated beforehand, were each decided as stated. No route here is the source’s code, so the replay is independently re-implemented. The [review of 11 November 2026](../../docs/project/reviews/review-2026-11-11-dewinter-132-157.md) accepted the replay for finite feasibility. That proves $s(157) \le 12.9933026113432237$, the verified upper bound: confirmed, independently re-implemented, by a Claude agent, a system family that also produced the certificate. It says nothing about optimality, rigidity or a local minimum.

The issue and the source’s README say the derivation, optimization, high-precision solution, rational certificate, checking and figure were produced with Claude Fable 5.2 (Anthropic) under Joost de Winter’s direction.

The issue says the packing is a pinwheel grown from the public 109-square packing of Samanyu-dev’s squares_in_squares by two cut-and-fill steps, the first giving his packing of 132 (T-292); credit for the pinwheel at 109 belongs to Samanyu-dev, and his own contribution is the growth steps and the optimization. The 11 November review measured the second step: under one symmetry the 132, cut at about x = 8.33 and y = 8.32 and moved apart, pairs each of its squares with one here within 0.126, the other 25 being 17 axis-aligned squares at the cuts and 8 tilted ones. It is the first packing of 157 squares below the 13 × 13 grid on record: no symmetry pairs it one to one with the grid’s squares, the optimal assignment moving 100 beyond a tenth, and its 61 tilted squares turn 24.0 to 27.5 degrees. So Joost de Winter is the packing’s finder, after Samanyu-dev’s pinwheel at 109.

The house it replaces, $13.0$ from the trivial grid, stays retained with its own replay as history.
