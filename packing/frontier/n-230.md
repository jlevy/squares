# `s(230)` — open

**Exact regularized certificate, 2026-10-12.** This project’s regularization pass, Evan
Daniel’s exact solver and regularizer run here on this register’s witness, produced an
exact rational packing of this arrangement, decided here over $\mathbb{Q}$ by two exact
checkers: it proves $s(230) \le 15.682926829268292683083658536586$, a bound Evan
Daniel’s exact certificate of this packing already proves at the same side (T-101). The
verified upper bound is now that side exactly, resting on both certificates (T-101,
T-155), and the record still prints it as $15.68292682926830$. Until then the verified
upper bound was exactly $15.68292682926830$, the side of Evan Daniel’s exact certificate
of this packing rounded up at the printed precision (T-101).

**Hiroaki Hosono’s closed form, 14 October 2026.** Hiroaki Hosono’s closed form
(wand125, 13 October 2026, T-180) states $s(N) \ge a(N)$ for every non-square
$N \ge 49$, from a measure of line segments on 34 intervals of the fractional part.
At $n = 230$, $k = 15$ and interval 6 give
$a(230) = 15.212985634623918034318218070316\ldots$, so the reported lower bound here is
now $s(230) \ge 15.212985634623$, rounded down, above Nagamochi’s closed form,
$15.17744687875$. The source replayed its certificate with its own checker; no record of
it has been replayed here, so the verified lower bound is unchanged.
The source states that its search, certificates, checkers and text were produced with AI
assistance (Claude and Codex), under the direction of and reviewed by Hiroaki Hosono.

**Exact certificate, 2026-10-06.** Evan Daniel’s
[`square-packing`](../resources/web/evand-square-packing-2026-10-05/README.md) published
on 5 October 2026 an exact rational certificate of this packing, decided here over
$\mathbb{Q}$ by two exact checkers that share no code with each other or with the
source, and by the source’s own two run here: it proves $s(230) \le 15.68292682926830$,
the verified upper bound from 6 to 12 October 2026 (T-101). Until then the verified
upper bound was the trivial grid bound $16$.

Open. The best known packing gives $s(230) \le 15.68292683$, and the strongest lower
bound independently verified here is $15.171400$ from Karakuş’s general theorem, leaving
a gap of $0.5115$. General bound: s(N) >= 1/2 + sqrt(N - floor(sqrt(N)) + 1/4).

## The verified upper bound is a ceiling

`verified_upper_bound` for this case is $15.682926829268292683083658536586$, exactly,
proved by Evan Daniel’s exact certificate of this packing
(`E-evand-exact-ceilings-2026-10-05-exact-replay`, T-101) and by the exact regularized
certificate of it (`E-regularized-bounds-2026-10-12-exact-decision`, T-155), and the
record prints it as $15.68292682926830$. As printed it is **larger** than the best known
$15.68292682926829$ two fields above it, by `1e-14`.

It is not the value of $s(230)$ and not a different packing: it is the certificate’s own
exact side. The catalogue gives the packing’s side exactly, as $15 + (28/41)$, and the
certificate’s side lies `1.6e-19` above that, so it bounds the exact side from above and
does not reach it. That side is rational, so an exact certificate of the packing at that
side would reach it.
The `mathematics` blocker in the frontmatter records the difference.
Read `reported_upper_bound` for the best known side length.

This project’s regularization pass of 11 and 12 October 2026 ran Evan Daniel’s exact
solver and regularizer
([`evand/square-packing`](https://github.com/evand/square-packing), MIT, vendored here)
on this register’s witness for this count, with the `conservative` angle policy and
`coincide` placement, which wrote its certificate at the side the solver reached, the
solved point scaled by $1 + 10^{-20}$ and the side rounded up.
The result is an exact rational packing of the same 230 squares in a square of side
$15.682926829268292683083658536586$, `7.3e-15` below the verified upper bound the record
held before, which was that same side rounded up at the printed precision: it is the
side of Evan Daniel’s certificate of this packing (T-101). Measured against the witness
under the box’s eight symmetries, its squares pair one to one with the witness’s; 32
move by more than `1e-3`, the largest by `3.5e-2`, and none turns by more than `1e-6` as
a sine. It redraws the packing visibly, and is drawn only once the regularized layer
admits it under the regularization policy.

Each pair and each wall is decided over $\mathbb{Q}$ twice, by `sqpack`’s exact
separating-axis test and by an independent checker that shares no code with it, and both
accept it; both refuse it with its tightest pair pushed into overlap and with its box
shrunk past its least clearance ([certificate](../cases/regularized_bounds/README.md)).

with the producer’s code.
That proves $s(230) \le 15.682926829268292683083658536586$. Evan Daniel’s certificate of
this packing already proves the same bound at the same side (T-101), and its replay
here, by two checkers that share no code with each other or with the source, is

certificates (T-101, T-155) and prints it as $15.68292682926830$, the decimal it printed

## The lower bound

The strongest lower bound independently verified in this record is Karakuş’s general
bound, which applies to every nonsquare $N \ge 8$:

```
s(N) ≥ ½ + √(N − ⌊√N⌋ + ¼)
```

**Correction, 2 October 2026.** Until that date the verified lower bound here was
Nagamochi’s general closed form,
$s(N) \ge \min(\lceil\sqrt{N}\rceil, \sqrt{N - 2\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor + 1} + 1)$, which
is stronger at this $n$ and is now recorded as a reported bound.
Its published proof rests on Nagamochi’s Lemma 1, which Karakuş showed false; nothing is
disproved, and no packing beating it is known
([review of 2 October 2026](../../docs/project/reviews/review-2026-10-02-nagamochi-lemma1-karakus.md)).
This register had recorded that proof as verified, its own error, logged as defect
[D-516](../../defects.md).

Source-reported bounds are recorded separately from this independently verified theorem.
