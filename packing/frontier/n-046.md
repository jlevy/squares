# `s(46)` — solved

$s(46) = 7$, proved by **Wolfram Bentz** (2010) in the same paper as $s(13) = 4$.

## The $m^2 - 3$ family

$46 = 7^2 - 3$. Until September 2026 the family $s(m^2 - 3) = m$ was known exactly only
at $m = 3, 4, 5, 6, 7$: $s(6) = 3$, $s(13) = 4$, $s(22) = 5$, $s(33) = 6$, and this
case, which was then the largest $n$ known exactly other than through the two general
families (perfect squares, and Nagamochi’s $m^2 - 1$, $m^2 - 2$). **Corrected 2 October
2026:** Nagamochi’s proof of both families is incomplete, his Lemma 1 being false;
$m^2 - 1$ is proved again by Karakuş (T-084) and $m^2 - 2$ by chelokot’s Lean proof,
replayed here (T-086;
[review](../../docs/project/reviews/review-2026-10-02-nagamochi-lemma1-karakus.md)).
This register had recorded that proof as verified, its own error, logged as defect
[D-516](../../defects.md).

The family is no longer a conjecture.
Evan Daniel proved $s(k^2 - 3) = k$ for every integer $k \ge 6$ (T-064), which with the
five cases above gives $s(m^2 - 3) = m$ for every $m \ge 3$.

## A second proof, replayed here

$s(46) = 7$ is the case $k = 7$ of Daniel’s theorem, and its finite statement, `Valid7`,
is a statement about this case’s own container: every closed unit square in $[0,7]^2$
has mass at least one under a measure of total mass below $46$. Daniel’s exact checker
of `Valid7` was replayed here in full on the retained cover on 2 and 3 October 2026,
every root’s leaves equal to the published run’s
([`E-k2m3-evand-valid7-qx2-replay`](evidence.yaml)), and his Lean reduction from it to
every $k \ge 6$ was built here with the pinned toolchain and depends only on the
standard axioms ([`E-k2m3-evand-bentz-lean-build`](evidence.yaml)). The verified lower
bound cites both beside Bentz’s proof since 11 October 2026; until then it cited the
proof and this repository’s audit of its Theorem 8 alone.
The source’s `CREDITS.md` says the work was produced by Claude (Anthropic) in a single
session under human direction.
