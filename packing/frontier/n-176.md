# `s(176)` — open

**Hiroaki Hosono’s closed form, 14 October 2026.** Hiroaki Hosono’s closed form
(wand125, 13 October 2026, T-180) states $s(N) \ge a(N)$ for every non-square
$N \ge 49$, from a measure of line segments on 34 intervals of the fractional part.
At $n = 176$, $k = 13$ and interval 6 give
$a(176) = 13.348684012099631101477510083689\ldots$, so the reported lower bound here is
now $s(176) \ge 13.348684012099$, rounded down, above Nagamochi’s closed form,
$13.28820572744$. The source replayed its certificate with its own checker; no record of
it has been replayed here, so the verified lower bound is unchanged.
The source states that its search, certificates, checkers and text were produced with AI
assistance (Claude and Codex), under the direction of and reviewed by Hiroaki Hosono.

**House adoption, 15 October 2026.** Francisco Couzo’s exact re-solve of his issue-561
packing, which moves Daniil Ostashev’s n = 176 arrangement (T-165) is now this case’s
house: its exact certificate proves $s(176) \le 13.8076372268022796$, the reported and
the verified upper bound, `5.22e-03` below the ceiling the case held until then,
$13.8128562532770564$ from Nate Chaoweeraprasit’s packing (T-151), which stays retained
as history ([the certificate](#francisco-couzos-certificate-t-165)).

**House adoption, 12 October 2026.** Nate Chaoweeraprasit’s SQUISH packing, composed
from Francisco Couzo’s n = 127 packing and the Kingbird catalogue’s n = 5 packing
(T-151) was this case’s house until 15 October 2026: its exact certificate proves
$s(176) \le 13.8128562532770564$, the reported and the verified upper bound, `1.00e-02`
below the ceiling the case held until then, $13.82287565553229$ from the catalogue’s
packing (T-101), which stays retained as history
([the certificate](#nate-chaoweeraprasits-certificate-t-151)).

**Exact certificate, 2026-10-06.** Evan Daniel’s
[`square-packing`](../resources/web/evand-square-packing-2026-10-05/README.md) published
on 5 October 2026 an exact rational certificate of this packing, decided here over
$\mathbb{Q}$ by two exact checkers that share no code with each other or with the
source, and by the source’s own two run here: it proved $s(176) \le 13.82287565553230$,
the verified upper bound until 12 October 2026 (T-101). Until then the verified upper
bound was the trivial grid bound $14$.

Open.
Previously, the best known packing gave $s(176) \le 13.82287566$, and the strongest
lower bound independently verified here is $13.276932$ from Karakuş’s general theorem,
leaving a gap of $0.5459$. General bound: s(N) >= 1/2 + sqrt(N - floor(sqrt(N)) + 1/4).

## The earlier verified ceiling

Until 12 October 2026 the verified upper bound here was $13.82287565553230$, proved by
Evan Daniel’s exact rational certificate of this packing
(`E-evand-exact-ceilings-2026-10-05-exact-replay`, T-101). It was **larger** than the
catalogue’s printed side $13.82287565553229$, then the best known, by `1e-14`.

It was not the value of $s(176)$ and not a different packing: it is the certificate’s
own side, $13.8228756555322952954\ldots$, rounded up at the fourteen decimals the
catalogue prints. The catalogue gives the packing’s side exactly, as
`(25/2) + (1/2)sqrt(7)`, and the certificate’s side lies `1.4e-19` above that: its
squares are rational and each is kept clear of its neighbours and the walls, so it
bounds the exact side from above and does not reach it.
That side is irrational, so no rational certificate reaches it.
A `mathematics` blocker in the frontmatter recorded the difference until Nate
Chaoweeraprasit’s certificate (T-151) replaced both lanes.

## The exact certificate

Evan Daniel’s
[`square-packing`](../resources/web/evand-square-packing-2026-10-05/README.md) published
on 5 October 2026 exact rational certificates of the packings this register lists as
best known, solved from its own witnesses, and offered those that do not lower a printed
side as a replay of the existing bounds, asking for nothing to be registered from them.
The certificate for this count holds the same 176 squares, each a rational centre and a
rational $t = \tan(\theta/2)$, in a square of side $13.8228756555322952954\ldots$,
`5.3e-15` above the side the Kingbird catalogue prints, $13.82287565553229$. Square for
square, its pose lies within `2.9e-4` of the binary64 pose the atlas pictures for this
count. 5 squares move by more than `1e-8`, all of them squares the source lists as
carrying no force; every other square moves by at most `1.8e-15`. Evan Daniel’s solver
moves the binary64 pose to a nearby exact KKT point of the problem of minimizing the
side under non-overlap, computed at 80 digits, and rounds it outward to rationals.

This repository decides the certificate exactly.
Converted without rounding, every pair and every wall is decided over $\mathbb{Q}$
twice, by `sqpack`’s exact separating-axis test and by an independent checker that
shares no code with it, and the source’s own two checkers, run here as retained, accept
it as well
([receipts](../resources/web/evand-square-packing-2026-10-05/README.md#replayed-here)).
That proved $s(176) \le 13.82287565553230$, the verified upper bound until 12 October
2026: the certificate’s side rounded up at the fourteen decimals the catalogue prints,
as the record writes any certificate of a printed side.
It says nothing about optimality.
On jlevy/squares#375 its author wrote that the solver, its checkers and the batch “were
written with Claude (Anthropic) as a coding and research agent, directed and reviewed by
me.”

## The lower bound

The strongest lower bound independently verified in this record is Karakuş’s general
bound, which applies to every nonsquare $N \ge 8$:

```
s(N) ≥ ½ + √(N − ⌊√N⌋ + ¼)
```

**Correction, 2 October 2026.** Until that date the verified lower bound here was
Nagamochi’s general closed form,
$s(N) \ge \min(\lceil\sqrt{N}\rceil, \sqrt{N - 2\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor + 1} + 1)$, which
is stronger at this $n$ and is now recorded as a reported bound.
Its published proof rests on Nagamochi’s Lemma 1, which Karakuş showed false; nothing is
disproved, and no packing beating it is known
([review of 2 October 2026](../../docs/project/reviews/review-2026-10-02-nagamochi-lemma1-karakus.md)).
This register had recorded that proof as verified, its own error, logged as defect
[D-516](../../defects.md).

Source-reported bounds are recorded separately from this independently verified theorem.

[review of 11 October 2026](../../docs/project/reviews/review-2026-10-11-upper-bound-imports-500.md)
accepted the replay for finite feasibility.
That proves $s(176) \le 13.8128562532770564$, the verified upper bound until 15 October
2026: confirmed, independently re-implemented.
It says nothing about optimality, rigidity or a local minimum.

The source states that SQUISH’s solver, its verification and the issue were written with
Claude Opus 5.5, and that the Mondrian composition method was formulated by the author
and implemented with Claude Opus 5.5, under his direction.
