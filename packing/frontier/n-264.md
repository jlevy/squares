# `s(264)` — open

**Hiroaki Hosono’s closed form, 14 October 2026.** Hiroaki Hosono’s closed form
(wand125, 13 October 2026, T-180) states $s(N) \ge a(N)$ for every non-square
$N \ge 49$, from a measure of line segments on 34 intervals of the fractional part.
At $n = 264$, $k = 16$ and interval 6 give
$a(264) = 16.308403094438469074118669059935\ldots$, so the reported lower bound here is
now $s(264) \ge 16.308403094438$, rounded down, above Nagamochi’s closed form,
$16.26433752247$. The source replayed its certificate with its own checker; no record of
it has been replayed here, so the verified lower bound is unchanged.
The source states that its search, certificates, checkers and text were produced with AI
assistance (Claude and Codex), under the direction of and reviewed by Hiroaki Hosono.

**House adoption, 12 October 2026.** Daniil Ostashev’s composition of his 38-square
rectangle with a 93-square piece of Evan Daniel’s exact optimum of Joost de Winter’s n =
126 packing (T-158) is now this case’s house: its exact certificate proves
$s(264) \le 16.7747351323878329$, the reported and the verified upper bound, `3.44e-03`
below the ceiling the case held until then, $16.77817459305202$ from the catalogue’s
packing (T-101, T-155), which stays retained as history
([the certificate](#daniil-ostashevs-certificate-t-158)).

**Exact regularized certificate, 2026-10-12.** This project’s regularization pass, Evan
Daniel’s exact solver and regularizer run here on this register’s witness, produced an
exact rational packing of this arrangement, decided here over $\mathbb{Q}$ by two exact
checkers: it proves $s(264) \le 16.778174593052022768577069729084$, a bound Evan
Daniel’s exact certificate of this packing already proves at the same side (T-101). The
verified upper bound was that side exactly until 12 October 2026, resting on both
certificates (T-101, T-155), and the record printed it as $16.77817459305203$. Until
then the verified upper bound was exactly $16.77817459305203$, the side of Evan Daniel’s
exact certificate of this packing rounded up at the printed precision (T-101).

**Exact certificate, 2026-10-06.** Evan Daniel’s
[`square-packing`](../resources/web/evand-square-packing-2026-10-05/README.md) published
on 5 October 2026 an exact rational certificate of this packing, decided here over
$\mathbb{Q}$ by two exact checkers that share no code with each other or with the
source, and by the source’s own two run here: it proves $s(264) \le 16.77817459305203$,
the verified upper bound from 6 to 12 October 2026 (T-101). Until then the verified
upper bound was the trivial grid bound $17$.

Open. Previously, the best known packing gave $s(264) \le 16.7781746$, and the strongest
lower bound independently verified here is $16.255951$ from Karakuş’s general theorem,
leaving a gap of $0.5222$. General bound: s(N) >= 1/2 + sqrt(N - floor(sqrt(N)) + 1/4).

## The earlier verified ceiling

Until 12 October 2026 the verified upper bound here was
$16.778174593052022768577069729084$, exactly, proved by Evan Daniel’s exact certificate
of this packing (`E-evand-exact-ceilings-2026-10-05-exact-replay`, T-101) and by the
exact regularized certificate of it (`E-regularized-bounds-2026-10-12-exact-decision`,
T-155), and the record prints it as $16.77817459305203$. As printed it was **larger**
than the catalogue’s printed side $16.77817459305202$, then the best known, by `1e-14`.

It was not the value of $s(264)$ and not a different packing: it is the certificate’s
own exact side. The catalogue gives the packing’s side exactly, as `9 + (11/2)sqrt(2)`,
and the certificate’s side lies `1.7e-19` above that, so it bounds the exact side from
above and does not reach it.
That side is irrational, so no rational certificate reaches it.
A `mathematics` blocker in the frontmatter recorded the difference until Daniil
Ostashev’s certificate (T-158) replaced both lanes.

## The regularized exact certificate

This project’s regularization pass of 11 and 12 October 2026 ran Evan Daniel’s exact
solver and regularizer
([`evand/square-packing`](https://github.com/evand/square-packing), MIT, vendored here)
on this register’s witness for this count, with the `conservative` angle policy and
`coincide` placement, which wrote its certificate at the side the solver reached, the
solved point scaled by $1 + 10^{-20}$ and the side rounded up.
The result is an exact rational packing of the same 264 squares in a square of side
$16.778174593052022768577069729084$, `7.2e-15` below the verified upper bound the record
held before, which was that same side rounded up at the printed precision: it is the
side of Evan Daniel’s certificate of this packing (T-101). Measured against the witness
under the box’s eight symmetries, its squares pair one to one with the witness’s; none
moves by more than `1e-3`, and none turns by more than `1e-6` as a sine.
It draws the same picture as the witness, so the atlas drawing does not change.

Each pair and each wall is decided over $\mathbb{Q}$ twice, by `sqpack`’s exact
separating-axis test and by an independent checker that shares no code with it, and both
accept it; both refuse it with its tightest pair pushed into overlap and with its box
shrunk past its least clearance ([certificate](../cases/regularized_bounds/README.md)).
The same two checkers accepted it in the run that made it, so this is a reproduction
with the producer’s code.
That proves $s(264) \le 16.778174593052022768577069729084$, the verified upper bound
until 12 October 2026. Evan Daniel’s certificate of this packing already proves the same
bound at the same side (T-101), and its replay here, by two checkers that share no code
with each other or with the source, is independent of the code that made this view.
That side was the exact form of the case’s `verified_upper_bound` until 12 October 2026,
which cited both certificates (T-101, T-155) and printed it as $16.77817459305203$, the
decimal it printed before and an upper display of it.
It says nothing about optimality, and the packing stays its finders’.

## The lower bound

The strongest lower bound independently verified in this record is Karakuş’s general
bound, which applies to every nonsquare $N \ge 8$:

```
s(N) ≥ ½ + √(N − ⌊√N⌋ + ¼)
```

**Correction, 2 October 2026.** Until that date the verified lower bound here was
Nagamochi’s general closed form,
$s(N) \ge \min(\lceil\sqrt{N}\rceil, \sqrt{N - 2\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor + 1} + 1)$, which
is stronger at this $n$ and is now recorded as a reported bound.
Its published proof rests on Nagamochi’s Lemma 1, which Karakuş showed false; nothing is
disproved, and no packing beating it is known
([review of 2 October 2026](../../docs/project/reviews/review-2026-10-02-nagamochi-lemma1-karakus.md)).
This register had recorded that proof as verified, its own error, logged as defect
[D-516](../../defects.md).

Source-reported bounds are recorded separately from this independently verified theorem.

## Daniil Ostashev’s certificate (T-158)

Daniil Ostashev’s composition of his 38-square rectangle with a 93-square piece of Evan
Daniel’s exact optimum of Joost de Winter’s n = 126 packing, reported on
[jlevy/squares#528](https://github.com/jlevy/squares/issues/528), at an exact rational
side, displayed upward as $16.7747351323878329$ (T-158). The certificate gives a
rational side and, for each of the 264 unit squares, a rational centre and the tangent
of its half-angle; the
[retained packet](../resources/web/dostashev-records-2026-10-12/README.md) holds it with
the complete native jobs that decided it.

Both maintained exact routes decided it here, `sqpack`’s exact separating-axis verifier
and the independent rational corner checker, with its duplicate-square and
outside-container controls, and a third exact route, half-extent containment and exact
intersection area, decided it again.
No route here is the source’s code, so the replay is independently re-implemented.
The
[review of 12 October 2026](../../docs/project/reviews/review-2026-10-12-upper-bound-imports-528-529.md)
accepted the replay for finite feasibility.
That proves $s(264) \le 16.7747351323878329$, the verified upper bound: confirmed,
independently re-implemented.
It says nothing about optimality, rigidity or a local minimum.

The issue says the author set up an AI-assisted research loop and drove it with his own
ideas, the rectangle library and the composition being his, and that AI agents wrote and
ran the search, composition and verification code.

The house it replaces, $16.77817459305202$ from the catalogue’s packing (T-101, T-155),
stays retained with its own replay as history.
