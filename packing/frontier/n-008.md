# `s(8)` — solved

$s(8) = 3$. El Moumni (1999) obtains the bound from parallel-line intersection lengths.

## The packing

The record catalogue does not picture $n = 8$: no arrangement has ever been found that
beats the trivial $\lceil\sqrt{8}\rceil = 3$ grid, so the grid is still the best known
packing. That is a statement about what has been searched, not a proof.

## The lower bound

[El Moumni’s Proposition 3](../resources/papers/el-moumni-1999-optimal-packings-unit-squares.pdf),
printed pp. 288–289 (volume PDF pp.
294–295), counts the lengths of intersections between the packed squares and parallel
line segments. Proposition 2 gives a minimum contribution from each square; the total
available segment length bounds their sum.
The last sentence of printed p. 289 applies this argument to $n = 8$.

[Friedman’s DS7, Theorem 3](../resources/papers/friedman-ds7-packing-unit-squares-in-squares.md)
gives a distinct proof using seven unavoidable points (Figure 25).

The verified lower-bound evidence recorded here is Karakuş’s rectangle bound,
`E-karakus-strip-lower`, which gives exactly $s(k^2 - 1) = k$ (T-084). **Correction, 2
October 2026:** until that date it was the Nagamochi result, `E-nagamochi-lower`, whose
published proof rests on Nagamochi’s Lemma 1, which Karakuş showed false; its value here
is the same, and it is now a reported bound
([review of 2 October 2026](../../docs/project/reviews/review-2026-10-02-nagamochi-lemma1-karakus.md)).
This register had recorded that proof as verified, its own error, logged as defect
[D-516](../../defects.md).
