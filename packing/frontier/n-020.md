# `s(20)` — open

Open. The best known packing gives `s(20) ≤ 5`. The verified lower bound is
`s(20) ≥ 24/5 = 4.8`, from this repository’s weighted fractional unavoidable-set
certificate at that side (`T-020`, 2026-09-04, `V4/C4`), leaving a gap of `0.2`. It
displaced Nagamochi’s general `1 + √13 ≈ 4.605551`, which had held this case since 2005,
by `0.194449`. Friedman’s DS7 survey had already listed the weaker case-specific
`6√2 − 4 ≈ 4.4852` bound for n = 19–20 by 2000; the new contribution is the stronger,
independently replayable `4.8` certificate, not the first bound about this size.

## The packing

The retained catalogue records the trivial `⌈√20⌉ = 5` grid and no pictured improvement.
That is a catalogue report, not a completeness proof.

## The lower bound

The operative bound is a weighted fractional unavoidable-set certificate at side `24/5`:
2260 rationally weighted atoms on a `D4`-symmetric site set, total mass
`946131/50000 < 20`, every closed `B`-square at every net direction capturing mass at
least one, the least being `50007/50000`. It is decided from its own bytes by an exact
event-cell sweep and by an interval branch and bound over centre boxes, which agree on
that least value to the digit.
The certificate was generated for `n = 20`, and it carries this case with no
monotonicity step: of the five conditions only **Condition 2**, total mass below `n`,
mentions `n`, so an atom set certifies its side for every integer strictly above its own
mass, which is 19 and upward.
DS7’s earlier n = 19–20 entry is not independently verified here.
Nagamochi’s general closed form later became the strongest pre-T-020 bound at n = 20 and
applies to every `N ≥ 4`:

```
s(N) ≥ min{ ⌈√N⌉,  √(N − 2⌊√N⌋ + 1) + 1 }
```

In the current verified register, Nagamochi supplies the lower bound for 58 of the 65
open cases at `n ≤ 100`.

## What the method can still add here

A certificate for `n` cannot exist above `⌈√n⌉·B`, which at `B = 9977/10000` is
`4.9885`, so a single certificate on this fixed 181-direction net cannot go farther.
That is a necessary ceiling, not evidence that `4.9885` is attainable.
Unlike `n = 19`, no known packing binds first: the retained catalogue gives the trivial
grid at `5`. Refining the direction net changes `B`, however, so this finite-net ceiling
does not rule out a proved family of certificates and a limiting argument at `5`.
