# `s(66)` — open

Open. The best known packing gives `s(66) ≤ 8.65685425`, and the strongest lower bound
independently verified here is `8.141428` from Nagamochi’s general theorem, leaving a
gap of `0.5154`. General closed form: s(N) >= min(ceil(sqrt(N)), sqrt(N -
2*floor(sqrt(N)) + 1) + 1).

## The packing

Stenlund’s diagonal strip of width three (1980), for which the survey states no
generating rule — only the figure.
`cases/lifted_q2` lifts the retained witness’s every coordinate into `Q(sqrt 2)` at
small height and verifies the lifted pose exactly, which is what moved
`verified_upper_bound` from the grid ceiling onto the published exact side.
The lift is a candidate generator and the exact verifier is the proof; the certificate
is the witness’s own geometry, made exact (`D-398`, and the operation `D-402` does not
foreclose).

## The lower bound

The selected external report, [Friedman DS7], gives the lower-bound expression
`2*sqrt(2) + 71/13` for `s(66)` (approximately `8.289965586285`). Friedman’s DS7 survey,
Theorem 9, k=8, reports this bound at n=65; reference [8] is Green’s private
communication (2000). The source proof has not been recovered.
Inherited at n=66 by monotonicity.
This changes the reported source field only; the independently verified lower bound
remains `8.14142842854`. The [source audit](../devtools/audit_ds7_lower_bounds.py)
compares the exact theorem expressions separately from opaque table decimals.

The strongest lower bound independently verified in this record is Nagamochi’s general
closed form, which applies to every `N ≥ 4`:

```
s(N) ≥ min{ ⌈√N⌉,  √(N − 2⌊√N⌋ + 1) + 1 }
```

At `n ≤ 100`, this theorem supplies the independently verified lower bound in 58 of the
65 open cases. Source-reported bounds are recorded separately.
