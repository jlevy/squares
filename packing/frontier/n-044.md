# `s(44)` — solved

$s(44) = 7$. Established by Mohsen Khalili and Sara Gharabaghi (2026) with a closed
mixed cover in Evan Daniel’s format, made by Daniel’s line-cover linear program from his
$s(45) = 7$ cover and decided by his interval checker `zmx2`, whose check was replayed
here in full (T-252). It is the case $k = 7$ of $s(k^2 - 5) = k$. The source says the
search, the certificate, the separation tool and the verification runs were produced
with Claude (Anthropic) under the authors’ direction.

## The packing

The upper bound is trivial: the $7 \times 7$ grid holds 49 unit squares, so it holds 44
with five cells empty, and $s(44) \le 7$. The record catalogue does not picture
$n = 44$, and no arrangement below side 7 has ever been found.
What is new is that none exists.

## The lower bound

The [certificate](../resources/web/moh63-dev-s44-equals-7-2026-10-29/README.md) is a
*closed mixed cover* in Daniel’s format: 15,525 rationally weighted points of $[0,7]^2$
plus mass spread uniformly along 3,912 segments of length $1/50$ on the interior grid
lines $x, y \in \lbrace1, \ldots, 6\rbrace$, total
$2199755099/50000000 = 43.99510198 < 44$, exactly invariant under the square’s eight
symmetries, such that every closed unit square inside $[0,7]^2$, at every centre and
every angle, captures mass at least $1$. If 44 unit squares fit in a side $s < 7$,
scaling by $7/s$ and shrinking each square about its centre gives 44 pairwise disjoint
closed unit squares in $[0,7]^2$, which would capture at least 44 from a total of
$43.995$. The margin is small: the total is 0.011% below 44.

The source reports the cover accepted by `zmx2`, with its binary64 enclosures widened
outward and exact integer mass tests, over all 4,900 roots of the `D4` fundamental
region and, assuming no symmetry, all 39,200 roots of the whole pose space.
It also reports `zm_mixed.py`, Daniel’s exact-rational checker, over all 9,800 of its
`D4` roots at depth 24, the 27 roots left open there closed whole at depth 30, about 143
CPU-hours; those records were read here and not replayed.
Nothing about this cover is in Lean.

The verified field rests on a complete replay here of `zmx2`
([`E-khalili-842-cover-replay`](evidence.yaml), `V3/C3`). Built from the retained source
that the source’s `verify.sh` builds, it certified all 4,900 `D4` roots (16,792,446
boxes) and all 39,200 unreduced roots (134,880,184 boxes) on 31 October with no
uncertified box, every root’s census equal to the source’s published log, and refused
two mutated copies of the cover; the receipts are in the
[packet](../resources/web/moh63-dev-s44-equals-7-2026-10-29/README.md).
That replay is interval-certified, so it assumes correctly rounded binary64 arithmetic
on the host, and it runs Daniel’s own checker: a reproduction with the producer’s code.
The
[review](../../docs/project/reviews/review-2026-10-31-khalili-gharabaghi-s44-mixed-cover.md)
of 31 October found no mathematical defect in the claim.

## Earlier lower bounds

Hosono’s mixed rectangle-density certificate of 4 October, decided here by
sqverify-fast, this repository’s clean-room measure verifier, at all 201 net directions
([`E-n044-wand125-mixed-69725-sqverify-fast-replay`](evidence.yaml)), gave
$s(44) \ge 2789/400 = 6.9725$ (T-090), which was the verified lower bound until 31
October 2026, and his fine-net certificate of 13 October reports $2799/400$ (T-177),
never replayed here.
Before them the verified lower bound was $2777/400$, Hosono’s rectangle-density
certificate of 1 October
([`E-wand125-rectangle-2026-10-01-source-replay`](evidence.yaml)).

Nagamochi’s general closed form, the verified lower bound before this certificate, is
now a reported bound (see the correction below).
Karakuş’s general bound, independently verified here and below this certificate at this
$n$, applies to every nonsquare $N \ge 8$:

```
s(N) ≥ ½ + √(N − ⌊√N⌋ + ¼)
```

**Correction, 2 October 2026.** Until that date the verified lower bound here was
Nagamochi’s general closed form,
$s(N) \ge \min(\lceil\sqrt{N}\rceil, \sqrt{N - 2\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor + 1} + 1)$, which
is stronger at this $n$ and is now recorded as a reported bound.
Its published proof rests on Nagamochi’s Lemma 1, which Karakuş showed false; nothing is
disproved, and no packing beating it is known
([review of 2 October 2026](../../docs/project/reviews/review-2026-10-02-nagamochi-lemma1-karakus.md)).
This register had recorded that proof as verified, its own error, logged as defect
[D-516](../../defects.md).

See the [Frontier corpus summary](README.md#what-the-corpus-shows) for the current
aggregate count; source-reported bounds are recorded separately.
