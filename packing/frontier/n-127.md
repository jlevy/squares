# `s(127)` — open

**Hiroaki Hosono’s closed form, 14 October 2026.** Hiroaki Hosono’s closed form
(wand125, 13 October 2026, T-180) states $s(N) \ge a(N)$ for every non-square
$N \ge 49$, from a measure of line segments on 34 intervals of the fractional part.
At $n = 127$, $k = 11$ and interval 6 give
$a(127) = 11.371666575347155125075549070184\ldots$, so the reported lower bound here is
now $s(127) \ge 11.371666575347$, rounded down, above Nagamochi’s closed form,
$11.29563014098$. The source replayed its certificate with its own checker; no record of
it has been replayed here, so the verified lower bound is unchanged.
The source states that its search, certificates, checkers and text were produced with AI
assistance (Claude and Codex), under the direction of and reviewed by Hiroaki Hosono.

**House adoption, 11 October 2026.** Francisco Couzo’s refinement of Ryan Xu’s packing
(T-128) is now this case’s house: its exact certificate proves
$s(127) \le 11.8108787875891799$, the reported and the verified upper bound, `5.79e-05`
below the ceiling the case held until then, $11.8109366475118110$ from Ryan Xu’s packing
(T-125), which stays retained as history
([the certificate](#francisco-couzos-certificate-t-128)).

**Exact certificate, 2026-10-06.** Evan Daniel’s
[`square-packing`](../resources/web/evand-square-packing-2026-10-05/README.md) published
on 5 October 2026 an exact rational certificate of this packing, decided here over
$\mathbb{Q}$ by two exact checkers that share no code with each other or with the
source, and by the source’s own two run here: it previously proved
$s(127) \le 11.82287565553230$, the verified upper bound (T-101). Until then the
verified upper bound was the trivial grid bound $12$.

Previously, the packing gave $s(127) \le 11.82287566$, and the strongest lower bound
independently verified here is $11.281929$ from Karakuş’s general theorem, leaving a gap
of $0.5409$. General bound: s(N) >= 1/2 + sqrt(N - floor(sqrt(N)) + 1/4).

## The earlier verified ceiling

The verified ceiling at that intake was $11.82287565553230$, proved by Evan Daniel’s
exact rational certificate of this packing
(`E-evand-exact-ceilings-2026-10-05-exact-replay`, T-101). It was **larger** than the
then-reported $11.82287565553229$, by `1e-14`.

It is not the value of $s(127)$ and not a different packing: it is the certificate’s own
side, $11.8228756555322952954\ldots$, rounded up at the fourteen decimals the catalogue
prints. The catalogue gives the packing’s side exactly, as `(21/2) + (1/2)sqrt(7)`, and
the certificate’s side lies `1.2e-19` above that: its squares are rational and each is
kept clear of its neighbours and the walls, so it bounds the exact side from above and
does not reach it. That side is irrational, so no rational certificate reaches it.
That certificate gap was the upper-bound blocker at that intake.
Read `reported_upper_bound` for the best known side length.

## The exact certificate

Evan Daniel’s
[`square-packing`](../resources/web/evand-square-packing-2026-10-05/README.md) published
on 5 October 2026 exact rational certificates of the packings this register then listed
as best known, solved from its own witnesses, and offered those that do not lower a
printed side as a replay of the existing bounds, asking for nothing to be registered
from them. The certificate for this count holds the same 127 squares, each a rational
centre and a rational $t = \tan(\theta/2)$, in a square of side
$11.8228756555322952954\ldots$, `5.3e-15` above the side the Kingbird catalogue prints,
$11.82287565553229$. Square for square, its pose lies within `1.5e-4` of the binary64
pose the atlas then pictured for this count.
10 squares move by more than `1e-8`, 4 of them squares the source lists as carrying no
force and the other 6 by at most `2.0e-8`; every other square moves by at most `2.6e-9`.
Evan Daniel’s solver moves the binary64 pose to a nearby exact KKT point of the problem
of minimizing the side under non-overlap, computed at 80 digits, and rounds it outward
to rationals. At this count it started from the witness after the source’s own local
squeeze, which it has not published; that bears on reproducing the solve and not on
checking the certificate.

This repository decides the certificate exactly.
Converted without rounding, every pair and every wall is decided over $\mathbb{Q}$
twice, by `sqpack`’s exact separating-axis test and by an independent checker that
shares no code with it, and the source’s own two checkers, run here as retained, accept
it as well
([receipts](../resources/web/evand-square-packing-2026-10-05/README.md#replayed-here)).
That previously proved $s(127) \le 11.82287565553230$, the verified upper bound at that
intake: the certificate’s side rounded up at the fourteen decimals the catalogue prints,
as the record writes any certificate of a printed side.
It says nothing about optimality.
On jlevy/squares#375 its author wrote that the solver, its checkers and the batch “were
written with Claude (Anthropic) as a coding and research agent, directed and reviewed by
me.”

## The lower bound

The strongest lower bound independently verified in this record is Karakuş’s general
bound, which applies to every nonsquare $N \ge 8$:

```
s(N) ≥ ½ + √(N − ⌊√N⌋ + ¼)
```

**Correction, 2 October 2026.** Until that date the verified lower bound here was
Nagamochi’s general closed form,
$s(N) \ge \min(\lceil\sqrt{N}\rceil, \sqrt{N - 2\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor + 1} + 1)$, which
is stronger at this $n$ and is now recorded as a reported bound.
Its published proof rests on Nagamochi’s Lemma 1, which Karakuş showed false; nothing is
disproved, and no packing beating it is known
([review of 2 October 2026](../../docs/project/reviews/review-2026-10-02-nagamochi-lemma1-karakus.md)).
This register had recorded that proof as verified, its own error, logged as defect
[D-516](../../defects.md).

Source-reported bounds are recorded separately from this independently verified theorem.

## Francisco Couzo’s certificate (T-128)

Francisco Couzo’s refinement of Ryan Xu’s packing, reported on
[jlevy/squares#451](https://github.com/jlevy/squares/issues/451), at exact side
$5905439393794589919821183554001/500000000000000000000000000000$, displayed upward as
$11.8108787875891799$ (T-128). The certificate gives a rational side and, for each of
the 127 unit squares, a rational centre and the tangent of its half-angle; the
[retained packet](../resources/web/couzo-exact-refinements-2026-10-08/README.md) holds
it with the complete native jobs that decided it.

Both maintained exact routes decided it here, `sqpack`’s exact separating-axis verifier
and the independent rational corner checker, with its duplicate-square and
outside-container controls, and a third exact route, half-extent containment and exact
intersection area, decided it again.
One maintained route is the verifier the source ran; the independent rational corner
checker and the third route are independently re-implemented.
The
[review of 10 October 2026](../../docs/project/reviews/review-2026-10-10-couzo-daniel-refinement-replays.md)
and the
[final review of 11 October 2026](../../docs/project/reviews/review-2026-10-11-final-upper-bounds.md)
accepted the replay for finite feasibility.
That proves $s(127) \le 11.8108787875891799$, the verified upper bound: confirmed,
independently re-implemented.
It says nothing about optimality, rigidity or a local minimum.

The house it replaces, $11.8109366475118110$ from Ryan Xu’s packing (T-125), stays
retained with its own replay as history.
The other certificates confirmed here at this count, T-138 at $11.81087878759$, stay
verified and are not selected.
