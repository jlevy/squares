# `s(24)` — solved

$s(24) = 5$. Established by an unavoidable point set, Erich Friedman (1999).

## The packing

Found by Erich Friedman in 1999, via a hand construction.

## The lower bound

Proved by exhibiting an **unavoidable set**: a set of points in the container that every
unit square placed inside must contain.
With $n - 1$ such points, $n$ disjoint squares are impossible by pigeonhole.

The verified lower-bound evidence recorded here is Karakuş’s rectangle bound,
`E-karakus-strip-lower`, which gives exactly $s(k^2 - 1) = k$ (T-084). **Corrected 4
October 2026:** until 2 October it was the Nagamochi result, `E-nagamochi-lower`, whose
published proof rests on Nagamochi’s Lemma 1, which Karakuş showed false; its value here
is the same, and it is now a reported bound
([review of 2 October 2026](../../docs/project/reviews/review-2026-10-02-nagamochi-lemma1-karakus.md)).
This register had recorded that proof as verified, its own error, logged as defect
[D-516](../../defects.md); this record’s prose was missed by the correction of 2 October
and is corrected here.
