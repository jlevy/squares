Run one 60–90 minute first-principles block on normal forms for an optimal or
inclusion-minimal packing of eleven congruent freely rotated squares. Separate facts
that follow from compactness or global translation from hypotheses that need rigidity,
quenching, equilibrium, or finite pose enumeration.

Test the ladder explicitly: existence of an optimum; at least one boundary contact;
support in two independent container directions; whether the same square can be made a
corner owner; and, if not, what finite alternative of contact graphs or stress
conditions remains. A counterexample to one perturbation step rejects only that step.
Do not claim that every optimum has a corner square without a proof, and do not use a
numerical jam as a universal argument.

The deliverable is one of: a proved useful normalization; an exact counterexample to a
proposed normalization; or an explicit finite disjunction whose branches can be joined
to conditional certificate targets. The block may run the already admitted compact
two-attainer construction only when its result distinguishes two formulations. Record
the sources and every unproved premise. No new global bound follows without certificate
coverage of all branches.
