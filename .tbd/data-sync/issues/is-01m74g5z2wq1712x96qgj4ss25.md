What it claims, by its README: Theorem 1.1 (thin regions under a concave ceiling g <= M), corollaries for a sloped ceiling (W >= 4e-5 L / sqrt(M)) and for L x (m + 1/2) and L x M rectangles, and Theorem 1.5: for g = w + zeta x with 0 < zeta <= 1 and rise R = zeta L >= 1, W >= 7e-6 sqrt(L R), which it says is Conjecture 1 of H. D. Bui, arXiv:2508.04603, with a constant. "Nothing is claimed for the waste of a square or for c*(k) over all packings." Section 8 says what the bounds imply for the constructions of Bui, McClenagan and Ryu, given the decompositions those papers describe.

Status by the source: claimed, not verified. The proofs were found and written by Claude (Anthropic) as a research agent under Fang's direction in one day; reviewed only by language models (six blind passes); tested by floating-point simulation; no person has checked them and nothing is formalised; no certificate a program can check. Credits named: Roth and Vaughan (the waste lemma), Sungjoon Ryu (the path method), H. D. Bui (the conjecture), Evan Daniel's notes on the exponent of c*(k) (the question). Licence: simulators MIT, note, working notes, reports and reviews CC BY 4.0.

Why a bead and not only a read: the register's asymptotic waste records (packing/frontier/asymptotic-waste-bounds.yaml) cite Bui's arXiv:2508.04603 and build on it (Ryu's staircase and wall fillers "after Bui"), and this note claims Bui's Conjecture 1 and states what it implies for Bui's, McClenagan's and Ryu's constructions. Slabs and trapezoids are another container than the square, as earlier reads of the strip notes said for rectangles of height below 2, so it may well be nothing to import; that is this bead's decision.
