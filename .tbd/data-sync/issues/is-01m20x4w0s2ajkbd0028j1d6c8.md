`_pytest_workers(jobs) = cpus - jobs + 1` is the right rule for a step running beside `jobs - 1` other outer slots, and it is what both behavioural lanes and (after D-485) the pre-push tier take. It has a consequence nobody has priced: at the default local shape, `packing-validate --push` with no `--jobs`, jobs defaults to the cpu count, so the rule yields one worker and the tier's behavioural step runs in one process — while the other outer slots, which finish in seconds to a couple of minutes, sit idle for the rest of the run.

The push tier's shape is unusual: its behavioural step is submitted first (highest budget, so first in `_submission_order`) and outlives every other step in the tier, especially in the whole-suite case, where the selector expands to everything for any workflow or suite-configuration change. So for most of the tier's wall the assumption behind `cpus - jobs + 1` — that `jobs - 1` other slots are busy — is false.

Options to price, not to assume: (a) let the pre-push tier default `--jobs` to 1, since it is a tier whose wall is one step; (b) size the behavioural step from the slots actually free when it starts rather than from `jobs`; (c) leave the rule and document `--jobs 1` as the way to run the pre-push tier, which is what D-485's fix makes true for the first time. Whichever is chosen, measure it: D-472 applies, and the reading that matters is the whole non-exhaustive suite, not a subset.

Found while validating PR 129, where a plain `packing-validate --push` ran past 16 minutes on a 4-cpu box without finishing its one behavioural step.
