Owner, 2026-09-11: "we are marking some things as ?rigid when it's clear they're not rigid. We need to have a solve agent go through the research and make sure that we identify the cases that are obviously not rigid. There are a few families where this is the case, and we should have a process for this."

The workbench's OPEN block shows `? rigidity` for every n whose rigidity state is `not-established`, and the panel draws that as a question. But "not established" and "not rigid" are different claims, and some of these are not open questions at all -- a packing with a square that visibly rattles is not a rigidity anyone is waiting on a proof of.

Two things, and the second is the one that lasts:

1. **Find them.** A packing with a free square -- one that can be translated or rotated without collision while every other square stays put -- is not rigid, and that is checkable rather than a matter of judgement. The literature also names families outright. Go through both: the archive under `packing/resources/` for what the sources say, and the atlas for what the arrangements show. Expect families rather than scattered n -- grid packings with a leftover, and the tilted-block constructions, are the obvious candidates.

2. **A process, so this does not drift again.** The record needs a third state. Today `rigidity.state` is `established` or `not-established`, and the second is doing two jobs: "nobody has proved it" and "it is visibly false". Those want separate values, separate evidence, and separate treatment on the page -- an open question belongs in OPEN, a settled negative belongs in PROVEN as a fact. Whatever the vocabulary becomes, the check that assigns it should be a program in `devtools` that the validation tier runs, not a judgement recorded once.

The mechanical half -- the free-square test -- is the part a solver agent can do at scale, and it is also the part that produces evidence rather than an opinion: for each n it either exhibits a motion or it does not.

Start by measuring how many n are affected before designing the vocabulary. If it is three, this is a correction; if it is eighty, it is a research question about what the atlas is claiming.
