# Review: The Mathematical Frontier, Its Gaps, and How to Search It Fast

**Date:** 2026-08-23

**Authors:** Claude and Codex agents, for joshuadlevy@gmail.com

**Status:** Complete after independent delta review

## Overview

This is a strategy review along the creativity and mathematical-depth axis: given the
tooling now standing (exact verifier, LP-in-cell quench, campaign record, readiness
agenda), where should aggressive, high-iteration search point so that the program’s most
likely outcome is a novel mathematical result rather than a well-documented miss?

The registered portfolio has the right central dependency — terminal-component meaning
before census and rarity inference — but it is too narrow around that spine and too
confident about several cheap-looking shortcuts.
The best near-term portfolio has four independent fronts: nonsmooth local geometry at
Trump’s packing; exact small-`n` configuration spaces; construction surgery calibrated
on held-out public records; and proof synthesis through piercing, forcing, and certified
relaxations. The `n = 11` record remains a high-value moonshot, while `s(12)`, `s(61)`,
catalogue reconstruction, and the asymptotic waste exponent are separate theorem and
construction programs.

Loop latency is an allocation variable, but mathematical meaning comes first.
A cheap grid is useful only when it maps a defined object.
The current record is roughly 25 agent-minutes per round against about two
machine-minutes, so batched experiments and generated views should dominate the first
screens. No overnight promotion is justified until a short run has a typed event,
independent validity evidence, a stable budget currency, and a result whose
interpretation survives the identity and topology rules below.

## Corrections made during the delta review

The first draft contributed six valuable ideas and also made six substantive leaps.
They are kept visible because a strategy review must audit its own attractive stories.

1. The verifier’s `20` boundary count is **corner coordinates**, not independent wall
   constraints. Collapsing repeated corners on one flush edge gives eleven square-wall
   incidences. Together with fourteen contacting pairs, that is at most twenty-five
   selected feature equalities, not `34 = 3n + 1`. Axis-aligned orientations and
   separating-axis choices are nonsmooth, so neither the false `34 = 34` count nor the
   corrected `25 < 34` count decides rigidity.
   H-022 needs a generalized one-sided feasible-tangent calculation, not a predeclared
   rank of 34.
2. A fixed-angle cell LP dual balances the container-side objective against active
   constraints. It is an equilibrium **load certificate** for that LP, not automatically
   a self-stress of the free framework and not a certificate for angle or cell changes.
3. An extreme-value fit to a small, budget-conditioned endpoint sample can diagnose a
   tail model. It cannot establish that a proposer will miss the record at every future
   budget.
4. A two-angle sheet is cheap for one imported contact cell.
   Mapping the global two-class stratum also requires class assignments and competing
   cells, so its cost and claim are much larger.
5. A valid continuation path gives an upper bound on a minimax clearance barrier.
   Coalescence of two numerical branches or algorithmic basins does not prove that
   feasible-set components merge.
6. A continuous angle-value sheet cannot have only one sufficiently refined grid point
   within a fixed positive objective tolerance of its minimum.
   H-028 now tests for one refined local minimizer and a boundary margin; nearby points
   with nearby values are expected.

The delta review also found a missing primary source.
Bašić and Slivková (2018) apply piercing-number theory directly to unit-square packing
and derive a specialized bound at `n = 61`. Their bound is weaker than the Nagamochi
value already stored here, but their method invalidates every claim that transversal
theory had never been applied to `s(n)`. Fractional piercing and certified column
generation remain open directions.

## Gaps in the Research Record

Eleven gaps, ordered by how directly each changes what to run next.
The first four are mathematical content absent from the record; the rest are method and
sequencing gaps.

### G-1: The apparent isostatic count was a duplicate-corner artifact

The exact verifier reports fourteen contacting pairs and twenty boundary **corner
coordinates**. The first draft treated every boundary coordinate as a different wall
constraint. That is false: an axis-aligned edge flush with a wall contributes two corner
coordinates but one containment equality.

Re-reading the exact witness gives eleven square-wall incidences:

```
square 0: left, bottom       square 1: right, bottom
square 2: top                square 3: left, top
square 4: top                square 5: left
square 7: bottom             square 10: right
```

There are therefore fourteen pair-contact incidences plus eleven wall incidences, not
thirty-four independent scalar equations.
Even `25` is only a feature-incidence count.
At axis alignment the support function contains absolute-value cusps, and pair
non-overlap is a disjunction over separating axes.
A smooth Jacobian of one selected branch can miss the one-sided inequalities that pin an
orientation; a count can neither prove flexibility nor rigidity.

The corrected fast result is still strong: **compute every one-sided linearized cone at
fixed side**. Enumerate every active wall and separating-axis branch in open real angle
charts and solve the homogeneous systems for a nonzero direction.
These cones overapproximate the true Bouligand tangent.
A nonzero vector nominates a continuation direction but is not yet a feasible mechanism;
a zero union proves the true tangent is zero and supports a separately stated
finite-branch local-isolation argument.
This is the first leg of H-022.

The jamming literature remains useful for methods — one-sided rigidity, equilibrium
loads, prestress stability — but it does not license importing an isostatic count law to
this nonsmooth container problem.
Contact-count pruning may be used only after a theorem states which generalized
constraints are necessary for the class being enumerated.

### G-2: The cell-LP dual is useful, but it is not automatically a self-stress

For fixed angles and one selected separating cell, the LP dual multipliers satisfy the
stationarity equation against the nonzero container-side objective.
They certify the optimal centers and side **inside that cell**. In mechanical language
they are an equilibrium load certificate including wall reactions and the external
container load; a free-framework self-stress would instead balance with zero external
objective.

That distinction leaves three valid uses:

1. **Replayable cell certificate.** Retain primal feasibility, dual feasibility, and the
   primal-dual gap for every polished cell.
   With rational trigonometric data this can be exact; with algebraic data it needs the
   corresponding number field or intervals.
2. **Generalized angle screen.** Parametric-LP one-sided derivatives can test particular
   angle directions. The measured Trump slice has positive unequal slopes on its two
   sides, but one slice does not span the full angle cone.
   A full statement requires the minimum directional derivative over all independent
   directions and every locally competing basis.
3. **Descriptors and moves.** Normalized active multipliers, unloaded squares, and
   stressed blocks are promising atlas descriptors and proposer signals.
   Their value is empirical until a held-out predictor or move-kernel comparison
   succeeds.

An interval neighborhood proof must additionally cover basis changes, separating-axis
switches, nonsmooth axis-aligned cusps, and second-order directions where the
first-order model vanishes.
The dual is an ingredient, not H-022’s certificate by itself.

### G-3: Fractional piercing can measure a ceiling, with asymmetric conclusions

The `n = 11` report’s open question — *is there a proved ceiling below 3.877084 on what
a finite unavoidable point set can certify?* — is answerable by a computation the
portfolio already almost contains.

Let `U_s` be the family of interiors of all unit-square poses contained in a side-`s`
square. Define the fractional piercing value `τ*(U_s)` by minimizing total nonnegative
point mass subject to every pose receiving mass at least one.
A ten-point unavoidable set can exist only if `τ*(U_s) ≤ 10`, so

> `s_frac = sup { s : τ*(U_s) ≤ 10 }`

is a ceiling for the **pure ten-point** version of the method, assuming the usual
monotonicity and open-set limiting step are handled.
By LP duality, `τ*(U_s) > 10` has a fractional pose-measure witness whose pointwise load
is at most one.

Bašić–Slivková (2018) already connects the integral piercing number of `U_s` directly to
`s(n)` and proves exact and upper-bound results for several container sizes.
The missing direction is therefore narrower and better: continuous **fractional**
piercing, its certified discretization, and its integrality gap in the `n = 11`
interval. The two outcomes are not symmetric:

- `s_frac < 3.877`: a **method-limitation theorem**. The classical method cannot close
  the gap even in principle; effort shifts to threshold and continuously-varying
  refinements (Bentz) or genuinely new certificates, with a precise statement of *why*.
- `s_frac ≥ 3.877`: the relaxation has not ruled out a ten-point certificate.
  It does **not** imply that an integral ten-point set exists; an integrality gap may
  remain. Primal support can still suggest candidate loci.

Finite grids can easily give the wrong inequality direction.
A restricted pose set lowers the primal optimum; a restricted point set raises it.
The experiment must retain both sides and promote only a bound whose interpolation error
is certified over all poses and points.
Threshold certificates (one square charged multiple resources), Stromquist’s conditional
forcing, and Bentz’s moving families are not bounded by this pure-point LP. H-006 tests
support stability; the new decision hypothesis is H-034.

### G-4: The corner generalizes — a kink-codimension principle worth registering

T-3 established one corner on one slice.
The general picture it instantiates deserves statement as a corpus law candidate:

> **At a record optimum with `k` free angle classes, the class-angle objective attains
> its minimum at a point of non-smoothness of codimension `k` — every free angle is
> pinned by its own basis-exchange condition, and the objective grows at first order
> (conically) in every angle direction.**

The evidence is deliberately small: T-3’s two positive unequal one-sided slopes on one
`n = 11` slice (`k = 1`) and the fact that a fixed-cell parametric LP has locally smooth
or piecewise-smooth value branches.
This does **not** imply that every record lies at a codimension-`k` intersection or
grows conically in every direction.
H-027 registers that claim as a corpus test, beginning with the published `n = 17`
geometry rather than promoting it to a law.
The prediction: the `s(17)` record (`k = 2` by its two non-trivial tilts) sits at a
codimension-2 corner; the corpus scan once O-01’s geometry lands.

Three consequences if it holds:

1. **Method selection is explained, not just observed.** Bracketing beats descent
   because the optimum has no gradient to follow; this stops being an empirical quirk
   and becomes a structural property of the target class (D-087’s separation of claims
   is respected: this is the registered-corpus-law version, not the algorithm claim).
2. **The optimum lies on the tie locus.** Basis-exchange points are where two cells’ LP
   optima agree — a codimension-1 set per condition.
   Records live on the intersection of `k` of them.
   A-01’s active-set search can walk *along* tie loci (follow a basis exchange,
   dimension drops by one each time) instead of sampling volume — the mathematically
   motivated version of the billiard/inflation family that produced records at
   `n = 29, 37`.
3. **Corner sharpness is a descriptor.** The one-sided slope pair per angle direction is
   a local-sheet descriptor the atlas should retain; it measures objective sharpness in
   that declared cell and is a candidate predictor for entry probability (feeding Q-04’s
   held-out predictor question).
   It is not an intrinsic basin invariant.

### G-5: H-012 as registered bounds rarity but cannot measure it, and cheaper evidence is available

If the record component’s attraction probability is of order `1e-6`, a modest direct
multistart census will probably observe zero arrivals.
A zero count gives a one-sided bound under the declared proposer, quench, event, and
sample count; it does not resolve rarity on a finer scale, and the atlas’s per-component
frequencies stay empty exactly where the strategy needs them.
A-02 already names adaptive multilevel splitting and subset simulation on nested side
thresholds. H-029 now makes that a separate estimator comparison: it must pass exact
synthetic probabilities and agree with an independent `n = 10` reference before a new
`n = 11` cell can be registered.

A cheap diagnostic is **extreme-value analysis of a much larger, fixed-budget multistart
sample**. The present archives are too small and mix retention and budget effects.
A generalized-Pareto or bounded-tail fit should first recover known synthetic endpoints,
remain stable across held-out batches, and report model disagreement.
Even then it says only what the fitted fixed-budget distribution suggests; changing the
move kernel, schedule, or budget changes the distribution and may reveal a previously
unseen tail. The fit can decide whether H-017 deserves its next budget rung.
It cannot answer “at any budget.”

### G-6: A known-cell two-angle sheet is cheap; the global stratum is not

The synopsis scans Trump’s selected cell along one shared tilt.
One step up is to keep the **same imported separating cell and square-to-class
assignment** and map

> `Φ_C(a₁,a₂) = the LP optimum in declared cell C at those class angles`.

a two-dimensional value sheet.
Trump’s packing is the point `(0°,40.18°)` on its `6+5` sheet.
This shows the local branch shape, active-basis changes, and whether the reference is
unique on a declared rectangle.
It says nothing about cells not on that continuation sheet.

The first cost is one LP per grid point plus basis tracking and is plausibly seconds to
minutes after benchmarking.
The **global** two-class object is the lower envelope over all assignments, separating
cells, and possibly disconnected center solutions.
Finding that envelope needs multistart or enumeration at every region and may be orders
of magnitude dearer.
A few restarts do not make it global.
H-028 therefore starts with the reference-cell sheet at `n = 11`, then uses imported
`n = 17` geometry as a second known answer.
Its accept rule concerns the local-minimum count and boundary gap, not the impossible
uniqueness of one grid point inside a positive value tolerance.
Multi-cell sheets are promoted only if the local view changes a decision.

This remains one of the cheapest meaningful pictures in the portfolio.
Novelty relative to unpublished or web work is unverified and is not part of its accept
rule.

### G-9: The asymptotic lane has current mathematics but no campaign question

The registry is dominated by `n ≤ 100`. It contains no hypothesis about the genuine
asymptotic gap

> `W(x) \notin o(x^{1/2})` versus `W(x) = O(x^{3/5})`.

Bui’s 2025 good-square reduction says the growth rate can be studied using uniformly
small inclinations, and Bui and McClenagan’s 2025–26 constructions expose explicit
stack, strip, and near-rectangular-quadrilateral primitives.
This is not evidence about Trump’s `n = 11` mechanism.
It is a separate lane with two interfaces to computation:

1. finite-size optimization of the published primitives, which may yield new upper
   bounds before it affects an exponent; and
2. automated symbolic balance of error terms and boundary synchronizations, which can
   falsify proposed exponent improvements before a proof is attempted.

H-035 registers the finite transfer test.
H-037 carries the exponent question without pretending the present searcher can decide
it.

### G-10: Cross-`n` genealogy is more specific than neighbor seeding

H-004 adds or removes one square from `n±1` records.
The public catalogue suggests a richer grammar: delete a boundary motif, duplicate a
strip, shear a block, change one continued-fraction synchronization, or compose two
verified parents. These operations define a directed genealogy of constructions.
A held-out test can ask whether a named surgery reproduces a known child from its public
parent before the same grammar is allowed to chase a new record.
This is the experimental geometer’s most credible route to breadth because every failure
teaches which construction move does not transfer.

### G-11: Basin data currently omit the object every serious map needs

`golden/basin-maps.yaml` retains side, coarse keys, contacts, class sizes, and counts.
It discards the full pose, active features, quench events, and transition provenance.
Consequently it can render a row chart but not a packing glyph, mechanism slice,
continuation path, rank screen, or ambiguity edge that another checker can replay.

The minimum map record is content-addressed full poses plus side, precision tier,
proposer/quench versions, active feature witnesses, and parent/event ids.
This is not a request for a database.
One append-only JSONL event stream and generated views suffice.
Without it, more samples make the atlas larger but not more intelligible.

## Nine Mathematicians, Nine Pushes

A completeness check on the portfolio: give the same tooling to nine senior
mathematicians of different schools and ask what each would do first.
The point is not imitation; it is to expose correlated blind spots in one strategy
culture.

**The rigidity and nonsmooth-geometry theorist.** First move: write every one-sided
active branch at Trump’s packing and compute its linearized cone in an open angle chart.
Then continue any surviving vector nonlinearly; do not rename it a feasible motion from
the linearization alone.
A zero union proves the true tangent is zero and motivates a separate local-isolation
statement. H-022 carries the destination; H-026 supplies the corrected first screen.

**The optimization and duality theorist.** First move: retain primal-dual cell
certificates, then compute certified bounds on `τ*(U_s)` (G-3); second move, ask whether
any Positivstellensatz certificate has been tried in the square-packing literature (none
was found in the retrieved corpus, which is not an exhaustive negative result).
Portfolio: A-04 and H-006 carry the machinery; the ceiling measurement is the missing
first-class question.
Bašić–Slivková supplies the integral-piercing precedent, so novelty rests on the
fractional and certified-continuous pieces.
SOS itself stays parked on cost — the honest note is that it is the one untried proof
technology, and a small-`n` pilot (`s(2) = 2` via SOS) would be a methods paper on its
own.

**The algebraic number theorist and experimental mathematician.** First move: parse the
currently verified record catalogue’s surviving minimal polynomials and ask what the
fields are — Galois groups, discriminants, whether degree is predicted by mechanism
(angle-class count and contact pattern through the elimination).
H-038 now carries that question.
It is cheap per case once O-01 lands.
The 2026 `n = 69` replacement is interval-validated but does not inherit its superseded
parent’s degree-82 polynomial, which is exactly why the field table must follow verified
witnesses rather than side values.
A mechanism/field association would be an empirical search prior, not a proof that the
degree-8 `n = 11` record has a unique mechanism.

**The statistical physicist.** First move: measure kernel-conditioned transition
networks and adaptive-splitting efficiency, while keeping them separate from feasible
topology. A directional escape curve from perturbed Trump starts is an algorithmic
observable. A verified path supplies a clearance-barrier upper bound.
Neither is an intrinsic funnel depth or merge level until matching topology evidence
exists.

**The discrete geometer and enumerative combinatorialist.** First move: solve the exact
`n = 3` quotient as the atlas control, attack one complete small-`n` classification,
then move to the theorem-shaped frontiers `s(12)` and `s(61)`. A broad tier-S finite
sweep remains useful, but it is a lottery portfolio rather than an enumeration theorem.

**The logician and formalizer.** First move: specify the certificate formats now — exact
upper-bound witness (T-1’s shape) and no-escape/unavoidability certificates — so every
artifact the search produces is born replayable; formalize Friedman’s lemma layer and
Stromquist Theorem 1 as the Lean pilot.
Portfolio: the Lean report and Stage 4 carry this; the sequencing point is that the
certificate *format* costs days now and months to retrofit, and the `n = 11` upper-bound
formalization has not been located in the retrieved literature — a potentially
stand-alone publishable artifact whose difficulty is bounded (one packing, one field,
one polynomial identity).

**The probabilist.** First move: calibrate discovery and missing-mass estimators on
synthetic distributions and proved cases; then compare adaptive splitting with restarts
at `n = 10`. Only afterward estimate a Trump-target event at `n = 11`. Extreme-value
fits are sensitivity analyses, not support proofs.

**The topologist and real algebraic geometer.** First move: define `F_n(S)/(D4 × S_n)`
and compute the exact optimal set at `n = 3`, including its strata and singular quotient
points.
Next, use certified continuation and semialgebraic cells at `n = 5`. This persona
rejects a point-cloud embedding as topology and asks for the filtration whose connected
components are actually being drawn.

**The asymptotic and analytic-number-theory geometer.** First move: reproduce the error
balances in the 2025–26 `O(x^{3/5})` constructions and search their discrete
synchronization parameters for finite improvements.
Separately, ask which step in the Roth–Vaughan `1/2` lower exponent resists
strengthening after the good-square reduction.
This lane shares geometry but not verdicts with `n = 11`.

The consolidated answer is a barbell.
Run exact local and small-`n` mathematics where a counterexample or certificate is
cheap; simultaneously run mechanism-diverse construction screens where held-out records
make failure informative.
Keep global proofs, asymptotics, and formalization alive as slower lanes with explicit
intermediate artifacts, rather than forcing every mathematician into the
stochastic-search spine.

## Basin Ontology and Visualization Ladder

“Basin” currently risks naming six different objects.
The atlas should store the level, not ask a picture to erase the distinction.

| Level | Object | What establishes it |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | **Observation**: one retained pose/event at one precision tier | content-addressed pose plus provenance |
| 1 | **Endpoint-key cluster** | declared numerical key and tolerance |
| 2 | **Algorithmic attraction class** under `P/Q` | repeated starts and deterministic terminator under named versions |
| 3 | **Stationary or terminal set** | KKT/generalized-gradient or declared fixed-point evidence |
| 4 | **Connected terminal family/component** | certified continuation path plus local dimension; nonconnection needs a separate obstruction |
| 5 | **Active/contact stratum** | stable active feature system and rank/tangent evidence |
| 6 | **Symmetry quotient object** | explicit `D4 × S_n` quotient, including stabilizers and singular orbits |
| 7 | **Feasible-sublevel component** in `F_n(S)` | topology of the valid configuration set at fixed side |
| 8 | **Kernel-conditioned transition network** | observed transitions under named move kernels and budgets |

For a pose `q`, let `r(q)` be its required container side.
For two certified subsets `A,B`, the intrinsic minimax level is

> `b(A,B) = inf_γ max_t r(γ(t))`,

where `γ` ranges over valid continuous paths joining them, after the declared quotient.
A computed valid path proves an **upper bound** on `b`. A certified absence of all paths
below a level proves a lower bound.
Failure of a continuation algorithm proves neither.
If all leaves are components of one sublevel filtration, exact merge levels can form a
merge tree; an ordinary quench-branch dendrogram is only an algorithmic picture.

This leads to six visualization rules:

1. **Packing glyphs first.** Every node opens to the actual pose, side, precision tier,
   and active features.
   Abstract points without geometry are almost impossible to audit.
2. **Families occupy regions.** The exact `n = 3` family must render as a parameterized
   arc/interval with strata at its special endpoints, not as many quantized dots.
3. **Typed edges.** Use different line styles for verified paths, path upper bounds,
   ambiguous identity, shared contact strata, and observed algorithmic transitions.
   Never let a force layout imply adjacency.
4. **Mechanism coordinates before embeddings.** Useful axes include side, class-angle
   coordinates, generalized tangent dimension, wall/pair incidence, normalized LP-load
   spectrum, and continuation parameter.
   UMAP/t-SNE may browse; they do not testify.
5. **Frequency always names `P/Q/E`.** A component has no intrinsic attraction
   probability. Put proposer, quench, identity policy, budget, and uncertainty in the
   legend.
6. **Show unresolved mass.** Discovery curves, unresolved identity, censored runs, and
   model disagreement belong in the same view as apparent saturation.

## Loop Speed as a Strategy Input

The measured latencies, consolidated:

| Operation | Measured | Source |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Annealer move | ~0.025 µs (39.7 M/s local) | bench, readiness agenda |
| One LP cell solve (`n = 11`) | ~1.28 ms | infrastructure report |
| Class-bracketing quench to floor | ~70 LP solves | exp-007/008 mechanism note |
| Exact verification (`n = 11`, degree 8) | 0.16 s | rerun for this review |
| Canonicalization | 0.098 s at `n = 7`; **7.91 s at `n = 9`** | readiness agenda |
| Normal gate | 118 s at the completed creativity checkpoint | current review checkpoint |
| One recorded round | **~25 agent-minutes**, ~2.1 wall-minutes | 275 agent-min / 11 rounds |

The stacked engineering review in
[PR #18](https://github.com/jlevy/thinking-scratchpad/pull/18) reports a candidate
22-second normal gate, a 40-second strict gate, and about 2.5 seconds or 1,600 LP solves
per quench. Those are not yet baseline measurements for this branch.
The shared pair-half-extent rewrite is a valid geometric identity and exposes a real
measurement problem: its floating-point evaluation-order change removed an `n = 3`
non-converged endpoint that the golden had counted in `distinct_basins`. That `3 → 2`
change is evidence that solver stalls and endpoint-key stability need separate
observables, not evidence that the mathematical landscape lost a basin.

The speedup stack also needs a small correctness pass before absorption: its targeted
mode still builds Rust and can hide a build skip, its worker cap is per concurrent step
rather than one global budget, and invalid negative job counts can hang the throttle.
These findings do not weaken the central profile result—the quench, not the annealer, is
the census bottleneck.
They do mean that this review prices work from the checked 118-second head until the
stacked implementation and its claims agree.

Two structural conclusions.

**The binding constraint is the agent loop, not the compute.** At 12:1 agent-to-machine
time, halving compute buys almost nothing; halving per-round overhead doubles
throughput. The readiness agenda’s efficiency list already targets this (recipes,
generated views, delegated checks); the *strategy* consequence is new: prefer
experiments whose analysis is a generated artifact — a map, a table, a curve — over
experiments whose result needs bespoke interpretation.
Every rung of the visualization ladder should therefore be generated from retained
events. Sweeps amortize preregistration and analysis, but only over a mathematically
fixed object: ten thousand fixed-cell LP pixels are one useful round; ten thousand
unlabeled cell switches are not a global landscape.

**Two efficiency investments carry mathematical payoff, not just speed.** The
canonicalizer’s `n = 7 → 9` jump (81×) will dominate any census and is already tracked
(D-049, `think-xzew`); fix before H-011 scales.
An **exact LP over the coefficient field** removes the `1e-11` polished floor (D-021)
inside a declared cell and makes G-2’s certificate replayable.
Rational sine/cosine constructions such as a 3-4-5 tilt reduce to `ℚ`; Trump’s algebraic
coefficients require `Q(u)` or intervals.
This is one interface with multiple mathematical payoffs, not a claim that every record
is rational.

The lane assignments below are functions of the measured latencies, not fixed judgments.
Efficiency work is a live lane of its own: when a latency moves by an order of magnitude
— the canonicalizer fix, a compiled or batched quench, a faster gate — re-derive the
assignment from the new numbers rather than inheriting it, since a lane demoted for
slowness may become a wide interactive lane overnight.

The lane assignment that follows from the current latencies:

- **Sub-second loops — run wide, now.** Branchwise tangent and cell-load extraction
  (G-1, G-2), one-dimensional angle slices (V-2), diagnostic tail fits (G-5), and
  dual-measure reads. Dozens of iterations per day per agent; these are the “fast to
  iterate, do more of up front” lanes.
- **Minute loops — daily cadence.** Small-`n` pose batches, fixed-cell angle sheets,
  equal-side continuation legs, and held-out parent-surgery probes.
- **Hour loops — queue for the runner.** `n = 11` sampling campaigns, splitting arms,
  fine-resolution ceiling LPs, MAP-Elites comparisons.
  These are the ones the launch gate governs; they should never occupy an interactive
  agent.
- **Day-plus loops — parallel background lanes.** Geometry corpus parsing (G-7), source
  retrieval (G-8), Lean certificate formats, superdisk geometry (H-014, correctly last).

Use successive halving across **mechanisms**, not only parameters:

- every family gets a one-minute known-answer screen where possible;
- ten minutes requires a valid witness and a mechanism-specific response, not merely a
  lower scalar;
- one hour requires replication or a paired advantage and retained trajectories;
- an overnight rung requires independent validity, target-host calibration, checkpoints,
  a nonempty priced queue, and a predicted threshold crossing;
- a barren lane records what failed and a concrete revisit trigger, such as a new pose
  corpus, faster quench, different move topology, or proof primitive.

D-101 means the historical exp-007/008 round-level wall fields cannot price sub-minute
maps. Rebenchmark the exact new operation three times; do not inherit those totals.

The identity/census dependency remains

> `H-023 → H-021 → H-011 → H-012`,

with any H-029 `n = 11` successor entering only after exact synthetic controls and the
independent `n = 10` reference pass.
Other lanes do not pretend to be census results.
The geometry importer, full-pose event stream, canonicalizer profile, and exact/interval
certificate interface are shared enablers, not reasons to block paper mathematics such
as H-033.

Every parked lane records one of four negative-result types:

- **mechanism absent**: the predicted structure never appears;
- **instrument insensitive**: the output does not move under a known-answer
  perturbation;
- **economically dominated**: another method gives more information per pair-test or
  agent-minute;
- **interpretation blocked**: the response exists but identity, validity, or topology is
  unresolved.

Its revisit trigger names what changes that diagnosis.
“Try more budget later” is not a trigger.

## Recommendations

1. **Make the next map true before making it large.** Retain full poses/events, render
   V-0, and solve the exact `n=3` quotient control.
   Do not call the current row table a basin atlas.
2. **Run the corrected Trump screen.** Build the generalized one-sided tangent system
   and retain the cell LP’s primal-dual load certificate.
   Drop every `34=34` or self-stress claim unless a later derivation earns it.
3. **Exploit held-out records.** Reproduce one UnitSquare parent-to-child improvement
   before trusting a surgery proposer on an unseen `n`.
4. **Diversify the proof lane.** Reproduce Stromquist’s conditional mechanism, pilot
   certified fractional piercing, attempt a robust restricted-angle theorem, and attack
   Bentz’s next `m²-3` case at `n=61` as independent programs.
5. **Use fast-first successive halving.** Give mechanisms short comparable screens;
   expand only on valid response curves; reserve unattended compute for checkpointable
   experiments with independent evaluators and a priced queue.
6. **Treat negative results as strategy state.** Record the failure type and revisit
   trigger in the experiment artifact and idea board.
   A barren method stays visible but consumes no automatic budget.
7. **Keep the asymptotic lane separate and alive.** Finite-size optimization of current
   stack/trapezoid primitives is the bridge; no small-`n` basin result answers the
   exponent question.
8. **Refresh sources continuously.** The Bašić–Slivková and UnitSquare misses show that
   novelty and frontier tables decay.
   Recheck primary catalogues before every claim of “first,” “none,” “all,” or “current
   best.”
