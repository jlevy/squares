# `s(100)` — solved

`s(100) = 10`, trivially: `100 = 10²`, so the grid is optimal and the area bound `√n` is
already tight.

## The edge of this corpus

This artifact is the last in the frontier corpus, which covers `n ≤ 100` to match the
range of Friedman’s survey.
The record catalogue itself goes considerably further — all `n ≤ 324`, plus selected
larger cases at `626, 1453, 1765, 1850` and `2043` — and the algebraic degrees out there
are what make exact verification interesting: degree 40 at `s(300)`, degree 62 at
`s(1453)`.

Extending this corpus past 100 is mechanical for the structured fields and would be
worth doing alongside the machine-readable record parser described in the research
document’s program. The editorial content is the part that does not automate.
